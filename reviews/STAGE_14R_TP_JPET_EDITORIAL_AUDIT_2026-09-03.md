Date: 2026-09-03

Manuscript: **Strategic Interaction among Public Innovation Hubs under Private Repricing**

Target: *Journal of Public Economic Theory*

## Editorial verdict

**Desk-reject risk: MEDIUM.**

The manuscript is suitable for submission to JPET once the remaining author metadata are completed. The residual desk/referee risk is substantive fit/novelty risk already documented in Stages 11–13, not a Stage-14 presentation or compliance failure.

## 1. First-page clarity — PASS

The first page now states the question, fixed-price versus endogenous-price mechanism, analytic sign-reversal result, exact canonical verification, scope limitation, keywords, and JEL codes. The structured abstract is denser than a conventional theory abstract but remains readable and follows the current Author Guidelines.

## 2. Public-economics relevance — PASS

The opening frames the object as decentralized regional public investment with a shared commercial intermediary. The manuscript does not read merely as a case study of a named Japanese institution.

## 3. Contribution visibility — PASS

The contribution is consistently stated as a qualitative reversal in the same public–public strategic object when the private pricing policy is activated. The manuscript does not claim novelty for network effects or follower pricing per se.

## 4. Main theorem readability — PASS

The theorem hierarchy is visible and the local nature of the result is explicit. The economic contrast at the beta-zero limit is understandable before the Appendix proof details.

## 5. Mechanism clarity — PASS

The fixed-price cross-side reinforcement and endogenous repricing feedback are separated before the headline theorem. The manuscript explains why repricing can change the sign rather than merely the magnitude of public strategic interaction.

## 6. Literature differentiation — PASS WITH RESIDUAL RISK

The paper concedes component overlap with follower-response, public/private investment, and two-sided-platform literatures. The surviving claim is narrow. Combination novelty remains the principal referee risk; Stage 14 found no reason to enlarge the novelty claim.

## 7. Mathematical machinery — PASS

The main text contains the economically necessary decomposition and theorem. Detailed small-beta proof and exact Krawczyk/interval material are placed in the Appendix. High-degree elimination objects are not dumped into the paper.

## 8. Institutional motivation — PASS

Institutional examples are presented as analogue/plausibility evidence, not causal validation. Model predictions are separated from observed institutional facts.

## 9. Welfare discipline — PASS

The manuscript explicitly distinguishes strategic interaction from welfare ranking and limits the welfare result to the reported coordination wedge. It does not claim that strategic substitution is desirable or that the benchmark is a policy prescription.

## 10. Conclusion accuracy — PASS

The conclusion restates the local theorem, exact canonical certificate, and limitations without inflating them into a global characterization.
