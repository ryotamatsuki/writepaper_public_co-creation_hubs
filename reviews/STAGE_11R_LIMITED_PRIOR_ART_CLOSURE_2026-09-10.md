# Stage 11R — Limited Prior-Art Closure

Date: 2026-09-10 JST

Purpose: close only the three literature items left explicitly open by the Astra Stage-11 audit. This record does not broaden the novelty claim and does not treat lack of access as proof of non-absorption.

## Target object

The only surviving proposition-level novelty object is:

> the local strategic cross effect between two decentralized public investments changes sign when moving from a matched-price fixed-private-fee benchmark to the full game with endogenous repricing by a shared private intermediary.

The audit asks whether that exact object is directly contained in the target papers.

## Mun and Nakagawa (2010)

Primary/author-hosted materials located:

- Published article: Se-il Mun and Shintaro Nakagawa, “Pricing and Investment of Cross-Border Transport Infrastructure,” *Regional Science and Urban Economics* 40(4), 228–240, DOI 10.1016/j.regsciurbeco.2010.03.008.
- Kyoto University repository author manuscript / working-paper version: `Pricing_and_investment100322.pdf` and KIER Working Paper 661.

The accessible abstract and article introduction show a two-country transport-infrastructure model comparing free access, decentralized government pricing/investment, private operation, and integrated provision. The paper explicitly studies strategic responses of infrastructure pricing and investment and welfare rankings across organizational regimes.

**Closest-proposition assessment:** this is close on decentralized public investment and endogenous user charges, but it is not the same strategic object. The accessible primary text does not present two public investors sharing a third private intermediary, does not define a matched-private-price fixed-price benchmark against an endogenous shared private price policy, and does not state the B3-to-G public-public cross-effect sign reversal studied here.

**Status:** checked against accessible primary/author-hosted material; no direct absorption identified. This is not a claim that every proposition in the published typeset version has been exhaustively re-proved.

## Mun (2019)

Materials located:

- Published article: Se-il Mun, “Joint Provision of Transportation Infrastructure,” *Economics of Transportation* 19, 100118, DOI 10.1016/j.ecotra.2019.06.001.
- Public precursor: Se-il Mun (2016), “Joint Provision of International Transport Infrastructure,” Kyoto University Discussion Paper e-15-015.

The published abstract and public precursor describe two governments jointly establishing an infrastructure operator, choosing financial contributions to maximize regional/national welfare, with the operator collecting user charges. The paper studies financing rules, first-best conditions under cost-covering tolls, congestion extensions, and participation in joint provision at Nash equilibrium.

**Version relation:** the 2016 working paper is a public precursor to the 2019 article and shares the joint-provision mechanism, but this audit does not assert byte-for-byte or proposition-by-proposition identity between the working paper and final publisher version.

**Closest-proposition assessment:** the accessible materials concern two governments' contribution decisions and a common operator's user charge/revenue rule. They do not expose the paper's exact matched-price identification experiment or a sign comparison of the same two public best-response slopes with the shared private price response switched off versus on.

**Status:** partial closure. Final published full text was not legally available through the accessible route used in this audit. Absence from the accessible abstract/precursor is therefore not treated as proof of non-absorption. This residual uncertainty is carried to Astra recheck.

## He et al. (2026)

Primary publisher material located:

Longfei He, Xiao Zhang, Baofeng Huo, Francisco Saldanha-da-Gama, and Xiaohang Yue, “Targeting Strategy of Pricing and Service Investment in Two-Sided Capacity-Sharing Platforms under Mixed Network Externalities,” *International Journal of Production Economics* 300, 110123, DOI 10.1016/j.ijpe.2026.110123.

The accessible publisher text develops monopoly and competing-platform models with pricing, value-added-service investment, mixed same-side/cross-side network externalities, and single-/multi-homing. Its highlighted Proposition 3 concerns how market structure changes which side receives VAS investment; the paper also derives pricing and investment responses under platform competition.

**Closest-proposition assessment:** this paper directly absorbs the generic ingredients “two-sided pricing + investment + network effects + platform competition.” It does not, in the accessible publisher text, study two welfare-oriented regional governments sharing a third private intermediary or the exact matched-price fixed-fee versus endogenous-repricing comparison for the same public-public local cross derivative.

**Status:** checked against accessible publisher text; no direct absorption of the surviving proposition identified.

## Final limited-literature status

- Mun & Nakagawa (2010): bounded primary-source closure; no direct absorption found.
- He et al. (2026): bounded publisher-text closure; no direct absorption found.
- Mun (2019): residual full-text uncertainty remains; public precursor and final abstract checked, but this is not treated as a proof of non-absorption.

Accordingly, the manuscript's novelty wording must remain narrow. The literature record supports only the statement that the inspected materials do not reveal the exact matched-price public-public sign-reversal object. It does not support a universal claim that no prior paper contains any mathematically equivalent specialization.
