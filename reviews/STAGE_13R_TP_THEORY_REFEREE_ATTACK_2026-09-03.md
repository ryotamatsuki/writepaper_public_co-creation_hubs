# Stage 13R-TP — Hostile Theory Referee Attack

Date: 2026-09-03

Scale: `FATAL / MAJOR / MODERATE / MINOR`.

## Attack 1 — “Your main theorem is only local in beta.”

- Severity: MODERATE
- Validity: VALID.
- Current defense: The paper now states the local scope in the theorem itself. The result is nevertheless analytic, not a numerical continuity claim: B3 has `M_B3(0)=0` with a strictly positive beta derivative, whereas G has `M_G(0)<0`. This creates a strict sign separation at the limiting benchmark and implies an open positive-beta interval.
- Required fix: none beyond explicit scope language already added.
- Residual risk: a general-theory referee may still want a primitive-explicit epsilon or wider region. For the selected field-theory positioning this is not publication-critical.

## Attack 2 — “The result is calibrated rather than theoretical.”

- Severity: MAJOR before revision; MINOR/MODERATE after revision.
- Validity: no longer accurate as a description of the headline result.
- Current defense: Theorem 1 is a sufficient analytic result on regular beta-zero branches. The canonical vector is explicitly labelled a constructive benchmark, not an empirical calibration. The exact canonical proposition plays a separate root/sign verification role.
- Required fix: keep theorem before the canonical table and avoid reverting to witness-first language.
- Residual risk: branch-existence/SOC assumptions are not reduced to primitive inequalities, so the theorem is not a full primitive classification.

## Attack 3 — “The exact certificate is just sophisticated numerics.”

- Severity: MODERATE.
- Validity: INVALID if the method is described correctly.
- Current defense: The canonical primitives are exact rationals; the coupled G/B3 solution is isolated by an exact rational Krawczyk inclusion/uniqueness argument; derivative signs are bounded using rational interval arithmetic. No sign decision relies on floating-point rounding or finite differences.
- Required fix: retain exact intervals and the verifier path in the Appendix; do not call the certificate a closed-form theorem.
- Residual risk: some readers may be unfamiliar with computer-assisted proof. The Appendix therefore states exactly what is certified and what is not.

## Attack 4 — “Why should I care about the sign of best-response slopes?”

- Severity: MODERATE/MAJOR exposition risk.
- Validity: VALID question.
- Current defense: The sign determines whether one region's facilitation crowds in or crowds out the rival region's optimal facilitation response. Repricing changes the qualitative game played by decentralized public investors, with direct implications for strategic coordination and for interpreting comparative statics that hold the shared commercial outside option fixed.
- Required fix: mechanism and conclusion now state that the result changes the type of strategic interaction, not merely a derivative magnitude.
- Residual risk: welfare consequences remain separate and local; the paper correctly avoids claiming that one sign is normatively superior.

## Attack 5 — “The reversal is driven by special functional forms.”

- Severity: MAJOR remaining risk.
- Validity: PARTLY VALID.
- Current defense: The exact beta-zero formulas use the frozen tractable specification, but the strict reversal survives local C1 perturbations of project-type distributions and network functions conditional on regularity. The positive B3 channel and negative repricing channel have transparent structural interpretations.
- Required fix: continue to distinguish structural mechanism from theorem-level functional-form scope.
- Residual risk: no arbitrary-distribution or arbitrary-network theorem. This is the strongest generality limitation after the proof upgrade.

## Attack 6 — “The private platform merely adds another endogenous variable.”

- Severity: MODERATE.
- Validity: INCOMPLETE.
- Current defense: The matched-price B3 comparison holds the level of the commercial price fixed while suppressing only its policy response. The exact decomposition shows the additional terms generated by `p_i`, `p_j`, `p_i p_j`, and `p_ij`. Economically these terms arise because public investment changes private demand, the private intermediary reprices, and that price changes routing in both regions.
- Required fix: none; the matched-price design and decomposition now make the economic feedback explicit.
- Residual risk: the follower-response component itself is known, so novelty must remain attached to the public-public sign reversal rather than to endogeneity per se.

## Attack 7 — “You have not characterized the full primitive parameter region.”

- Severity: MODERATE for JPET-type field positioning; MAJOR for a general-theory target.
- Validity: VALID.
- Current defense: The paper supplies a nontrivial analytic sufficient theorem plus an exact canonical equilibrium certificate and local robustness. Semialgebraic primitive characterization exists in principle, but computing and compressing the full projection with uniform root selection is not economically informative for the present contribution.
- Required fix: none for the present target. Do not imply mathematical impossibility.
- Residual risk: this remains the clearest reason the paper is not “theory-heavy stretch ready.”

## Attack 8 — “The paper is mathematically overengineered relative to its economics.”

- Severity: MODERATE if algebra is foregrounded; MINOR after revision.
- Validity: WOULD BE VALID under an L3-first presentation.
- Current defense: Main text uses only the fixed-price derivative, the repricing decomposition, the small-beta sign formulas and the theorem. Krawczyk/root machinery is confined to the Appendix and machine-readable verifier. The degree-31 polynomial is not printed.
- Required fix: keep exact algebra as referee defense rather than a headline contribution.
- Residual risk: Appendix length must remain controlled.

## Attack 9 — “Public co-creation hubs are too narrow an application.”

- Severity: MODERATE.
- Validity: PARTLY VALID.
- Current defense: Label stripping yields two decentralized public intermediaries, cross-side participation and a shared adaptive commercial outside option. The mechanism applies to a broader class of public two-sided or ecosystem intermediaries even though innovation hubs motivate the primitives.
- Required fix: retain the broader public-intermediary language in the Introduction without claiming a general theory of all platforms.
- Residual risk: the institutional mapping remains an analogue class rather than an empirical validation exercise.

## Attack 10 — “The theorem does not materially improve on the old numerical result.”

- Severity: MAJOR before revision; MINOR after revision.
- Validity: INVALID after integration.
- Current defense: The theorem identifies a primitive direction (`beta`) and an exact limiting asymmetry: B3 requires positive network interaction to generate complementarity, whereas endogenous pricing creates negative public coupling already at beta zero. This explains why reversal occurs and proves a nonempty analytic neighborhood independently of the canonical solver.
- Required fix: keep these beta-zero formulas visible in the main text.
- Residual risk: epsilon is implicit, so the theorem improves mechanism identification more than global comparative statics.
