# Threat T2 — Damiano–Li Absorption Test

## Prior-art object

Damiano & Li (2008), “Competing Matchmaking,” studies how competing matchmakers use prices to sort heterogeneous participants into matching markets and how competition changes sorting efficiency and total match value.

## Similarities

- heterogeneous participants/projects;
- endogenous choice among competing matching intermediaries;
- outside participation margin;
- match value/compatibility is economically meaningful;
- provider competition affects sorting and welfare.

## Differences

- Damiano–Li matchmakers are private pricing competitors rather than two regional welfare-maximizing public investors plus a third private pricer;
- the candidate public strategies are matching capability investments, not access prices;
- the candidate has region-specific welfare objectives and cross-region incidence;
- the metropolitan price is chosen as a follower to two public investments;
- the candidate's partner opportunity sets are regionally anchored.

## Stage 2 verdict

`NOT EXACTLY ABSORBED — MATCHING/SORTING CLAIMS HEAVILY OCCUPIED`.

The candidate may not claim heterogeneous sorting, matching-market choice, compatibility, or participation as novel mechanisms.

## Stage 4 kill condition

If after solving the model the public investment choices merely induce the same sorting comparative statics that would result from changing two matchmaker prices/qualities in a standard competing-matchmaker model, with no new regional/private strategic feedback or welfare wedge, the route should be killed.
