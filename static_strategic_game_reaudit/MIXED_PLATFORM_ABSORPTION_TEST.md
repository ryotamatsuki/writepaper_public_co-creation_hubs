# Threat T3 — Mixed-Platform Absorption Test

## Kim (2024)

One public platform and one private platform compete in a two-sided linear-city/full-coverage market. The paper studies platform prices and investment incentives and finds that the private platform's response depends on public platform quality.

## Liu, Reshidi & Rivadeneyra (2026)

One welfare-maximizing public payment platform competes with one profit-maximizing private platform. Fees are strategic; participation, switching, network fragmentation and welfare are explicitly analyzed. The private platform can respond to public competition by changing fees.

## What these papers absorb

- public welfare vs private profit objective asymmetry;
- strategic public-private platform competition;
- private pricing response to a public competitor;
- participation versus switching/displacement logic;
- quality/investment incentives in mixed platforms.

## What they do not reproduce

The current audit did not find in these papers:

- two separate regional welfare-maximizing public investors;
- `x_1,x_2 -> p_T` follower pricing;
- productive matching over regionally anchored opportunity sets;
- immobile firms choosing among `H_1,H_2,H_T,0`;
- a decentralization wedge generated jointly by public-public and public-private competition.

## Stage 2 verdict

`STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE, NOT WHOLE-GAME ABSORPTION`.

## Hard-kill condition

Adding the second public hub must do more than duplicate the one-public comparative statics. Stage 4 must show a result that depends on the joint presence of both public investments and the endogenous private price.
