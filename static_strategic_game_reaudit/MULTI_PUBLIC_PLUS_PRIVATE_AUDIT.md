# Multiple Public + Private Strategic-Game Audit

## Purpose

This family tests whether the simple player count “two public providers plus private competitors” is already known. It is.

## Dadpay & Heywood (2006)

An international mixed oligopoly has two countries, each with public and private firms, competing in one market. Strategic interaction among multiple public firms can affect welfare.

## Han (2008)

An international mixed oligopoly explicitly permits multiple public firms alongside private firms under Cournot-Nash competition.

## Bárcena-Ruiz & Garzón (2020)

A country has two regions, each with a local public firm and a domestic private firm. A national authority studies merger of the local public firms. The paper explicitly situates itself in regional mixed oligopoly where multiple local public firms can compete across regions.

## Inoue/Kamijo/Tomaru and related work

Local public firms maximize local welfare, and horizontal intergovernmental strategic interaction is explicitly analyzed in the regional mixed-market literature.

## Consequence

The candidate cannot claim novelty from:

- having two regional public actors;
- combining public and private objectives;
- allowing local-welfare and profit maximizers in a common market.

## Remaining difference

The established multi-public/private literature is overwhelmingly product-market/spatial/ownership/quantity competition. The candidate instead places the actors in a **matching-intermediary market** with public capability investment, a metropolitan price response, and endogenous user allocation among intermediaries.

Therefore the player configuration is `COMPONENT OVERLAP`, while the matching-game interaction remains to be formally tested.
