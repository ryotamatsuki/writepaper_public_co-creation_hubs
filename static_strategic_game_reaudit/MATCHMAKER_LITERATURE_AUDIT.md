# Competing Matchmaker / Intermediary Literature Audit

## Core findings

### Gehrig (1993)

Establishes intermediation as an endogenous alternative to decentralized search/exchange for heterogeneous agents. The candidate cannot claim that choosing an intermediary instead of searching is new.

### Caillaud & Jullien (2003)

Establishes strategic competition among intermediation service providers, including pricing and participation/network effects. The candidate cannot claim provider competition itself as new.

### Damiano & Li (2008)

This is the closest classic matching threat. Competing matchmakers use prices to sort heterogeneous participants into matching markets; price competition affects market structure and sorting efficiency.

Implication: heterogeneous project sorting among `H_1,H_2,H_T,0` is not by itself novel.

### Sülzle (2009)

Competing B2B marketplaces demonstrate that intermediary ownership and competition can be formally analyzed in matching-market settings. Again, intermediary competition is occupied territory.

### Gal-Or (2020)

Two competing dating platforms segment heterogeneous users according to quality/compatibility and pricing. Compatibility-based sorting is therefore not open territory.

### Strategic matching-quality investment

The 2022 recommender-system paper makes platform matching quality endogenous through investment. Therefore `x_i` cannot earn novelty merely because it improves match quality.

## Whole-game distinction

The candidate differs from this family not by “having matching,” but by provider objectives and strategic architecture:

- two providers are chosen/financed by separate regional welfare maximizers;
- their controls are costly matching-capability investments rather than user prices;
- a third profit-maximizing intermediary chooses price after observing public investments;
- firms do not relocate; allocation among intermediaries changes productive partner access and regional incidence.

## Hard-kill warning

If Stage 3 expresses `M_i` as a generic scalar service-quality index and all provider competition can be rewritten as standard vertical/horizontal platform differentiation, the matching-specific distinction disappears.
