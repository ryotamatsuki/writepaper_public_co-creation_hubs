# Strategic Feedback Map

## Benchmark feedbacks

### B1 — one regional public hub + metropolitan private intermediary

Potential feedback:

\[
x_i\rightarrow D_T\rightarrow p_T^*\rightarrow D_i/W_i.
\]

This is close to one-public/one-private platform competition and cannot be the sole contribution.

### B2 — two regional public hubs, no metro intermediary

Potential feedback:

\[
x_i\rightarrow \text{allocation }H_i/H_j/0\rightarrow W_j\rightarrow BR_j.
\]

This is close to interregional facility/input competition.

### B3 — two regional public hubs + fixed metro outside option

`H_T` affects participation and substitution, but `p_T` does not react. The strategic network remains essentially public-public.

## Full game G

The distinctive candidate network is

\[
(x_1,x_2)\rightarrow D_T(x_1,x_2,p_T)
\rightarrow p_T^*(x_1,x_2)
\rightarrow h^*(\theta)
\rightarrow (W_1,W_2)
\rightarrow (BR_1,BR_2).
\]

Thus each public best response contains both a direct rival-public effect and an indirect metropolitan-price effect.

Conceptually,

\[
\frac{dW_i}{dx_i}
=
\left.\frac{\partial W_i}{\partial x_i}\right|_{p_T}
+
\frac{\partial W_i}{\partial p_T}
\frac{\partial p_T^*}{\partial x_i},
\]

with additional cross effects through `x_j` in equilibrium.

This equation is a decomposition target, not yet a proved formula for a selected model.

## Candidate Stage 4 objects

1. `dp_T^*/dx_i`.
2. `d BR_i/dx_j` with and without the private-price channel.
3. `x_i^N-x_i^SP`.
4. masses/types of `creation`, `quality improvement`, `displacement`, `unaffected` projects.
5. dependence of the above on partner-set geometry.

At least one must be qualitatively different in `G` from `B1-B3` for the architecture to survive.
