# Innovation / Technology Intermediary Audit

## Formal theory

Howells (2006) establishes innovation-intermediary functions as an important institutional object. Hoppe & Özdenören (2005) formalize intermediation in innovation and sorting expertise. Banal-Estañol, Macho-Stadler & Pérez-Castrillo (2018) formalize heterogeneous university-industry matching. Calcagnini, Giombini, Liberati & Travaglini (2019) model endogenous matching between technology-transfer offices and innovative firms with search intensity and advertising; search externalities can generate excessive or insufficient outcomes.

These papers eliminate claims that innovation matching, intermediary expertise, endogenous search intensity, or university/firm sorting are new primitives.

## Public/regional intermediary evidence

Duan & Jin compare public innovation intermediaries in two Japanese regions. Kohsetsushi research documents local public technology-center intermediation and heterogeneous firm outcomes. Feser documents cooperation/competition among innovation/creativity labs. Recent reviews/typologies of public innovation intermediaries remain largely institutional/empirical rather than solving the candidate strategic game.

## What was not located

The audit did not locate a formal innovation-intermediary paper with all of:

- two decentralized regional governments choosing matching capability;
- a profit-maximizing metropolitan intermediary strategically pricing against both;
- immobile project choice among all three providers plus no-use;
- regionally anchored partner opportunity sets;
- regional-vs-social equilibrium investment comparison.

This absence does not establish novelty. It supports moving to a minimal formal model to determine whether the architecture produces anything new.

## Empirical bridge

The institutional literature supports the plausibility of regional public intermediation and heterogeneous take-up, but empirical realism cannot rescue a theoretically absorbed model.
