# Whole-Game Absorption Test

## v1.1 test

A strong absorption verdict requires either:

1. one prior model that reproduces the economically relevant players, objectives, strategies, timing, endogenous allocation, strategic-feedback network, equilibrium and welfare through direct relabeling/restriction; or
2. an existing theorem that makes the intended full-game result an immediate corollary.

Neither condition is established at Stage 2R-MC-G.

## Closest models

### Damiano & Li (2008)

Two competing matchmakers use prices to sort heterogeneous participants. This is close on endogenous matching-market choice and sorting, but the providers are private price setters; there are no two region-specific welfare-maximizing public investors and no metropolitan follower price responding to public investments.

**Classification: STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE on matching allocation; not whole-game absorption.**

### Takahashi (2004)

Governments strategically invest in public facilities in a two-stage spatial model and welfare is analyzed. This is close on decentralized public investment competition.

**Fatal limiting threat:** if `M_i(theta,x_i;S_i)` can be replaced without loss by scalar facility quality, the public-public layer becomes generic facility competition.

**Classification: STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE on public investment; not whole-game absorption.**

### Inoue, Kamijo & Tomaru (2009) and related local-public-firm work

A local public firm maximizing local welfare competes with a private firm in a spatial location/price game. Related work considers strategic interaction between local governments and two local public firms. Regional welfare objectives are therefore known.

**Classification: STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE on regional objective/mixed competition; not whole-game absorption.**

### Kim (2024)

One public and one private two-sided platform compete in a linear-city/full-coverage environment; public/private pricing and investment incentives are analyzed.

**Classification: STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE on mixed platform competition; not whole-game absorption.**

### Liu, Reshidi & Rivadeneyra (2026)

One welfare-maximizing public payment platform competes with one profit-maximizing private platform. Private fees respond and participation/switching matter.

**Classification: strongest single public-private strategic-response threat; not whole-game absorption.**

### Multiple local public/private mixed oligopoly

Dadpay & Heywood (2006), Han (2008), Bárcena-Ruiz & Garzón (2020), and related models establish that multiple public firms and private firms can coexist and interact strategically.

**Classification: COMPONENT OVERLAP / STRUCTURALLY CLOSE on player-objective mixture; not matching-game absorption.**

## Conclusion

\[
\boxed{\textbf{EXACT WHOLE-GAME ABSORPTION NOT LOCATED}}
\]

This is a bounded search conclusion through 2026-09-02, not proof that no such paper exists anywhere.

The architecture remains vulnerable to later formal collapse. Stage 3/4 must show that the three-node best-response network and opportunity-set matching produce an economic result not obtainable from the benchmark families alone.
