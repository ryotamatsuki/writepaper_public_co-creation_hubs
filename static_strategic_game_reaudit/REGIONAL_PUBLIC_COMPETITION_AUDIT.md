# Regional Public Competition Audit

## Takahashi (2004)

Takahashi studies governments that strategically invest in public facilities, with a two-stage spatial structure and welfare analysis. This kills any broad claim that “local governments strategically invest in facilities to attract/use beneficiaries” is new.

For the present paper Takahashi is the decisive reduction test: if the matching technology can be summarized without loss by scalar facility quality, the public-public investment layer is likely a relabeling of known public-facility competition.

## Walz (1996) and regional public-input competition

Walz develops a model in which two regional governments compete for a mobile oligopolistic firm through public provision of local inputs. Subsequent public-input/agglomeration work studies strategic provision and decentralized inefficiency.

The candidate differs by fixing firm/project location and making **intermediary choice** rather than firm location the endogenous allocation margin.

## Inoue, Kamijo & Tomaru line

Interregional mixed-duopoly work places a local public enterprise under a local-welfare objective and studies strategic competition against a private firm. The 2007 working-paper material also studies horizontal strategic interaction between neighboring local governments and the case in which two local public firms remain publicly owned.

Thus “two local governments with local-welfare objectives strategically interact” is not novel.

## Other regional competition

Tax, environmental-policy, subsidy and local-input competition all establish that decentralized regional governments may over- or underprovide policies because they ignore cross-border incidence or compete for mobile tax bases/firms.

## Surviving distinction

The candidate must derive any decentralization wedge from **matching allocation among immobile projects and partner opportunity sets**, not reuse a mobile-firm business-stealing story under new labels.

A valid Stage 4 result should identify a primitive matching-specific channel into `BR_i(x_j)` and the planner wedge.
