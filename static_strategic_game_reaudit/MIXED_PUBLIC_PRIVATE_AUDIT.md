# Mixed Public-Private Competition Audit

## Kim (2024)

Kim studies a public and private platform in a two-sided mixed duopoly. The model uses a linear-city/full-coverage framework and examines prices and investment incentives. The result that private and public platforms react differently because of different objectives is therefore known.

The candidate differs in having two regional public strategic players, regional rather than aggregate public objectives, productive matching rather than taste-location differentiation, an uncovered option, and the timing `public investment -> private price`.

## Liu, Reshidi & Rivadeneyra (2026)

This is the strongest current single-paper threat to the metropolitan-response story. A welfare-maximizing public payment platform competes with a profit-maximizing private platform; public entry changes participation and switching, and the private platform can respond strategically by changing fees.

Consequences for the candidate:

- private fee response to public competition is **not** novel by itself;
- market creation versus switching/displacement is **not** novel by itself;
- welfare-maximizing public versus profit-maximizing private provider competition is **not** novel by itself.

What remains to test is whether **two decentralized public matching investments jointly induce a private price response that changes the public-public equilibrium and regional-vs-social investment wedge**.

## Inoue et al. (2009)

A local-public/local-welfare provider competing against a private provider is also known in regional mixed duopoly. Its strategic variables are location and price in a spatial service/product model, not matching capability and nationwide intermediary pricing.

## Verdict

`MIXED PUBLIC-PRIVATE COMPETITION` is an occupied literature family. The candidate survives only if the second public government and matching-opportunity geometry create a full-game theorem beyond one-public/one-private results.
