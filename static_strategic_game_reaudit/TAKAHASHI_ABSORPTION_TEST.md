# Threat T1 — Takahashi Absorption Test

## Prior-art object

Takahashi (2004), “Spatial competition of governments in the investment on public facilities,” studies governments that strategically choose public-facility investments in a two-stage linear-economy model, emphasizing strategic interaction, spatial relationships and welfare.

## Similarities

- multiple governments;
- costly strategic public investment;
- beneficiaries can respond to facility attractiveness;
- strategic cross-effects between government choices;
- decentralized-vs-welfare implications.

## Material differences in the candidate

- the public action is intended to change a productive matching process over heterogeneous partner opportunities rather than a scalar facility/service quality;
- projects are regionally immobile and change intermediary rather than location;
- a profit-maximizing metropolitan intermediary chooses a strategic price after public investments;
- firms have an explicit no-match/no-use option;
- regional incidence depends on which productive partners are reached.

## Stage 2 verdict

`NOT EXACTLY ABSORBED — FATAL REDUCTION THREAT REMAINS`.

## Formal kill condition

If Stage 3 shows that all economically relevant information in `S_i` and `theta` can be summarized by a scalar

\[
q_i=q_i(x_i)
\]

such that firm choice and government payoffs are unchanged, the project loses its matching-specific architecture and moves sharply toward generic public-facility/platform quality competition.

Therefore Stage 3 must demonstrate why at least one relevant equilibrium/welfare object depends on opportunity-set/project-fit structure rather than only on expected scalar quality.
