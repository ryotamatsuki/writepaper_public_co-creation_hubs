## Objective asymmetry that matters

The public hubs are not profit-maximizing platform firms. Each regional government values its own residents and real regional incidence. The metropolitan intermediary values private profit. This objective mixture is itself not novel—mixed oligopoly has long analyzed public/private objective asymmetry—but it changes the strategic feedback once combined with regional matching allocation.

## Information/timing discipline

The baseline gives the metropolitan intermediary a follower position with respect to public matching investment. This should be interpreted as long-run public capability investment followed by more flexible private pricing. Alternative simultaneous timing is not part of Stage 2 and may be a later robustness check only if the baseline survives.
