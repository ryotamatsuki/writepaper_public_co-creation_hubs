# Stage 18 — Existing-model shortlist for simultaneous public/private co-creation-hub entry
Date: 2026-10-27
Status: SOURCE-VERIFIED MODEL SELECTION / NO NOVELTY CERTIFICATION / NO SPNE PROOF.

## Precise research task
No incumbent hub initially (N0=0). Municipality G and prospective private P independently/simultaneously choose build/entry at stage 1; both may enter, so realizable counts are 0,1,2. Later locations, two-sided fees, separate innovator/partner subscription, endogenous actual matches, noncontractible joint-experiment investment, ex-post Nash bargaining. Municipality maximizes W, private entrant maximizes profit. Solve lower subgames, derive four outcome payoffs W00,W10,W01,W11 and private entry payoffs pi01,pi11. Solve first-stage simultaneous Nash (possibly multiple equilibria). Compare *identity and number* of facilities to second-best social planner controlling entry only and keeping downstream play fixed.

## Model contenders — verified primary publication / exact fit / how to build on
1. Jeong-Yoo Kim (2024), **Mixed duopoly in two-sided markets**, The Manchester School 92(3):281–295, DOI 10.1111/manc.12467. A welfare-maximizing public two-sided platform vs profit-maximizing private platform, linear-city full coverage, differentiation, platform quality investments, competition and pricing; the CLOSEST skeleton for Stage 2-4 mixed platform structure. Does NOT in its abstract characterize both build decisions simultaneous or bilateral ex-post investment/hold-up; verify full paper before claiming any exact omission. High absorption risk if merely adding binary entry. https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/manc.12467
2. Basak & Petrakis (2021), **Social efficiency of entry: Implications of network externalities**, Journal of Economics & Management Strategy 30(4):820–829, DOI 10.1111/jems.12431. Exactly two-stage game with firms simultaneously deciding whether to enter; then quantity competition with compatible/incompatible positive network demand, welfare-optimal entry comparison. Both TOO-MANY and TOO-FEW network-entity results ALREADY known. It is one-sided network-goods oligopoly, not a public/private two-sided matchmaker. The methodology for stage-1 entry thresholds is excellent, but introducing public-player objective, cross-side L=mu N_I N_R and endogenous investor effort must materially change the economics. https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jems.12431
3. Tan & Zhou (2021), **The Effects of Competition and Entry in Multi-sided Markets**, Review of Economic Studies 88(2):1002–1030, DOI 10.1093/restud/rdaa036. Pricing and entry with heterogeneous membership benefits, cross-side externality, socially excessive free entry and example socially insufficient. Stronger publication-level absorption kill test; good tractable pricing alternative to Stage 12 Hotelling, but symmetric profit-seeking platforms in source. https://academic.oup.com/restud/article-abstract/88/2/1002/5879999
4. Ghosh & Morita (2007), **Social desirability of free entry: A bilateral oligopoly analysis**, IJIO 25(5):925–934, DOI 10.1016/j.ijindorg.2007.02.002. Supplier-buyer bilateral bargaining with endogenous entry; strong input suppliers may imply socially insufficient entry even under homogeneous final-good production. Direct kill test: bargaining generating under-entry is NOT NEW; differentiated two-sided matching and PUBLIC player's chosen entry, endogenous sunk experimentation effort would need a non-absorbed threshold result. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167718707000203
5. Hattori & Yoshikawa (2016), **Free entry and social inefficiency under co-opetition**, Journal of Economics 118(2):97–119, DOI 10.1007/s00712-015-0469-x. Competing firms jointly depend on common-property resources; early commitment vs joint quantities/investment changes direction of entry distortion. Natural alternative if knowledge/testbed shared stock is core. Already shows both too-many and too-few, so ownership and endogenous matching differentiation are essential. https://ideas.repec.org/a/kap/jeczfn/v118y2016i2d10.1007_s00712-015-0469-x.html
6. Armstrong (2006), **Competition in two-sided markets**, RAND J Econ 37(3):668–691 DOI 10.1111/j.1756-2171.2006.tb00037.x. Monopoly vs competing single-homing and competitive-bottlenecks two-sided pricing; use as low-cost analytic microfoundation for two sided join fees and externalities. No state-owner binary entry comparative theorem. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1756-2171.2006.tb00037.x
7. Caillaud & Jullien (2003), **Chicken & Egg: Competition among Intermediation Service Providers**, RAND J Econ 34(2):309–328. Two competing matching firms with registration and success-conditional transaction fees, optional multi-registration; user participation may have multiple equilibria and winner-take-all. Mandatory kill test for global price-game existence and matching functional details, not easiest first baseline. https://www.edegan.com/pdfs/Caillaud%20Jullien%20%282003%29%20-%20Chicken%20and%20Egg.pdf
8. Matsumura & Matsushima (2004), **Endogenous Cost Differentials between Public and Private Enterprises: A Mixed Duopoly Approach**, Economica 71(284):671–688 DOI 10.1111/j.0013-0427.2004.00394.x. Mixed Hotelling spatial public/private objectives AND endogenous cost-reduction investment, but not both-sides matching. An alternative lineage for endogenous locations/investment; absent user sorting endogeneity it won't answer two-sided question. https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.0013-0427.2004.00394.x
9. Zhang & Li (2013), **Endogenous R&D spillover and location choice in a mixed oligopoly**, Annals Regional Sci 51(2):459–477 DOI 10.1007/s00168-013-0556-2. Endogenous multihoming and insufficient platform entry. Use as later robustness for dual-platform participation. https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/joie.12345

Number of trading buyers chosen endogenously with bilateral Nash bargaining; investment may be higher with fewer partners. Direct kill test for contact-scope n=1/2 extension, not primary platform entry base. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167268114002182

## Ranked models for user
A — **Preferred research base: Kim (2024) + Ghosh/Morita (2007) + entry protocol of Basak/Petrakis (2021)**. Stage 1 simultaneous binary public build and private entry with costs FG,FP; Stage 2 endogenous location (relax only for tractable initial propositions); Stage3 fees; Stage4 innovator/partner attendance and matches L_j=mu N_j^I N_j^R; Stage5 PREMATCH sunk effort e_j; Stage6 ex-post bargaining. Search for sign changes in social/private additional entrant gains caused by endogenous hold-up and matching fragmentation vs market creation, not merely classic network or business stealing.
B — **Shortest path to a fully solvable theorem: Basak/Petrakis (2021) quantity-competition entry, replacing the network-consumption term with a two-sided matching technology and a public objective**; potentially depart from desired physical facility/Hotelling specificity. Use as independent solvability control/benchmark.
C — **Knowledge commons / co-opetition: Hattori/Yoshikawa (2016) + public ownership entry + endogenous matching/cross-hub spillovers**; existing prior heavily absorbs simple entry sign reversal.
D — **Pricing microfoundation: Armstrong (2006)/Tan/Zhou (2021) + public-player entry**; downside full-coverage zero participation-extensive margin, after adding investor effort can give a tractable alternative to nonexistence-prone Hotelling.
E — **Multi-homing contact continuation: Adachi/Sato/Tremblay (2023) or Caillaud/Jullien (2003) + matching investment + mixed public objective**; hard global user fixed-point equilibrium, a robustness stage, not base.

## Crucial NOT-A-NOVEL-MECHANISM check
With Stage 11's AFTER-MATCH effort choice, e*=beta A/c independent of subscription masses, so match-specific innovation behavior enters only via fixed S=beta(1-beta/2) A²/c; simply adding a bilateral Nash bargain does NOT create a new endogenous *investment-margin* mechanism and is likely absorbed by Armstrong/Tan/Basak.
A meaningful model should let the innovative enterprise make noncontractible effort BEFORE match realization, for example with success probability q_j=mu N_j^R:
   expected innovator payoff = q_j beta A e -c e²/2 => e_j*=q_j beta A/c.
This introduces nontrivial network-thickness investment complementarity absent the prior matched-project-afterward benchmark. But pre-match effort and cost timing is a MODEL CHANGE requiring empirical justification and rigorous audit vs Hattori/Yoshikawa and Ghosh/Morita. Prove a sign reversal from these forces; do not just assert.
Also distinguish public facility ownership from optimal public subsidization/procurement: W_public vs W_private+subsidy must be compared to claim a PUBLIC OWNERSHIP justification.
