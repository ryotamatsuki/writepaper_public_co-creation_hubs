# Model assumptions, audit and unresolved identification problems

## Restricted Stage 3 game
- A continuum of potential firms with mass M and type x uniform on [0,1].
- Service characteristics of public hub and private hub are fixed at 0 and 1 in the computed benchmark.
- Firm utilities: U_G(x) = v-p_G-t*x^2, U_P(x) = v+delta_n-p_P-t*(1-x)^2; not participating yields zero.
- The public actor maximizes total regional surplus, including consumer surplus and producer profits but net of private and public fixed establishment costs.
- The private entrant maximizes p_P times its participating market mass less F_P.
- Pilot investment e_n occurs before private entry; its net direct social value H_n is additive to downstream adoption value and is not scaled with M in the archived benchmark.
- Pilot return coefficient r_n=alpha*n/(1+sigma*(n-1)); privately selected effort e_n=r_n, spillover delta_n=gamma*n*e_n.
- Public-sector intervention at fixed cost F_G is chosen anticipating the private entry condition. Private entrant is unique; additional entry is excluded.
- Private-only monopoly has an outside option and partial demand coverage, assuming 0<v<3t. Duopoly calculations use an interior sharing condition, 0<=delta_n<t, and sufficiently large v for the full market to be covered.

## Useful formulas (fixed hub characteristics)
Private-only gross profit = (2*M*v/3)*sqrt(v/(3*t)).
In public-private duopoly: p_G=p_P=t+delta_n; private share=(t+delta_n)/(2*t); private gross operating profit=M*(t+delta_n)^2/(2*t).
Conditional incremental gross welfare from private entry over public-only = M*(t+delta_n)^2/(4*t).
No-public welfare is zero if a private monopoly does not enter; otherwise monopoly gross surplus minus F_P.
Build policy W_n = M*(v-t/3)+H_n-F_G + 1{entry under n}*(DeltaS_n-F_P).
Government compares max_n W_n to W_no_public (the corresponding private-entry equilibrium).

## Mandatory methodological caveats
1. The price results require full coverage and interior competition; using them for a no-entry monopoly would be invalid.
2. Welfare is price-invariant only under assumed quasi-linear utility, no marginal public funds cost and no pricing distortions other than allocation. These are strong assumptions.
3. The spillover to private customers is generated by pilot investment but the matching/bargaining microfoundation is not modeled. The label "endogenous spillover" is valid only within this reduced-form effort-then-entry mechanism.
4. The private entrant may face a discontinuous regime switch at its fixed-cost threshold. State a tie-breaking rule or report both possibilities at equality.
5. The binary matching scope n is not the endogenous number of realized partners; no bilateral consent, bargaining, exclusivity or project failure has been modeled.
6. Program value H_n does not scale with M; this is a modeling normalization and has to be justified or replaced for empirical/public-finance interpretation.
7. Public-private co-existence is not assumed to imply physical hub necessity. A subsidy or digital platform may create the same spillover more cheaply.
8. The Stage 1 model used different share-dependent investment and scope preferences from Stage 3. The sequence is a research progression, not one closed consistent general model.
9. Expressions for fully endogenous location from preliminary discussion are candidate first-order conditions only, without a solved global equilibrium, and are intentionally excluded from certified results here.
