# Stage 11R P1f — adversarial predecessor/absorption audit
Date: 2026-10-28 JST. Applies to the **unchanged PR #61 theoretical model** and the model-specific government cross-interaction results in PR #62 / P1f. No claim that a literature search proves absence of a theorem.

## Definition of the exact candidate
The government first-stage payoff is induced by (i) cross-region skill-weighted matching and new-product Cournot profits allocated by region, (ii) two active private innovation intermediaries reoptimising prices after all public entry deviations, (iii) two discrete public basic-access choices. Our candidate is the *primitive formula* for the weak-matching strategic cross-effect and the explicit sign condition `M_A>M_Acrit`, `M_B<M_Bcrit`, plus a rigorously certified nonempty no-pure equilibrium **cost rectangle** in the specific four-actor game. Merely identifying asymmetric substitutes/complements or a mixed equilibrium is not the claimed novelty.

## Formal nonabsorption test
1. The **strategic game induced by the two public-entry decisions** is exactly an instance of a generic 2x2 game with one complement and one substitute under sign restrictions. Therefore existence of a unique fully mixed equilibrium and absence of pure equilibrium are mathematically **absorbed by standard normal-form game theory** (including examples of strategic heterogeneity).
2. The **primitive-to-game mapping** (`Gamma_i = N_iN_j[M_i A_i + omega_i B_i]` as gamma tends to zero) is a legitimate additional analytical result of this particular model. It can be presented as a sufficient-condition corollary explaining why regions differ. But deriving the game's coefficients from an economic model **does not prove** it is independent of models with more general surplus externality functions. In Kalamov–Staal, the sign of public provision interaction is already allowed to vary by underlying welfare-curvature primitives; those primitives may be able to encode our incidence mapping.
3. Zennyo confirms that low-cost private-entry dominance and asymmetric public/private-type configurations are familiar and must not be main priority claims.
4. Hua's inaccessible theorem text and the exact Peitz-Sato appendix comparison prevent asserting `non-absorbed` as a fact. Peer-reviewed priority is not identifiable from abstract-level comparisons.
5. Original benchmark government costs generate an existing **pure** equilibrium; the new mixed equilibrium occurs only in a changed, narrow governmental cost window. Its interpretation is a possibility theorem, not an observed government randomisation prediction.

**Referee verdict:** `model-specific new derivation / formal generalized novelty NOT certified / JICT primary contribution HOLD`. A 2x2 mixed equilibrium alone is not a defensible stand-alone theoretical contribution. This work could enrich the appendix or a limited main-text comparative-static paragraph if claims are accurately delimited, but the current basis does not justify a full rewrite.
