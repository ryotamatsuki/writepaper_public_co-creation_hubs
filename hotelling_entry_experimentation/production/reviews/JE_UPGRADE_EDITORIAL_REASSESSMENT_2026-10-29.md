## 1. Mathematical objection: closed on the identified point

The revised proposition explicitly includes `p_b>0`, using the pre-existing definition. The supplement now states the same restriction at the proof's outset. The original identity is

`S_i^0 = p_b K_i - omega_i zeta^2 Pi^2/(2k)`.

Under `K_i<0`, positive `p_b`, and the maintained nonnegative/positive restrictions, this is strictly negative. At the previously admitted degenerate zero baseline it would instead be zero. Adding the positive-baseline condition is therefore the appropriate minimal domain correction, not a new economic mechanism. The current explanation no longer asks a referee to repair the statement mentally by importing an unstated strict hypothesis.

I continue to accept the reconstructed derivative and weak-matching coefficient in my earlier report. The current change does not require a new optimisation problem or a different numerical example. The independent verification report supplies separate execution evidence for unchanged outputs and displayed mathematics; I do not claim to have rerun those checks in this reassessment. In particular, the general proposition is not newly Lean-formalised. **This particular mathematical-scope objection is closed.**

## 2. What the stronger predecessor maps establish

The new maps are substantive because they identify candidate theorem objects, try variable and objective substitutions, and report the failed hypotheses. Examples include exclusive matched pools versus common matching with a direct premium, positive transaction-surplus restrictions versus adverse local incidence, logit platform shares versus upper-tail nonparticipation, and an optimal contract menu versus an independently priced posted-fee allocation evaluated by a separate government. These are stronger evidence than merely counting governments or attaching regional labels.

The updated Ostrizek–Sartori comparison also uses the February 2023 author manuscript, rather than treating a 2021 working paper as an unquestioned final publication. Kim–Li–Xu's entry-fee underparticipation result is a valuable adverse comparison: it removes a tempting but untenable claim that private over-exclusion despite a harmful participation effect is itself a new general insight. The manuscript now recognises that prior result.

Three qualifications are essential:

1. A failed literal parameter identification is not proof of non-absorption under every change of variables or limiting argument. The reports appropriately stop short of that conclusion.
2. Requiring a predecessor to print the identical fraction, the same Cournot terminology or the identical binary service alternative would set an artificially easy novelty test. The relevant question is whether its theorem yields the same economic inference by a short, hypothesis-preserving restriction. The reports' stronger functional/objective restrictions do address that question for the inspected statements.
3. A generic chain-rule decomposition may explain the mathematics without supplying an already published economic comparison. The current manuscript claims an applied boundary, not a new mathematical method or universal screening theorem. Elementary derivation is consequently not, by itself, a fatal absorption finding.

**Originality conclusion:** the specific conditional policy boundary is not shown to be directly absorbed by the inspected predecessor propositions, and the supplied maps give affirmative, source-scoped reasons for retaining it as an applied contribution. Generic screening, public/private coexistence, asymmetric public-entry welfare and the foreign-surplus wedge remain inherited. No evidence in the new package justifies reversing that distinction or calling the whole theory generally novel.

## 3. Are the remaining source gaps submission blockers?

The final Damiano–Li versions, exact GEB version-of-record changes and Hua's technical package remain unresolved in the precise sense stated by the source reviewers. An inaccessible text is never evidence that the target result is absent. The current manuscript should therefore keep author-version specificity where a technical exclusion comparison relies on that version; it should not imply a verified final-version identity.

These gaps have different editorial weights. Damiano–Li's final versions are close to the sorting component, but that component is already conceded as known, and the inspected matching objectives and feasible pools do not supply the present policy derivative. The publication-year Ostrizek–Sartori text substantially narrows the version uncertainty without pretending to remove it. Hua is an important public/private comparator, yet its verified abstract establishes component overlap that the manuscript already acknowledges; there is currently **no inspected theorem or other positive evidence** that it establishes the negative regional-incidence/complementarity derivative being claimed.

Accordingly, I would not impose an automatic journal-submission veto solely because those remaining texts could not all be obtained. That would effectively require universal absence proof and confuse bounded applied contribution with a claim to a new general theorem. The appropriate disposition is to retain the specific source-access limits in the audit, make no exclusion claim about Hua's unseen mathematics, and revise if a later accessible theorem actually absorbs the target. The manuscript's source-scoped assertions can be evaluated on their present evidence. This editorial judgment does **not** assert that a formal Stage-6 completeness condition chosen by the workflow owner has been met.

The strongest counterargument remains that an encompassing screening/public-option result could supply the policy inference under a short restriction even though the inspected named propositions do not. That is a real residual priority risk. It warrants restrained contribution language and a maintained falsifier, not an invented new general theorem to compensate for uncertainty.

## 4. Economic importance: improved explanation, unchanged limitations

The narrower opening question is materially better. The paper now asks for the affordable fixed-cost boundary of restoring basic access after private pricing, rather than promising a classification of heterogeneous regional market structures. Its most useful lesson is a policy-statistic distinction: a negative regional commercialisation return does not by itself determine how improved technology changes the value of basic access. The founder share operates twice, in private willingness to pay and in local commercialisation incidence; an intermediate band separates those roles.

This is a precise economic inference beyond the statement that externalities exist. It identifies when a decision based only on the sign of commercialisation incidence can misread the marginal value of access. It is not evidence that actual policymakers make that error, nor evidence of a large measured loss from doing so. The current text distinguishes those propositions.

A skeptical referee can still make a strong case for rejection:

- The positive level of access value is dominated by direct-service benefits. Complementarity moves the ceiling by a small amount in the numerical baseline, and a discrete entry switch is obtained near indifference.
- Matching is weak in the analytical result, partner participation is fixed, and willingness to pay is perfectly aligned with technical ability. The service premium does not improve research success. These choices make the result intelligible but limit portability to actual innovation advice.
- The four-party examples are rigorous witnesses, yet strict private profitability reduces their strategic interest, and no general entry comparative static is provided.
- The Cournot layer supplies economically meaningful incidence primitives, but the proposition can also be read as a scalar cutoff allocation evaluated with adverse external effects. The editor must decide whether the primitive interpretation adds enough economic knowledge.

The new scale paragraph confronts the first objection instead of advertising certification as quantitative importance. None of the remaining objections is solved by reorganising the paper. However, for a deliberately model-specific applied theory contribution, they are reasonable matters for editorial/referee assessment rather than automatic evidence of no publishable result. A diagnosis-only contribution need not add an optimal subsidy or a universally robust equilibrium theorem by fiat.

## 5. Comparison with the requested publication benchmarks

The source audit's Kawasaki reading supports the presentation sequence: private choice, meaningful boundary, separately defined welfare comparison, mismatch and policy limits. The current article now follows that sequence without importing a location mechanism or corrective transfer. It has narrower policy completeness than Kawasaki's intervention analysis, but that is acknowledged in the restricted policy question. Kawasaki itself also uses simulation-supported steps and has a cost-specification boundary; it should not be treated as a uniformly analytic generality requirement for this paper.

Benassi et al. supply the closer JE editorial analogy: familiar mixed-oligopoly models can support a contribution when an economically motivated perspective yields informative policy boundaries with disclosed reversals and restrictions. The current article meets that *form* of contribution more credibly than before. Its founder appropriation/access distinction is different from Benassi's income-demand channel and should be judged on its own. Benassi's publication is neither a guarantee of this article's novelty nor a rule that all applied threshold results warrant publication.
