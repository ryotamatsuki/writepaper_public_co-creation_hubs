## A. Kawasaki (2026): exact architecture and reading depth

Source: [published open-access HTML](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/boer.70066), *Bulletin of Economic Research* 78(4), 950–967. Read model §2, equilibrium §3, private choice §4, welfare §5, policy §6, competition-mode discussion §7, robustness/endogenous privatization §8, conclusion §10, and Appendix proofs of Propositions 1–3, 5 and 7. HTML notation contains apparent rendering/typographic anomalies; no source formula was transplanted.

One public/semi-public incumbent serves both regions; a constrained private entrant chooses one region before Cournot quantities. Regional convex costs and privatization govern the private-profit threshold (Propositions 1–2). A separate welfare comparison yields another boundary (Proposition 3), and Proposition 4 compares choices. Section 6 adds a corrective transfer. Crucially, the Appendix's welfare-polynomial shape and several policy signs use simulation; the reference is not uniformly an analytic generality benchmark. Alternative linear costs remove one direction of mismatch. Bertrand and demand-size asymmetry remain stated future questions.

**Editorial inference:** borrow the order private choice → economically meaningful boundary → separately defined welfare boundary → discrepancy → bounded implications. Do not borrow its economic mechanism, formulas, transfer instrument, or strength of claims. Our frozen manuscript has no general bidirectional full-game mismatch theorem or corrective-policy implementation result.

## B. Benassi, Chirco and Colombo (2025): scope discipline

Source: [published open-access HTML](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00712-025-00899-w), *Journal of Economics* 145, 129–145. Read Introduction, §§2–4, displayed equilibrium/welfare derivations, and table discussions; did not reproduce numerical tables independently.

The paper revisits De Fraja and De Fraja–Delbono Cournot models with income-related reservation-price distributions. It compares ownership welfare and derives cost/firm-count boundaries, explaining demand-size and elasticity channels. General income increases are analyzed using uniform-support changes; concentration uses a uniform-to-symmetric-Beta comparison. The DFD income result changes direction beyond the stated cost-curvature boundary. In the DF concentration example, the reported direction changes with the number of rivals. Alternative public objectives also matter. Accordingly, its abstract cannot be read as an unrestricted theorem that poverty or concentration always favors privatization.

**Editorial inference:** a known model can support a contribution when the new economic perspective yields informative, explicitly bounded policy comparisons. This is an argument for honest applied theory, not proof that our specific conditional sign band meets JE's novelty standard. Our model does not analyze income distribution and must not import that explanation.

## D. Canonical reduction and novelty decision

Strip regional and innovation labels. A monopolist sets an admission fee against uniform upper-tail participation. The selected skill mass is a deterministic nonlinear function of participation. Public entry restores the excluded basic-service margin while preserving an independently priced premium. Local welfare combines direct-service surplus, matched-pair incidence, fixed costs and a quality-weighted bilateral term. Summing two local welfares produces the familiar foreign-benefit wedge.

This reduction rejects novelty claims for monopolistic selection, an irreducible extra quality state, coexistence, and local/national externality accounting. It does **not** establish that the entire conditional derivative result is already a corollary: that conclusion would require an explicit applicable predecessor theorem, its hypotheses, and a substitution recovering the same endogenous price response and negative local commercialization condition. No such complete mapping was obtained in this task.

The strongest candidate remains the frozen Proposition 1's primitive incidence band, supported separately by the finite-matching conditional rectangle. Call it a **conditional model-specific applied-theory result**. Call author-level priority **UNRESOLVED**, not certified. The three full-game examples demonstrate possible configurations; their small cost interval and negative alternative-distribution/competition records reduce portability and cannot be erased by exposition.
