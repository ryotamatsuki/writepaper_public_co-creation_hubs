## 1. Exact candidate object, stripped of application labels

Use the manuscript's own notation and identities, with partner participation fixed:

- Uniform type `theta in [0,1]`; private willingness to pay `U + D theta`.
- `U = u + gamma N_j omega R_0`, `D = d + gamma N_j omega Pi lambda/2`.
- A seller chooses one admission fee, maximizing `N_i f (1-ell)`, with `ell=(f-U)/D` on the interior branch. Its optimum gives `ell=(D-U)/(2D)`.
- A separate binary public alternative restores the basic service to all types while premium demand is repriced independently.
- The evaluator's gross value difference is exactly the existing

`T = N_i[u ell - d/8 + d ell^2/2] + C[S^0 ell + lambda K ell^2/4]`,

where `K=M+omega Pi` and `C=gamma N_i N_j`.

The candidate is the **total derivative of this particular public/private allocation difference at the privately reoptimized fee**, while `K<0`. It is not the sign of monopoly revenue, efficient sorting, welfare at fixed participation, or the difference between arbitrary public and private platforms.

The Supplement already decomposes the derivative into cutoff-response and direct incidence terms and obtains the first-order coefficient

`M(d-u)^2 + omega Pi(d^2+3u^2)`.

This audit uses that existing result; it does not propose another theorem. The direct basic-service term and the adverse incidence term belong to a different evaluator than the seller. Applying the seller's envelope theorem to remove the cutoff response would change the candidate object.

## 2. Explicit mapping tests

### 2.1 Ordinary admission monopoly: DIRECTLY ABSORBED component

Normalize seller demand to one population and write `f=U+D ell`. The objective becomes `N_i(U+D ell)(1-ell)`. The interior fee and the rising cutoff when `D` rises are ordinary uniform-demand monopoly outcomes. Positive affine rescaling of type/value recovers them without complementary matching or public provision. Therefore the fee formula, exclusion, and average-quality movement are preparatory results, not theorem novelty.

What remains is not a new optimization method: it is the evaluation of the seller's allocation by the specified regional objective and its alternative basic-plus-premium allocation. This should be presented as a primitive policy boundary in applied theory.

### 2.2 Damiano–Li: monopoly sorting theorem test

Primary technical source: [author-hosted full PDF](https://lihao.microeconomics.ca/sites/lihao.microeconomics.ca/files/content/research/Luis.pdf), **June 2, 2004**, 36 PDF pages. Inspected printed pp.4–6, 13–15, 17–24: Proposition 3.5 (feasible weak sorting), Proposition 3.6 (virtual-match revenue), Propositions 4.2–4.3 (local/global path-supermodularity tests), and 4.4–4.5 (finite pooling/refinement). The final [Economic Theory article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00199-005-0058-2) is 2007; only its abstract was accessible. Preprint numbering is **not represented as final numbering**.

Attempt: map founder types to matching types; uniform distributions to `F=G`; fee exclusion to the cutoff. Product-type complementarity and incentive-adjusted revenue are inherited. An affine type transformation also represents the local linear willingness to pay conditional on fixed partner quality.

Failure: the inspected sorting theorems impose feasible paired pools with equal masses and unit conditional matching probability. Our fixed full partner pool and changing local participation affect pair volume; our `T` also includes direct basic-service restoration and a negative evaluator coefficient absent from virtual-match revenue. Substituting `K_i` for their virtual-match function confuses welfare incidence with information rents. Adding that objective and binary alternative would be a new application, not a corollary of the inspected theorem.

**Disposition:** PARTIALLY ABSORBED ingredients; candidate derivative NOT ABSORBED IN INSPECTED THEOREMS. Exact final-2007 proof-version differences remain a narrow UNVERIFIED gap.

### 2.3 Damiano–Li: competing-matchmaking theorem test

Primary source: [author/university PDF](https://economics.ubc.ca/wp-content/uploads/sites/38/2013/05/pdf_paper_hao-li-competing-matchmaking.pdf), **October 17, 2005**, 33 PDF pages. Inspected printed pp.13–20: Proposition 3.5/Corollary 3.6 (coverage), Proposition 3.7/Corollary 3.8 (differentiation), Lemmas 3.9–3.10 (monopoly versus planner), and the welfare comparison. The final 2008 publisher abstract was checked; full publisher text did not load. No final-version theorem-number identity is asserted.

Attempt: set uniform types, identify a lower threshold with basic access and an upper threshold with premium selection. This reproduces the broad coverage/quality tension.

Failure: in the inspected model, market thresholds determine the quality of distinct exclusive matching pools, and private competitors' objectives include reallocations between those pools. Our public-plus-premium users share one matching technology, the premium is direct service value, and its purchase does not move partners into an exclusive high-quality pool. Fixing the partner moment does not convert their endogenous pool-quality difference into our constant premium slope while preserving their revenue and feasibility equations. Their total-match-value planner also cannot supply our negative local coefficient and separate direct-service difference by a parameter renaming.

**Disposition:** PARTIALLY ABSORBED selection/coverage mechanisms; candidate derivative NOT ABSORBED IN INSPECTED THEOREMS. Final-2008 technical-version comparison remains UNVERIFIED.

### 2.4 Hagiu (2004): pricing and open-platform welfare

Primary sources: RIETI [04-E-035](https://www.rieti.go.jp/jp/publications/dp/04e035.pdf), 46 PDF pages, Proposition 1 (printed p.18), §4/Proposition 4 (pp.29–36), Appendix A2; and [04-E-036](https://www.rieti.go.jp/jp/publications/dp/04e036.pdf), 30 PDF pages, Proposition 2 (p.11), Proposition 3 (pp.16–17), surrounding conditions/proofs.

Attempt: map developer quality cutoff to `ell`, weighted application mass to selected skill, and open access to free basic access. The generic claim that openness and proprietary provision can have either welfare ranking is already absorbed.

Failure: their platform controls both-side pricing or endogenous application entry; quality affects user value, and the open-platform comparison replaces proprietary platform rules. Our public alternative coexists with the specialist and leaves the premium's matching productivity unchanged. Their decreasing marginal aggregate-value/entry-cost conditions do not give our evaluator's negative regional commercialization coefficient together with positive privately appropriable technology. Replacing their benefit function by the desired negative-incidence objective would alter the theorem's primitives and still would not produce our repriced public-basic alternative.

**Disposition:** general pricing/quality and indeterminate ranking PARTIALLY ABSORBED; our conditional sign band NOT ABSORBED IN THE INSPECTED PROPOSITIONS. This excludes those concrete propositions as direct parents, not every possible platform extension.

### 3.1 Ostrizek–Sartori: a substantive additional test

[Primary CSEF WP605 PDF](https://www.csef.it/WP/wp605.pdf), February 2021, 46 PDF pages; inspected §§2–4, Proposition 1 (PDF p.16), Theorem 1 (p.19), their objectives and proof passages. [Published 2023 GEB record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0899825623000143), DOI 10.1016/j.geb.2023.01.011, was found but full publisher retrieval returned 403. It is not a verified final-version substitution.

Attempt: identify payoff type with ability/value, influence with an ability weight, action with admission, and aggregate influence with selected skill. This is a genuine parent-class threat to any claim that screening with externalities is new.

Failure: the inspected propositions characterize efficient or optimal nonlinear contracts over actions, including incentive constraints and the principal's externality objective. Our seller offers a single posted fee, ignores regional incidence, and is evaluated against a predetermined binary service alternative. Restricting their feasible menu to this fee and binary admission does not preserve their optimal-contract theorem; setting their principal's externality objective to the government's objective changes our private decision. Their lexicographic allocation order does not imply a technology derivative of `T`.

**Disposition:** screening/externality parent class PARTIALLY ABSORBED; candidate NOT ABSORBED IN INSPECTED STATEMENTS. The publication-year author-version check below narrows this residual gap.
