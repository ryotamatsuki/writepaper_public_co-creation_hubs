## 1. What is added beyond familiar IO economics?

The useful economic object is a conditional *cost ceiling for restoring basic access*. Private returns to improved bilateral technology can raise the fee and exclude more founders from a valuable basic service, even while the successful ventures' net regional commercialisation effect is negative. The primitive share band translates that tension into an interpretable regional condition. That is more specific than saying that screening changes quality or public provision has spillovers.

However, the monopoly fee, upper-tail selection, coexistence premium and revenue-crowding argument are standard pricing implications of the maintained uniform linear-value specification. They are preparation, as the revised text now correctly states. The private provider has no technology-improving service margin; the object is participation allocation, not quality choice, innovation effort incentives or a general model of specialist advice. Because effort is additive in success, it is independent of ability and complementarity. Nothing new should be claimed on endogenous R&D effort.

The whole-game examples are economically legible but weak as an independent general contribution. ## 2. Independent reconstruction of the headline proof

Put d = Delta_i, gamma = rho*mu, U = u + gamma*N_j*omega*R0, eta = gamma*N_j*omega*Pi/2, D = d + eta*lambda. Conditional on full partner participation, the interior monopoly cutoff is ell = 1/2 - U/(2D), so ell_lambda = U*eta/(2D^2). This is a direct derivative of the privately optimal allocation; an envelope theorem for social welfare would incorrectly drop this term.

Differentiating the conditional ceiling gives:

T_lambda = [N_i(u+d*ell)+C*S0]*ell_lambda + (C*K/4)*(ell^2+2*lambda*ell*ell_lambda).

This matches the displayed exact derivative. As gamma tends to zero with rho and u fixed, ell tends to (d-u)/(2d) and ell_lambda has first-order coefficient N_j*omega*Pi*u/(4d^2). The baseline matching term C*S0*ell_lambda and the response part of the technological term are second order. The remaining first-order coefficient is proportional to

4*omega*Pi*u*[u+(d-u)/2] + (M+omega*Pi)*(d-u)^2
= M*(d-u)^2 + omega*Pi*(d^2+3*u^2).

Thus the displayed critical share is exactly the lower sign boundary, while omega < -M/Pi makes K negative. The finite-gamma sign is not obtained by simply treating this leading expression as exact: strict margins plus a uniform remainder on the stipulated compact feasible technology domain are required. Those bridges are visible in the supplement. Positive public value further needs the separate limiting service-value condition, not just a positive slope. No new theorem has been derived in this review.

## 3. Known-model-in-disguise / absorption attack

Eliminate ability moments using q = m - m^2/2. Conditional on fixed partner coverage, welfare is a scalar nonlinear participation-externality objective plus the service benefit and fixed costs. Private pricing selects m from an affine willingness-to-pay schedule whose slope depends on the technology parameter. The central sign calculation can consequently be represented as an ordinary allocation-response decomposition: the direct change of a negatively valued external effect is offset by the welfare consequence of a monopoly-induced shift in the access allocation.

This reduction defeats any claim of an irreducible second network state, a newly discovered quantity-quality trade-off, or a new general intergovernmental externality theorem. It does **not** by itself prove that a particular published theorem already contains the entire primitive band or four-party game. The existing register identifies serious parent threats in heterogeneous matching/platform pricing, public alternatives and cross-jurisdictional welfare. Its partially accessed sources prevent a complete theorem-by-theorem exclusion claim. The appropriate status for Proposition 1 is **UNRESOLVED at theorem-priority level**, with a defensible model-specific applied interpretation. Institutional nouns and an exact threshold formula are insufficient to change that status.

A defensible future Stage-6 closure must map the actual strongest predecessor theorem to the scalar cutoff/public-access problem, including objective, intervention, private choice and comparative-static parameter, and state precisely what economic inference remains unavailable.
