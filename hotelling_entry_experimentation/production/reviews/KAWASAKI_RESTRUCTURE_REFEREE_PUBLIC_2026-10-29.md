## Independent reconstruction of the public-provision argument

Starting from the stated regional welfare function, compare local private-only service with public basic plus private premium service, holding the partner at full participation and keeping the private intermediary active. Private-only mass and first moment are `1−ell` and `(1−ell²)/2`; public coverage gives `1` and `1/2`. The changes are therefore `ell` and `ell²/2`. With the partner's first moment `1/2`, the change in the skill-moment product is `ell²/4`. Subtracting the two stated direct-service benefits yields precisely the displayed `H_i`. The private fixed cost cancels. Regional fees are transfers and introduce no additional resource benefit. This reconstructs the local cost ceiling without invoking a general externality theorem.

For the partner, direct service benefits and its institutional costs do not change in this comparison. Its matching and skill terms change by the same allocation increments, evaluated with `S_j^0` and `lambda K_j`. Adding its welfare change gives exactly the displayed national-minus-local threshold. Thus the wedge is `Delta W_j`: it is not an independent strategic theorem. No assertion about corrective subsidies follows from this identity.

With positive foreign increment and a public cost strictly between local and national ceilings, the local government declines the same public addition that national evaluation favours. A negative increment permits the opposite **conditional** ranking. Neither sign establishes a complete four-actor equilibrium: private entry incentives, both governments' other deviations and admissible off-path pricing continuations remain necessary. The revised section says this explicitly. Equality of this wedge does not establish equality of all sixteen-profile optima or equilibrium selections.

The central derivative retains the change in the privately optimal cutoff instead of applying an invalid social envelope theorem. Its positive access-restoration component can outweigh a negative commercialisation component. This is an allocation mechanism, not a gain from counting fee transfers. The weak-matching condition and the separate finite-intensity conditional certificate answer different mathematical questions; neither expands the equilibrium domain.

## Policy interpretation and stop rule

The defensible question for a local government is the real cost it can justify for restoring basic access under specified private participation and foreign coverage, counting consumers, incumbents and founder earnings separately. National evaluation additionally counts the other region. A negative local venture surplus does not itself settle the service decision; nor does a positive public-cost ceiling prove that any particular programme, building, ownership arrangement or tax is efficient. A numerical example of underprovision cannot justify fiscal transfers that the model never analyses.

Absence of an optimal transfer instrument is a **bounded limitation**, not automatically a fatal defect for this diagnostic paper. If the editor instead requires corrective implementation or a general equilibrium regional classification, that demand cannot be met by editing the conclusion; it would be a separate research choice outside the frozen scope. No such expansion is recommended as an automatic next step.
