### Editorial reasoning and alternative attacks

- **JE** is first because its readership and actual Benassi-style publication practice match a *narrow but explicit microeconomic policy boundary*. It imposes less implicit demand for an empirically actionable innovation-policy design than JICT, and does not require the optimal public instrument that a JPET reader could reasonably expect. Main fatal attack: is the conditional appropriation-share band materially economically important, rather than an elementary cutoff response? Evidence of small magnitude and Beta/Bertrand non-portability must remain visible. **Subjective**: plausibly reviewable; not a prediction of send-out/acceptance.
- **JICT** wins on explicit application/policy alignment and is not to be treated as a lower-quality choice simply because of indexing. It has rigorous theory papers. But its editorial requirement of *empirical implications and policy effects* makes the existing no-data diagnostic vulnerable: adding a real empirical test is **not authorised** by this Stage 12.
- **JPET** has the closest public-economics institutional audience, but Lee (2026) is an instructive stronger comparator (explicit optimal R&D tax/subsidy, regular/nonregular networks, Bertrand test). This PR has no optimal government choice among policy instruments and no general four-party equilibrium theorem. Do not imply that Stage-12 journal fit alone repairs that gap.
- **RIO** invites theory and applied competition analysis, but the paper may be viewed as public-program cost accounting built from standard supplier screening. No antitrust/merger theory is claimed.
- **JRS/RSUE**: regional labels alone do not substitute for geographic primitives; moving there to improve ranking would violate the theory freeze. **IJIO**: comparatively crowded IO/platform/pricing novelty bar, no case to prioritize it without a stronger headline theorem. **Economic Modelling**: older fee gate excluded its submission-charge route; not reopened without an official new fee check.
