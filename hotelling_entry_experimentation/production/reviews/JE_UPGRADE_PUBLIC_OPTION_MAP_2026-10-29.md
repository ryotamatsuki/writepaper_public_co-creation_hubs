## 1. Exact target and the absorption test

Use the frozen manuscript's notation, conditional on partner full coverage:

\[
U=u_i+\gamma N_j\omega_iR_0,\quad D=d+\gamma N_j\omega_i\Pi\lambda/2,
\quad \ell=(D-U)/(2D),\quad C=\gamma N_iN_j,
\]
\[
T_i=N_i(u_i\ell-d/8+d\ell^2/2)
+C(S_i^0\ell+\lambda K_i\ell^2/4),\qquad K_i=M_i+\omega_i\Pi.
\]

The substantive target is the simultaneous **negative local commercialisation coefficient** and **positive derivative of the public fixed-cost ceiling** following private fee reoptimisation. It is not merely that entry affects prices, that some users lose, or that welfare is ambiguous. The exact frozen weak-matching boundary is

\[
\frac{-M_i}{\Pi}\frac{(d-u_i)^2}{d^2+3u_i^2}
<\omega_i<\min\{1,-M_i/\Pi\}.
\]

For direct absorption, an inspected theorem must admit a mapping that preserves the private allocation rule, local welfare measure, policy comparison and parameter variation. Merely appending a negative externality to a predecessor is an extension of its theorem, not a substitution establishing absorption. Conversely, failure of one mapping does not prove that all general parent results fail.

## 2. Primary-source access and theorem-level mapping

### A. Laine and Ma (2017)

**Source:** [final author-hosted JEBO PDF](https://people.bu.edu/ma/Laine-Ma-JEBO2017.pdf), pp. 338–343, 348–349 and Appendix pp. 350–351; model, Lemma 1, Proposition 1, and outside-option discussion §5.4 inspected. PDF also downloaded and text-extracted locally.

Lemma 1 fixes the indifferent type at `(c(q2)−c(q1))/(q2−q1)` and equalises both price-cost margins. Attempt `v→theta`, `q2−q1→d`, `p2−p1→f`: this reproduces the premium cutoff under coexistence, but not the private-only cutoff `(f−U)/D`, because it reallocates a covered population between qualities. Setting `p1=0` additionally requires the lemma's price equations, rather than being a freely imposed public rule. Proposition 1 chooses qualities through three equilibrium equations; our `d` is fixed. Section 5.4 acknowledges an outside-option allocation distortion but does not state the target derivative theorem. There is no argument slot in the inspected equations for an independent local `M_i` while leaving private willingness to pay unchanged.

**Disposition:** **PARTIALLY ABSORBED** for vertical sorting and private/public allocation distortions. **NOT ABSORBED IN VERIFIED SCOPE** for the target by Lemma 1/Proposition 1 substitution. This excludes those inspected results, not every public-option theorem.

### B. Peitz and Sato (2026)

**Source:** [published full HTML](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1756-2171.70008), §2.2 equations (1)–(2), Proposition 1, §3.5, and Proposition 6/§5.1 inspected.

Attempt `group k→region i`, `n_i^k→m_i`, `beta→lambda`. Equation (2) normalises platform shares to one within each group; our private-only mass can be below one with excluded founders. Equation (1) uses common logarithmic network coefficients plus extreme-value tastes; our slope `D` multiplies uniform founder ability and the partner's first skill moment. Thus the same substitution fails the demand hypothesis and loses the ability-linked fee response. Proposition 6 changes the active-platform set and ranks user-group surplus, conditional on equilibrium selection; it does not differentiate a basic-access fixed-cost ceiling with respect to technical complementarity. Identifying its two group surpluses with two regional welfares would omit incumbents and assigned venture returns.

**Disposition:** **PARTIALLY ABSORBED** for endogenous network prices and asymmetric welfare consequences of entry. **NOT ABSORBED IN VERIFIED SCOPE** for the target by Propositions 1/6. Their non-applicability is stronger than an institutional distinction, but is no literature-wide originality certificate.

### C. Liu, Reshidi and Rivadeneyra (2026)

**Source:** [Bank of Canada WP 2026-10 PDF](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/swp2026-10.pdf), model pp. 7–10, Propositions 2–3, 6–8 (pp. 13–22), Appendix A.6–A.8 inspected; March 10, 2026 version.

Attempt `consumer/merchant networks→regional founder populations`, `transaction gain b_i^c+b_i^m−c_i→K_i`. Model p. 10 explicitly requires this gain **positive**, so the identification violates the target `K_i<0`. Keeping positive transaction gain while appending negative local incumbent incidence requires an additional welfare term not contained in that mapping. Proposition 3's `omega_u,omega_p` weight user surplus and rival-platform profit; neither is the founder appropriation share `omega_i` affecting private `D`. Propositions 6–7 compare public/private competition with all-private competition. Proposition 8 fixes zero consumer price and optimises merchant price under insulated equilibrium; it supplies a sufficient welfare-improvement condition, not the derivative of a binary public-addition cost ceiling. Our premium-only price is not its two-part tariff.

**Disposition:** **PARTIALLY ABSORBED** for public competition, fee responses and conditional zero-price welfare. **NOT ABSORBED IN VERIFIED SCOPE** for the target under the inspected model/propositions; especially, the direct negative-surplus mapping violates a maintained hypothesis.

## 4. Contribution and JE implications

The strongest defensible applied question is whether local innovation-market losses are sufficient grounds to reject free basic access when technical improvements alter the privately selected founder population. The frozen result answers no, under a precise boundary: private gains can strengthen exclusion while the government values restored basic services. This clarifies an economic decision that neither generic asymmetric-user welfare nor an unpriced foreign benefit alone answers. It still relies on uniform aligned types, the stated direct-service role of the premium, partner coverage and weak matching; the separate finite-intensity certificate and equilibrium examples do not remove these boundaries.

A safe manuscript positioning statement is: “Existing mixed-service and platform results establish fee selection and heterogeneous welfare effects. In the inspected predecessor models, the regional commercialisation coefficient and the fixed-cost value of restoring fee-excluded basic access do not map to the same theorem objects. Our result gives an explicit conditional appropriation-share boundary for their opposing signs.” This is a source-scoped distinction, not a claim that a general screening theorem could never imply it.

**Gate decision:** **NO-GO for complete Stage-6 novelty certification from this report alone.** Component overlap is settled and three direct-absorption attempts fail within verified scope, but Hua's technical gap and broader parent-theorem absorption are unresolved. Do not silently collapse these into one resolved blocker or make JE acceptance conditional only on a download. The present task authorises better evidence and exposition, not new economic primitives. The editorial reconstruction remains usable and can now state a more precise, defensible result-level difference.
