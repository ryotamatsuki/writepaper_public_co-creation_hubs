\section{Related literature and the exact contribution boundary}\label{sec:literature}

Our contribution must be evaluated against several established literatures. We distinguish prior mechanisms from the narrower integrated provision game studied here. Neither free public provision, private fee screening, heterogeneous R\&D collaboration, downstream business stealing, nor cross-jurisdictional inefficiency is new by itself.

\paragraph{Public--private service competition.}
\citet{HuaChenZhang2016} examine a two-tier system containing a free public provider and a fee-charging private service, studying quality, waiting time and policy coordination. \citet{LaineMa2017} examine a mixed public--private quality-then-price game with heterogeneous consumers and multiple possible equilibria. \citet{Kim2024} analyses a mixed duopoly of two-sided platforms with full coverage. Accordingly, our novelty claim cannot rest on the existence of free and paid tiers or on consumer sorting. Instead, we study two independently deciding governments \emph{and} two independently entering private providers whose service choices affect cross-region founders' technical composition and the product-market welfare of both regions.

\paragraph{Endogenous platform pricing and sorting.}
\citet{Hagiu2004} already demonstrates trade-offs among proprietary/open access, pricing, diversity, network effects and business stealing. \citet{PeitzSato2026} develops asymmetric two-sided platform oligopoly with within-group and cross-group network effects, fees, entry and partial participation. Fee-driven participation and entry are inherited mechanisms, not our own first discovery. The distinctive object here is a \emph{technical skill moment} $q_i$ selected along with founder mass $m_i$, which enters the probability of successful matched venture innovation and subsequently the spatial incidence of new-product profits and incumbent losses.

\paragraph{Knowledge diversity and R\&D competition.}
\citet{BerliantFujita2008,BerliantFujita2011} establish the importance of heterogeneous knowledge composition in innovation; \citet{GoyalJoshi2003} model collaboration links formed before oligopoly competition; and \citet{DAspremontJacquemin1988} study cooperative R\&D with spillovers and Cournot production. Therefore complementarity of skills, collaboration links, and downstream product competition all have predecessors. Our specific integration makes premium-service selection endogenous \emph{before} bilateral skill-complementary product commercialisation and then uses the resulting regional surplus effects as inputs to public/private provision incentives.

\paragraph{Decentralised local public investment.}
\citet{CremerMarchandPestieau1997} already compare Nash and social-optimal discrete local public investment with interjurisdictional spillovers. \citet{LongWong2009} already place two local governments alongside two private local service firms that set prices, with public infrastructure determining private competitiveness. We therefore do not claim to discover the local/national welfare wedge or a four-actor geographic environment. In the present model, however, \emph{public basic services and private fee-based facilities each enter independently}; the cross-border welfare term is generated by selective founder matching and differentiated-product venture competition, rather than being imposed as an exogenous infrastructure benefit.

\paragraph{Empirical context, not identification.}
\citet{NishimuraOkamuro2026} report positive associations between city-level nonfinancial R\&D assistance, local support organisations and firms' innovation performance in a Japanese survey, including estimated public--local-support complementarity. This is consistent with coexistence as a relevant empirical possibility, but it does not test our four-entrant equilibrium or establish a causal spillover sign. The 2026 British Catapult controversy and European Digital Innovation Hubs pricing policy are similarly institutional motivations, not observations of the model's counterfactual.

\subsection{The bounded integrated contribution}\label{sec:contribution}
Our contribution is the \emph{joint characterisation} of (i) skill \emph{composition} as well as participation \emph{scale} selected by endogenous private tariffs, (ii) gains to new ventures and asymmetric consumer/incumbent effects across two regional product markets, and (iii) four independent public/private provision decisions evaluated both by local players and by a restricted two-region planner. Conditional analytic price and public-addition thresholds are complemented by a fully enumerated \emph{baseline numerical example} in which a skill-complementarity change switches the decentralised ownership profile while the restricted national benchmark can favour more public provision.

To clarify what is added, Table~\ref{tab:prior} maps the nearest existing mechanisms to the paper's additional conjunction. This is a \emph{model-specific} contribution, not a demonstrated first-ever sorting-induced ownership switch. A complete theorem-level absorption audit against every nearest predecessor is not available; stronger priority language would be unwarranted. We also do not establish the necessity of a public building, subsidy superiority, arbitrary-correlation robustness, global uniqueness of the private-private price game, or a first-best national optimum.
