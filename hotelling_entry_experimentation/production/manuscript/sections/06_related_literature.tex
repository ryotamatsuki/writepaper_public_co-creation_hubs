\section{Related literature and the contribution boundary}\label{sec:literature}

\paragraph{Private matchmaking, screening, and heterogeneous network value.}
\citet{DamianoLi2007} study an optimising monopoly matchmaker that posts type-contingent entrance-fee schedules to sort privately informed agents into complementary random bilateral matches; \citet{DamianoLi2008} analyse matching-market competition and the potentially inefficient sorting it produces. These papers already combine \emph{fee-induced selection with complementary match value}, so neither the product of two founder abilities nor price-dependent sorting is a new mechanism here. In our model the regional private providers do not control exclusive meeting places or assortative matching: each prices a distinct local founder population whose realised cross-region partners are complementary. A welfare-maximising public basic-access provider in each region adds an instrument absent from the private matchmaking models; a successful matched venture also competes against incumbent firms in two separate product markets.

\citet{Hagiu2004,Hagiu2004b} study proprietary and open platform design and developers' heterogeneous contributions, while \citet{VeigaWeylWhite2017} emphasise quality-weighted sorting and the differing network values of participants. Our current uniform upper-tail selection obeys \emph{exactly} $q_i=m_i-m_i^2/2$ in every service state, and therefore can be represented by a single nonlinear participation index. We do not claim the innovation of an irreducible second network state, independent mean quality, or the general quality--quantity trade-off. \citet{PeitzSato2026} further integrate platform fees, endogenous participation and entry, and \citet{LuMatsushimaShekhar2025} connect network externalities to competitive platform pricing. The specific posted-fee response in our model is a specialisation of familiar pricing and network feedback.

\paragraph{Public alternatives and endogenous private-market responses.}
\citet{HuaChenZhang2016} consider free public and paid private services under delay- and capacity-sensitive provision and fiscal policy; the published abstract verifies the presence of this mechanism, but its inaccessible full technical proof does not support a formal theorem-by-theorem exclusion claim. \citet{LaineMa2017,Kim2024} also study mixed public/private market competition. \citet{AtalCuestaGonzalezOtero2024} document the entry of public pharmacies and the resulting segmentation of private customers and increases in private drug prices. The public-private payment-platform model of \citet{LiuReshidiRivadeneyra2026} demonstrates private merchant-fee increases in the presence of a welfare-oriented public platform, through differentiated preferences and network fragmentation. Therefore public entry, private fee responses, adverse distributional effects and mixed provision are all known. Our public service has no rationing, a private premium does not directly improve matching, and the distinguishing policy object is \emph{regional venture-commercialisation incidence} interacting with a fee-induced basic-access exclusion margin.

\paragraph{Innovation policy, local market incidence and governmental objectives.}
\citet{TakaloTanayamaToivanen2013} analyse strategic interaction among R\&D project applicants, public funding agencies and private financiers with fixed project costs and financing frictions; they demonstrate that subsidy additionality and welfare can have a nontrivial relation. Thus the observation that an innovation-support programme's value cannot be judged by the count of supported projects is not itself new. Our public instrument is \emph{free basic intermediation} rather than project finance or R\&D subsidy, and premium payment does not raise the underlying research technology. \citet{DAspremontJacquemin1988,GoyalJoshi2003} study research cooperation and rivalry, while \citet{BerliantFujita2008,BerliantFujita2011} address heterogeneous knowledge. \citet{Lee2026} links local knowledge spillovers and oligopolistic rivalry to sign-dependent R\&D intervention. These studies establish that commercialisation benefits need not be positive for every market participant or region. What is specific here is that the sign of local venture-market incidence $K_i$ may be \emph{opposite} to the sign of the derivative of the local government's willingness to restore founder access with respect to technological complementarity.

\citet{LongWong2009} model two city governments and two private service firms in inter-city competition; the local-government/private-provider architecture alone is not new. Local and national investment incentives have long differed when cross-jurisdictional public-good benefits are not internalised \citep{CremerMarchandPestieau1997,MankiwWhinston1986}. Accordingly, Lemmas~1--3 are preparatory accounting/selection results, not priority claims. Nor is proof-oriented exact certification of the chosen equilibrium calibration a new theory of competition, although it makes the model's examples more credible.

\paragraph{The narrower contribution.}
The economically distinctive result is Proposition~1, specifically the \emph{primitive profit-share band} \eqref{eq:screen-inversion-band}: in a region where the new venture's commercialisation incidence is negative, higher technical skill complementarity can increase the publicly affordable cost of basic access because profit-related price screening excludes firms from socially valuable direct services. The condition is derived from reoptimised private fees and the local Cournot-derived $M_i$, not the assumption of a positive local innovation externality. An independent finite-$\gamma$ interval certificate proves this sign inversion on an explicitly stated continuous primitive rectangle. The model is more structured than a generic nonlinear participation-externality representation, but none of these differences is a formal proof that no general predecessor framework can nest the result. It is a \emph{conditional, model-specific applied-theory proposition}, not a universal new law of public options.
