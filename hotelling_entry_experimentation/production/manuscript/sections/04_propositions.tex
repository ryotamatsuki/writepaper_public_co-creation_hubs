\section{Public provision and regional economic characteristics}\label{sec:propositions}
The policy question is whether the real fixed cost of adding free basic access is justified by the change in regional welfare. The relevant regional characteristics are the consumer and incumbent effect, the share of venture profits retained by founders, and basic-service value relative to premium value. They enter a conditional comparison with the partner region fully participating; no general entry comparative static is assumed.

\subsection{Regional commercialisation incidence}
\paragraph{Lemma 1 (regional incidence).}
With full founder participation, fixed interior Cournot quantities and independent uniform abilities,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\partial W_i}{\partial\lambda}=\frac{C}{4}K_i,\qquad
 \frac{\partial(W_A+W_B)}{\partial\lambda}
 =\frac{C}{4}(M_A+M_B+\Pi).
 \label{eq:P1}
\end{equation}
Local and aggregate signs can differ because incumbent displacement and assigned venture earnings differ across regions.
The derivatives follow from $q_Aq_B=1/4$ and $K_A+K_B=M_A+M_B+\Pi$.
No participant or price response is included in this full-coverage calculation.


\subsection{The conditional public cost ceiling}
\paragraph{Lemma 3 (conditional public addition).}
Fix the other region at $(m_j,q_j)=(1,1/2)$ and let region $i$ have a private-only continuation with $\ell_i\in(0,1)$.
Define the direct-service gain before government cost by
\begin{equation}
 H_i=N_i\left(u_i\ell_i-\frac{\Delta_i}{8}
                         +\frac{\Delta_i\ell_i^2}{2}\right).
 \label{eq:H}
\end{equation}
Adding free basic access while retaining the private specialist is locally preferred exactly when $F_{G_i}\leq F_i^{\mathrm{local}}$, where
\begin{equation}
 F_i^{\mathrm{local}}=H_i+C\left(S_i^0\ell_i+
                          \frac{\lambda K_i\ell_i^2}{4}\right).
 \label{eq:localcutoff}
\end{equation}
The national comparison uses
\begin{equation}
 F_i^{\mathrm{national}}-F_i^{\mathrm{local}}
 =C\ell_i\left(S_j^0+\frac{\lambda K_j\ell_i}{4}\right).
 \label{eq:wedge}
\end{equation}
This foreign increment may be positive, zero or negative.

To verify the result, public access raises local mass by $\ell_i$ and local ability moment by $\ell_i^2/2$; with the opposite moment $1/2$, skill exposure rises by $C\ell_i^2/4$.
The direct-service change is $H_i$ and private real costs cancel.
Adding the neighbouring welfare increment gives the national threshold.
The same accounting extends to full-coverage corners using the realised cutoff.
It does not establish a full four-entry equilibrium or an optimum over fees, research allocations, subsidies or buildings.
The Online Supplement provides the continuation and accounting proofs.

\subsection{Regional primitives and technical complementarity}\label{sec:diagnostic}
Consider the public-access margin from private-only provision ($P$) to free basic access coexisting with private premium provision ($B$). Fix the partner jurisdiction in $G$ or $B$, so $(m_j,q_j)=(1,1/2)$; the private intermediary in region $i$ nevertheless reoptimises its fee in the private-only continuation. This is a \emph{conditional institutional comparison}, not a global comparative static of all four stage-1 entry decisions.

Write $d=\Delta_i$, $\gamma=\rho\mu$, $C=\gamma N_iN_j$, and set
\begin{equation}
 U=u_i+\gamma N_j\omega_iR_0,\quad
 \eta_i=\frac{\gamma N_j\omega_i\Pi}{2},\quad
 D=d+\eta_i\lambda,\quad
 \ell=\frac{D-U}{2D},
 \label{eq:screen-primitive}
\end{equation}
on the interior branch $0<U<D$. The private-only fee is $(U+D)/2$, whereas free basic provision restores access to all founder types, with premium pricing at $d/2$.
Using Lemma~3, the gross local public-access value, before government fixed cost, is
\begin{equation}
 T_i(\lambda)=N_i\left(u_i\ell-\frac d8+\frac{d\ell^2}{2}\right)
        +C\left(S_i^0\ell+\frac{\lambda K_i\ell^2}{4}\right),
 \qquad K_i=M_i+\omega_i\Pi .
 \label{eq:screen-threshold}
\end{equation}
Differentiating while keeping the research-market primitives, $\rho$, and $u_i$ fixed gives the exact identity
\begin{equation}
 \begin{split}
 T_i'(\lambda)={}&
 \left[N_i(u_i+d\ell)+CS_i^0\right]
       \frac{U\eta_i}{2D^2}\\
 &+\frac{CK_i}{4}
       \left(\ell^2+\frac{\lambda\ell U\eta_i}{D^2}\right).
 \end{split}
 \label{eq:diagnostic-derivative}
\end{equation}
The first line describes the access restored by the rising private fee-induced exclusion cutoff, including its baseline matching component; the second is the regional return to the technically different additional research pairs. The two need not have the same sign.

\paragraph{Proposition 1 (the screening--incidence inversion).}\label{prop:inversion}
Maintain the model's uniform and perfectly aligned technical-skill/premium-value distribution, a positive interior Cournot continuation, an interior local private-only fee, partner full participation, $M_i<0$, $0<u_i<d$, $\Pi>0$, and nonnegative $R_0$. Fix a compact interval of technologically feasible $\lambda\geq0$. Define
\begin{equation}
 \omega_i^{\mathrm{crit}}
 =\frac{-M_i}{\Pi}
     \frac{(d-u_i)^2}{d^2+3u_i^2}.
 \label{eq:omega-critical}
\end{equation}
Suppose the interval
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\qquad
 \omega_i^{\mathrm{crit}}<\omega_i<
            \min\left\{1,\frac{-M_i}{\Pi}\right\}
 \qquad}
 \label{eq:screen-inversion-band}
\end{equation}
is nonempty. As $\gamma=\rho\mu\downarrow0$, holding $\rho$ and the basic-service value $u_i$ fixed, the conditional public-access threshold satisfies, uniformly on the specified $\lambda$ interval,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\partial T_i}{\partial\lambda}
  =\frac{\gamma N_iN_j}{16d^2}
    \left[M_i(d-u_i)^2+
          \omega_i\Pi(d^2+3u_i^2)\right]
       +O(\gamma^2).
 \label{eq:main-primitive-slope}
\end{equation}
Consequently, at every sufficiently small positive $\gamma$,
\begin{equation}
 K_i<0,\qquad S_i^0<0,\qquad
 \frac{\partial T_i}{\partial\lambda}>0
 \label{eq:main-inversion}
\end{equation}
throughout the $\lambda$ interval. If in addition $0<u_i<2d/3$, then $T_i(\lambda)>0$ uniformly at sufficiently small positive $\gamma$. Thus public provision can have a positive affordable fixed cost and become more valuable as technical complementarity rises even though its incremental research-commercialisation surplus is strictly negative in the financing region.

The critical share $\omega_i^{\mathrm{crit}}$ strictly decreases in $u_i/d$ on $(0,1)$. At $\omega_i=0$ and $M_i<0$, by contrast, the private screening response to $\lambda$ disappears and $T_i'(\lambda)=CK_i\ell^2/4<0$ on the interior branch. These are implications of the existing primitives rather than new policy instruments. The proof and the uniformity conditions are in the Online Supplement. The proposition concerns the \emph{gross conditional public cost ceiling}, not an all-parameter four-player subgame-perfect equilibrium or an optimum across taxes, subsidies and physical facilities.

Two distinctions matter. First, $S_i^0=p_bK_i-\omega_i\zeta^2\Pi^2/(2k)<0$ when $K_i<0$; the adverse regional return applies to baseline as well as skill-complementarity-induced ventures. Second, \eqref{eq:screen-inversion-band} provides a nonempty feasible band only when $\omega_i^{\mathrm{crit}}<\min\{1,-M_i/\Pi\}$; positive basic-service value alone does not guarantee that its intersection with feasible profit shares is nonempty. The sign restriction is first-order in weak matching, while Section~\ref{sec:numerical} separately certifies a finite-matching closed rectangle for the original economic environment.


\paragraph{What regional characteristics identify.}
The market effect $M_i$ aggregates consumer and incumbent changes derived from the existing Cournot demand, cost and incumbent-count primitives; it is not an independent urban or rural indicator. The share $\omega_i$ assigns joint-venture profits to the region's founder. Proposition~1 identifies an intermediate share: large enough for private technological returns to increase exclusion, but small enough that incumbent and consumer losses still dominate locally. The ratio $u_i/\Delta_i$ describes the value of free basic access relative to specialist services. Its proved effect is a decrease in the critical profit share on the stated interior domain, not a general monotonicity theorem for the level of public value.

Candidate populations $N_i,N_j$ and matching intensity $\rho\mu$ enter both private willingness to pay and the scale of regional matching surplus. The leading weak-matching slope displays their scale explicitly, holding the remaining primitives fixed. It does not prove that larger populations or stronger matching always raise the public cost ceiling or change the four-party equilibrium. Similarly, the Cournot formulas identify how market structure feeds into $M_i$ and $\Pi$, but the paper has no general signed comparative static for every demand, cost or incumbent-count change. The scope of the research question is therefore conditional analytical boundaries plus certified provision examples, rather than a complete classification of heterogeneous regional economies.

\paragraph{Reading the strict baseline-incidence claim.}
The Online Supplement's proof of the strict inequality for $S_i^0$ explicitly uses a positive baseline success intercept $p_b>0$. The numerical baseline satisfies that condition. The statement above is retained from the frozen version; its strict baseline-incidence conclusion should be read with that proof condition visible. If both $p_0$ and $\zeta$ are zero, the baseline term is zero rather than strictly negative. This inherited scope issue does not change the fee-selection slope or the certified finite-matching example, but it prevents treating the printed statement as a theorem on the degenerate zero-baseline case.
