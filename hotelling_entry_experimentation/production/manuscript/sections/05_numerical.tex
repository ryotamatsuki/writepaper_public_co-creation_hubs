\section{A deliberately fragile numerical equilibrium example}\label{sec:numerical}

The previous sections establish conditional mechanisms; they do not establish that the entire four-provider entry configuration changes for generic parameter values. We therefore use a computational example for a narrower purpose: to demonstrate that the endogenous \emph{composition} of participating founders can affect the public/private configuration, while revealing exactly how close the example is to an entry threshold. We keep the original Stage~8 calibration intact rather than choosing more favourable numbers after the referee's objection.

\paragraph{Calibrated environment.}
The baseline has $(n_A,n_B)=(5,2)$, $(N_A,N_B)=(3,1)$, $\Delta_A=\Delta_B=1$, $\rho=0.4$, $\mu=0.25$, $(\omega_A,\omega_B)=(0.8,0.2)$, $(F_{G_A},F_{G_B})=(0.09,0.0405)$, $(F_{P_A},F_{P_B})=(0.65,0.24)$, $p_0=0.05$, $\zeta=0.005$, $k=0.0004$, $\chi=0.9$, $(v_A,v_B)=(0.15,0.10)$, and $(w_A,w_B)=(0.22,0.45)$. The homogeneous comparison replaces $\lambda\theta_A\theta_B$ by $\lambda/4$. Thus it preserves average innovation success under \emph{full} participation; it does not hold the fee-sensitive participation composition fixed.

The equal-mean comparison has identical fully covered $(B,B)$ national welfare, $2.407438368575148$, in both specifications at $\lambda=0.5$. With a private-only region $B$ facing $(B,\cdot)$ in $A$, the heterogeneous model produces $f_B=0.6410074280605$, $m_B=0.639945174957$ and $q_B\approx0.435180261481$. It is the endogenous fee and selected skill composition, not a changed fully participating technology, that alter government $B$'s private-versus-joint provision incentive.

\paragraph{How narrow is the reversal?}
Table~\ref{tab:fragility} exposes the critical $B$ government fixed costs rather than hiding the near-indifference values inside a chart. The local threshold equals the baseline cost plus the local welfare change from replacing $(B,P)$ with $(B,B)$.

For the heterogeneous and homogeneous cases at $\lambda=0.5$ to have opposite local public-addition signs, their common fixed cost must lie in the exact-to-displayed-precision interval
\begin{equation}
 0.040043229233<F_{G_B}<0.040531670102.
 \label{eq:fragility}
\end{equation}
Its width is $0.000488440869$, only about $1.21\%$ of the baseline $F_{G_B}$. The latter is $0.000031670102$ below the upper endpoint, approximately $0.078\%$ of the cost. Increasing $F_{G_B}$ by $0.0001$ reverses the heterogeneous model's \emph{local} preference for joint provision at $\lambda=0.5$. This is a material vulnerability of the selected illustration, not a solver error or an argument for relocating the baseline to a more convenient point.

The full baseline first-stage pure equilibrium changes at approximately $\lambda^\star=0.3511840318401$, where region~$B$'s binary public-entry best response ties. At the tie, a uniqueness claim is inappropriate. The comparison also has a separate national dimension: despite the difference in $B$'s own decision, the foreign regional gain from additional cross-region matches is positive in all three displayed cases. Thus local non-entry does not imply that total welfare favours non-entry.

\paragraph{Expanded reproducibility.}
The Stage~9 implementation computes all sixteen entry profiles under the selected fee continuation and tests four one-player deviations per profile. The independent Stage~11 checker separately reconstructs those deviations from the exported forty-eight-row payoff panel. A post-referee sensitivity program, \texttt{verification/referee\_sensitivity.py}, recomputes all sixteen downstream states across predeclared grids in $(\lambda,F_{G_B})$, $(\Delta_B,F_{G_B})$, $(N_B,F_{G_B})$ and $(\chi,F_{G_B})$; it records the corresponding numerical first-stage Nash and restricted planner selections as a CSV, plus a black-and-white phase plot. Cost-only changes are evaluated using the fact that the government fixed cost is absent from fee and product-market best responses. These grids establish \emph{which cases were checked}, not a continuum or arbitrary-demand robustness theorem.

\section{Negative portability and institutional limitations}\label{sec:robustness}
The frozen adverse tests under $\operatorname{Beta}(2,2)$ skills and differentiated Bertrand competition do not reproduce the same full ownership switch at the fixed baseline costs. We retain these as \emph{negative portability results}. Because the original complete alternative-technology code and parameter mapping have not been reconstructed within the revised independent verification package, we do not present new sensitivity estimates for those cases or certify a successful robustness replication.

The link $z_i=\theta_i$ between private-service valuation and technical ability is essential. If selection on willingness to pay does not select productive ability, the premium-sorting effect cannot simply be identified with higher quality of collaboration. The benchmark does not endogenise physical site quality, subsidy contracts, procurement, taxation, alternative product niches, or privately supplied digital-only access. Consequently, a result about free basic \emph{service provision} is not evidence that building a publicly owned innovation centre is necessary.
