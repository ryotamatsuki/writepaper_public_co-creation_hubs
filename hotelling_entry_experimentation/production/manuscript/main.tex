\begin{abstract}
Should publicly financed innovation intermediation coexist with privately priced services when complementary technical skills are dispersed across regions? We construct a two-region game in which two local governments and two private intermediaries independently decide on provision, private intermediaries set fees, heterogeneous firms select into cross-region R\&D collaboration, and successful new ventures compete against incumbent producers. Conditional analytical results distinguish the number and technical composition of participating firms, characterize private-price selection, and derive the difference between a local government's and a restricted national planner's public-addition thresholds. A numerically verified baseline exhibits an ownership-configuration change induced by skill complementarity, while its equal-mean homogeneous-success counterpart does not exhibit the same change. The contribution is the integrated, model-specific interaction of these previously studied mechanisms; it is not a universal ownership-reversal theorem or a proof that physical public hubs are necessary.
\end{abstract}
