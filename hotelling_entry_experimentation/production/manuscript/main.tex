\begin{abstract}
What determines the maximum fixed cost a local government is willing to bear to restore basic innovation access when a private specialist selects founders through its fee? We study two local governments and two private intermediaries with endogenous fees, founder selection, cross-region research matching and differentiated Cournot competition. Conditional on full participation in the partner region, separate local and national public-cost ceilings depend on service values and regional commercialisation incidence. A primitive founder-profit-share band shows that stronger technical complementarity can raise the local ceiling even when additional ventures reduce regional commercialisation surplus. Private exclusion increases the basic-service value recovered by public access. The result is analytical for sufficiently weak matching under a positive baseline success intercept; exact rational certification establishes its signs on a continuous two-parameter region at the original finite intensity. Three separately certified four-party entry examples compare decentralised provision with national welfare maximisation over sixteen configurations: two exhibit public underprovision and one coincidence. Their configuration switch requires a narrow public-cost band and fails a recorded alternative ability distribution. The contribution is a conditional policy boundary linking private screening to regional incidence, rather than a general equilibrium classification or a quantitative case for constructing public facilities.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Innovation intermediation; Public access; Participant selection; Mixed provision; Regional welfare.
