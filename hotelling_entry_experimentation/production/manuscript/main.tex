\begin{abstract}
When can better innovation technology make free public intermediation more valuable even if successful ventures reduce the financing region's welfare? We study independent entry by two local governments and two private intermediaries, endogenous posted fees, ability-selected cross-region research matching and differentiated-product Cournot competition. Our central conditional proposition derives a primitive profit-incidence interval in which skill complementarity raises private screening and a government's willingness to restore basic access despite strictly negative local commercialisation returns. The sign condition is analytical and uniform for sufficiently weak matching; a separate exact-rational certificate establishes the same opposing signs, a positive public-access value, and strictly increasing value on a continuous two-parameter region at the original finite matching intensity. The proof applies to a private-only-to-coexistence comparison with the partner region fully participating, not an unrestricted four-entrant comparative static. Three independently certified entry equilibria illustrate the channel, but their ownership switch requires a narrow public-cost band and fails under a recorded alternative skill distribution. We distinguish access value from successful-project welfare, local from national incidence, and a robust conditional mechanism from a fragile discrete policy switch. The result supplies a model-specific public-access diagnostic, not a general priority claim or a case for physical hub construction.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Innovation intermediation; Public access; Participant selection; Mixed provision; Regional welfare.
