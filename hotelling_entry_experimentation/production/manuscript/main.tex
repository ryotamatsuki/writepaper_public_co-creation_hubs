\begin{abstract}
We study a two-region innovation-intermediation game with local public providers and private fee-setting intermediaries, heterogeneous complementary firm capabilities, and downstream product-market effects. Conditional analytical results characterize private-price sorting and the discrepancy between local public-entry and restricted national welfare thresholds. A calibrated numerical example features a change in decentralized public--private provision as skill complementarity strengthens, while the national restricted-instrument benchmark can rank provision differently. The results are bounded by the specified firm-type distribution, price-continuation conditions, and product-market assumptions. They do not establish the necessity of physical facilities or a universal public-entry reversal.
\end{abstract}
