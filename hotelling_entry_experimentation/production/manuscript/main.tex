\begin{abstract}
Can a publicly financed basic innovation service complement a privately priced specialist when founders' productive abilities are heterogeneous? We study a two-region model in which two local governments and two private intermediaries independently enter, private operators post fees, cross-region founders form skill-complementary research ventures, and successful new goods compete with incumbent producers. We distinguish participating founder mass from an unnormalised skill moment and derive conditional fee-selection and regional welfare-incidence identities. The four-player numerical illustration admits a change in public/private co-provision between a skill-complementary technology and an equal-mean homogeneous alternative. This comparison is sharply sensitive to the smaller region's public fixed cost: the contrast requires a cost interval approximately 1.21 percent of that baseline cost, and negative portability checks remain under alternative skill distributions and product-market competition. The paper contributes a bounded synthesis of established screening, knowledge matching and decentralised public investment mechanisms, not a general new welfare theorem or a rationale for constructing a publicly owned physical hub.
\end{abstract}
