Dear Editor-in-Chief,

Please consider my manuscript, “Private Innovation Intermediation, Regional Incidence, and Public Access,” for publication as an original research article in the *Journal of Economics*.

The paper asks when a local government can justify the fixed cost of providing free basic innovation-intermediation access alongside a fee-setting private specialist, even when the regional market return from additional ventures is negative. In a model with endogenous private fees, heterogeneous founder participation, complementary cross-regional research and differentiated Cournot competition, Proposition 1 derives a conditional founder-profit-share interval in which greater technical complementarity increases the value of restoring basic access despite adverse regional commercialisation incidence.

The contribution is a precise, bounded policy-cost comparison rather than a claim that private screening, mixed provision or interjurisdictional externalities are new mechanisms. The analytical result requires an interior private fee, full participation in the partner jurisdiction, a positive baseline success probability and sufficiently weak matching. Exact rational certification confirms the relevant signs on a finite-matching parameter region, while three independently certified four-party provision examples illustrate the distinction between local and national welfare assessments. The model does not establish a general four-player equilibrium comparative static or prescribe the construction of a physical facility.

I believe this narrowly stated applied-theory contribution and its transparent treatment of mathematical and economic limits are suitable for the *Journal of Economics*.
