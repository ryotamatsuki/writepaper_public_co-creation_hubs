Dear Editor-in-Chief,

Please consider our manuscript, “Private Innovation Intermediation, Regional Incidence, and Public Access,” for publication as an original research article in the *Journal of Economics*.

The article asks when publicly supplied basic innovation-intermediation access can be justified despite a negative regional commercialisation return from additional ventures, once fee-based selection by a private intermediary is taken into account. In a model with two regions, private fee setting, complementary research matching and product-market competition, Proposition 1 identifies an explicit conditional founder-profit-share interval. With a positive baseline success probability, interior continuations, full participation in the partner region and sufficiently weak matching, greater technical complementarity raises the region's maximum justifiable cost of basic public access despite negative regional venture incidence.

Fee-induced sorting, mixed public/private provision and cross-jurisdictional externalities are established in the literature. The paper's narrower contribution is an analytical policy-cost boundary integrating those channels under the maintained regional incidence and service-access comparison. A separate exact-rational finite-parameter certificate and three bounded four-party entry examples illustrate, but do not generalise, the result. The article does not establish an unrestricted subgame-perfect equilibrium theorem or an optimal physical-facility construction policy.

We believe the paper's analytical microeconomic approach, explicit public/private welfare boundary and carefully qualified economic interpretation are appropriate for the *Journal of Economics*.
