# Mechanism Card

## Phenomenon

Regional public innovation investments can switch from mutual reinforcement to strategic crowding-out once a shared private innovation hub is allowed to reprice.

## Friction

Cross-side participation links focal innovation projects to partners, experts, investors, and other supporting actors. Public facilitation therefore changes not only local project allocation but also network participation across the competing intermediation routes.

## Strategic response

A shared private hub chooses its paid access price after public investment changes. When one public hub expands, private demand changes and the private hub optimally adjusts its price.

## Equilibrium effect

With the private price fixed, the cross-side network channel can make the two public investments strategic complements. With endogenous private pricing, the induced price-response contribution can dominate that positive channel, yielding

`BR_i^{B3′} > 0 > BR_i^{G′}`.

## Welfare effect

The strategic-sign reversal is not a welfare-sign reversal. Under the frozen aggregate accounting, private access payments are transfers, but decentralized public investment still creates a real cross-regional coordination externality through allocation and network participation.

## Empirical implication

Where the relevant private-hub price is more flexible, the cross-regional public-investment response should be less positive than under relatively rigid private pricing and can become negative under the model's sign-reversal conditions.
