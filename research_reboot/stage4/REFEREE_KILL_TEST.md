# Stage 4 — Referee Kill Test

Date: 2026-09-01

## Attack 1

> “This is just a biased platform with different labels.”

**Assessment: FATAL.**

After stripping institutional labels, a principal chooses bias, bias reduces participation on another side, and the principal self-limits bias because participant exit lowers the value of the intermediary. This is a generic biased-intermediary/platform participation trade-off. The exact functional form is simpler than existing platform models, but simplicity is not a contribution.

## Attack 2

> “Outside participation is just a standard cross-side network effect.”

**Assessment: MAJOR to FATAL.**

The Stage 4 model uses congestion rather than a canonical positive cross-side network effect, but the economic role is the same class of endogenous participation discipline: a platform/intermediary decision changes user participation, which feeds back into platform/principal value. Rysman-type two-sided participation theory and Zennyo's bias-participation feedback substantially occupy this logic.

## Attack 3

> “The result follows because the regional principal values outsiders less.”

**Assessment: MANAGEABLE for P3; FATAL for P1 as a contribution.**

P3 survives this attack because it concerns the regional sponsor's own payoff and disappears when participation is fixed. P1 does not survive as a distinct contribution because a planner/regional wedge can exist even under fixed participation through direct outsider welfare accounting.

## Attack 4

> “The intermediary itself does nothing strategic.”

**Assessment: MAJOR.**

This is intentional under the Stage 3 minimality contract: the intermediary implements `alpha`. But it also means the model is fundamentally a principal/platform policy-choice problem, not a distinct theory of intermediary organization. Adding an intermediary best response now would be feature accumulation and would require returning to Stage 3.

## Attack 5

> “Home bias is an exogenous taste parameter rather than an institutionally generated distortion.”

**Assessment: FATAL for the public-regional contribution.**

`A` measures the sponsor's marginal value of home priority. The model does not derive this value from funding rules, political accountability, governance, or contracts. Thus geographically bounded sponsorship is interpretation, not an endogenous economic source of bias.

Repair would require a new governance/agency mechanism, which is outside the Stage 4 contract.

## Attack 6

> “The local-welfare reversal is just concavity imposed by the payoff function.”

**Assessment: MANAGEABLE mathematically.**

The regional payoff is linear in both direct bias and pool thickness. Its concavity in `alpha` is generated by the outsider participation equation under fixed attention congestion:

`n(1+n)=v(1-alpha)`.

With participation fixed, `U_R` is linear and the reversal disappears. Therefore the reversal is not inserted through an ad hoc quadratic regional payoff.

However, this does not solve the novelty problem: a demand/participation response disciplining bias is standard economics.

## Attack 7

> “Public sponsorship is irrelevant; a private biased intermediary behaves the same way.”

**Assessment: FATAL.**

This is the decisive attack.

Replace the regional sponsor by any private principal that receives benefit `A alpha` from favoring one side and benefit `B n` from keeping another side active. Every Stage 4 equation and proposition remains unchanged.

Therefore public/regional sponsorship is not mathematically essential.

## Current-frontier reinforcement

The prior-art problem is not limited to the two original comparators.

- Reimers & Waldfogel's platform-bias welfare framework was revised in April 2026.
- Chi, Choi, Hahn & Kim (2026) characterize self-preferencing jointly with transaction fees and extend to consumer search.
- Xu et al. (2025) study a continuous bias control factor and nonlinear platform/matching outcomes.

The general bias/welfare space is active and structurally richer than the Stage 4 model.

Meanwhile, innovation-intermediary studies already treat neutrality, impartiality and trust as productive institutional attributes. Klerkx & Leeuwis (2008), Kant & Kanda (2019), Feser (2023), and Eldebo & Hjelm (2026 volume) make the institutional bridge plausible but do not create a new theorem.

## Overall referee verdict

The model is analytically coherent and P3 is a genuine result of its participation mechanism.

But the decisive publication question is not whether P3 is true. It is whether P3 constitutes a new economic mechanism.

It does not.

The public/regional interpretation can be removed without changing any equation, best response, equilibrium, or welfare ordering. That makes the contribution application-level rather than theorem-level.

**Final kill recommendation: NO-GO — RESULT TOO WEAK.**
