# Stage 4 — Exact Derivations

Date: 2026-09-01

## 1. Participation equilibrium

An outside participant enters iff

`c <= v(1-alpha)/(1+n)`.

With `c ~ Uniform[0,1]` and an interior cutoff,

`n = v(1-alpha)/(1+n)`.

Therefore

`n(1+n)=v(1-alpha)`

and the unique nonnegative solution is

`n(alpha) = [sqrt(1+4v(1-alpha))-1]/2`.

Let

`D(alpha)=1+4v(1-alpha)`.

Then

`n'(alpha)=-v/sqrt(D)<0`,

`n''(alpha)=-2v^2/D^(3/2)<0`.

For `0<v<2`, `n(0)<1`; therefore the upper support of the uniform CDF does not bind.

There is no interior participation-collapse threshold in this minimal model: `n(alpha)>0` for every `alpha<1`, and `n(1)=0`.

## 2. Regional sponsor problem

The reduced regional payoff is

`U_R(alpha)=A alpha + B n(alpha)`.

Derivative:

`U_R'(alpha)=A-Bv/sqrt(D)`.

Second derivative:

`U_R''(alpha)=-2Bv^2/D^(3/2)<0`.

Hence the regional problem is globally strictly concave.

An interior solution satisfies

`sqrt(D(alpha_R))=Bv/A`.

Thus

`alpha_R = 1 + 1/(4v) - B^2 v/(4A^2)`.

Equivalently, using `alpha=1-n(1+n)/v`, regional-optimal outside participation is

`n_R=(vB-A)/(2A)`.

The regional optimum is interior iff

`Bv/sqrt(1+4v) < A < Bv`.

The lower bound gives `U_R'(0)>0`; the upper bound gives `U_R'(1)<0`.

## 3. Local-welfare reversal

Because `U_R''<0`, the derivative is strictly decreasing in `alpha`.

On the interior region,

`U_R'(alpha)>0` for `alpha<alpha_R`,

`U_R'(alpha_R)=0`,

and

`U_R'(alpha)<0` for `alpha>alpha_R`.

Therefore excessive local priority lowers the regional sponsor's own payoff once `alpha` exceeds `alpha_R`.

This result is generated by the participation term in

`dU_R/dalpha = partial U_R/partial alpha + (partial U_R/partial n)n'(alpha)`

`= A + B n'(alpha)`.

The second term is negative and becomes more negative as `alpha` rises because `n''<0`.

## 4. Planner problem

At equilibrium the entrant gross payoff equals the participation cutoff `n`, so outsider aggregate net surplus is

`S_X=n^2/2`.

Planner welfare is

`W(alpha)=A alpha+B n(alpha)+n(alpha)^2/2`.

Derivative:

`W'(alpha)=U_R'(alpha)+n(alpha)n'(alpha)`

`= A-v/2-v(B-1/2)/sqrt(D)`.

Second derivative:

`W''(alpha)=v^2(1-2B)/D^(3/2)`.

Thus for `B>1/2`, planner welfare is strictly concave in `alpha`.

The interior planner solution satisfies

`sqrt(D(alpha_SP)) = v(B-1/2)/(A-v/2)`.

Therefore

`alpha_SP = 1 + 1/(4v) - v(B-1/2)^2/[4(A-v/2)^2]`.

Equivalently,

`n_SP=(vB-A)/(2A-v)`.

A convenient joint interior region for both regional and planner solutions is

`Omega = {0<v<2, B>1/2, v/2+v(B-1/2)/sqrt(1+4v) < A < vB}`.

The planner lower bound is stronger than the regional lower bound, so `Omega` guarantees both problems are interior.

## 5. Composition wedge

At the regional optimum,

`U_R'(alpha_R)=0`.

Using the planner decomposition,

`W'(alpha_R)=n(alpha_R)n'(alpha_R)<0`.

On `Omega`, `W` is strictly concave. Therefore its unique maximizer lies to the left of `alpha_R`:

`alpha_SP < alpha_R`.

Equivalently,

`n_SP > n_R`.

The difference follows from the endogenous participation response plus outsider surplus. It is not sufficient as a publication claim by itself because a planner-versus-geographically-bounded principal wedge is standard.

## 6. Fixed-participation counterfactual

Fix `n=nbar` exogenously.

Regional payoff:

`U_R^F(alpha)=A alpha+B nbar`,

hence

`dU_R^F/dalpha=A>0`.

So the regional sponsor chooses

`alpha_R^F=1`.

The local-welfare reversal disappears completely.

If outsider welfare is still counted for the planner while the entrant mass is held fixed, then

`S_X^F(alpha)=nbar*v(1-alpha)/(1+nbar)-nbar^2/2`,

and

`W_F'(alpha)=A-nbar*v/(1+nbar)`.

Thus a regional/planner difference can still arise in the fixed-participation model solely because the planner values outsider payoff. This is exactly the generic welfare-weight channel the project sought to avoid.

Consequently:

- P1 (`alpha_R>alpha_SP`) is not by itself sufficient evidence of a distinct mechanism;
- P3 (the regional sponsor's own local-welfare reversal) is participation-essential and is the strongest mathematical result of the minimal model.

## 7. Boundary cases

### `alpha=0`

Outside participation is maximal:

`n_0=[sqrt(1+4v)-1]/2`.

### `alpha=1`

Outside gross payoff is zero and

`n=0`.

Regional payoff is `A`.

### `n=0`

Consistent only with `alpha=1` in the interior-uniform benchmark.

### Full participation

For `0<v<2`, `n(alpha)<1` for all `alpha`; the full-participation cap never binds.

## 8. Nonempty open parameter region

Take for example

`v=1`, `B=1`, `A=0.8`.

Then

`n_R=0.125`,

`alpha_R=0.859375`,

`n_SP=1/3`,

`alpha_SP=5/9 ~= 0.555556`.

Regional welfare is maximized at the interior bias:

`U_R(alpha_R)=0.8125`,

while

`U_R(1)=0.8`.

Thus increasing bias beyond `alpha_R` lowers local welfare even though home priority directly enters local payoff positively.

This is not a knife-edge result: the joint interior set `Omega` is open and nonempty whenever `0<v<2` and `B>1/2`.

## 9. Algebraic interpretation

The model succeeds mathematically in producing:

1. endogenous outsider exit as home bias rises;
2. a unique interior local-welfare-maximizing bias;
3. a planner choosing less bias;
4. a local-welfare reversal that disappears when participation is fixed.

The remaining Stage 4 question is not algebraic. It is whether this is a distinct economic theorem or a stripped-down instance of existing biased-intermediary/platform-participation economics.
