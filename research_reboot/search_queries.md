## 10. Final intersection kill search

After Stage 1, the remaining blocker was whether a formal paper already jointly contained (i) an innovation intermediary with endogenous matching/search and (ii) strategic regional/local-government provision with spillovers and planner comparison. The following targeted searches were therefore run as the single Stage-2 conditional blocker check:

No exact joint model was located in this search. This is **not** used as standalone evidence of novelty. The Stage 2 classification instead rests on model-level comparison with the strongest located papers: Hoppe–Ozdenoren (2005), Macho-Stadler–Pérez-Castrillo–Veugelers (2007), Calcagnini et al. (2019), Carayol–Sterzi (2021), Cremer–Marchand–Pestieau (1997), and the local-buzz/global-pipelines literature.

## Citation-chain checks

For serious closest papers, searches included:

- earlier working-paper / published-version reconciliation;
- references cited as closest predecessors;
- same-author neighborhood searches;
- later papers citing or extending the mechanism where accessible;
- alternative keywords (`TTO`, `technology transfer`, `knowledge broker`, `intermediary`, `living lab`, `cluster organization`, `innovation pole`, `digital innovation hub`).

Particular chains inspected included:

- Howells (2006) → later intermediary systematic reviews and search/capability papers;
- Hoppe & Ozdenoren (2005) → TTO/intermediation theory and competition extensions;
- Bathelt et al. (2004) → Aarstad et al. (2016), Sotarauta et al. (2011), Wang et al. (2023);
- absorptive-capacity theory from Kamien & Zang (2000), Kaiser (2002), Leahy & Neary (2007), and Bae (2016);
- Japanese cluster/intermediary evidence from Kodama (2008), Nishimura & Okamuro (2011), and Fukugawa (2022, 2024).
