## 1. Executive novelty verdict

**Canonical final verdict: GO.**

Routing/status: **GO TO STAGE 3 — CANDIDATE MECHANISM SEARCH.**

This is a deliberately narrow GO. **No main contribution claim from the legacy paper survives Stage 2 as a defensible novelty claim.** The old `hub + pipeline` theory should not be repaired or resubmitted as such.

What survives is only a research space worth one Stage 3 mechanism search:

> **Can regional/jurisdictional provision of innovation intermediation generate a genuinely intermediation-specific distortion in the composition of local matching and extra-regional knowledge access—rather than merely reproducing standard innovation-intermediary matching models, standard interjurisdictional public-good spillovers, or the established local-buzz/global-pipelines narrative?**

The literature audit identified very strong formal prior art that was underweighted in the legacy paper. In particular:

- Hoppe & Ozdenoren (2005) already provide a formal theory of innovation intermediation with screening expertise, participation, matching, coordination failure, and intermediary competition;
- Calcagnini et al. (2019) already model endogenous matching between TTOs and innovative firms and show that search externalities can make technology progress inefficient, including a result where too-intensive intermediary search is harmful;
- Macho-Stadler, Pérez-Castrillo & Veugelers (2007) already provide a formal asymmetric-information/reputation rationale for a TTO;
- Carayol & Sterzi (2021) already endogenize the choice between direct and intermediary transfer;
- Cremer, Marchand & Pestieau (1997) already compare decentralized regional public-good investment with interjurisdictional spillovers against a social optimum;
- Bathelt, Malmberg & Maskell (2004) and subsequent work already establish the local-buzz/global-pipelines conceptual distinction;
- absorptive capacity is already endogenous in several formal IO/R&D models with planner and policy comparisons.

Accordingly, Stage 3 is authorized only to search for a **new interaction theorem**. It is not authorized to combine known components and label the combination as novelty.

## 3. Literature-family findings

### 3.1 Regional innovation systems / thinness

The thin/peripheral-region motivation is heavily occupied. Tödtling & Trippl already distinguish peripheral systems characterized by organizational thinness and argue for differentiated innovation policy. Oughton, Landabaso & Morgan identify the regional innovation paradox linking greater innovation need with weak absorptive capacity. Recent 2026 living-lab work explicitly embeds adaptive intermediaries within peripheral RIS.

**Implication:** `thin regions need differentiated innovation policy/intermediaries` is background, not a contribution.

### 3.2 Local interaction and external pipelines

Bathelt et al. provide the canonical local-buzz/global-pipelines framing. Sotarauta et al. extend this to local/digital buzz and global/national pipelines. Aarstad et al. are particularly damaging to the legacy complementarity story: local and international collaboration are not universally complementary, with substitution/subtractive effects for SMEs and complementarity concentrated in larger firms. Wang et al. also report nonlinear external-linkage effects.

**Implication:** the project cannot assume either complementarity or monotone external-link benefits.

### 3.3 Innovation intermediary theory

The field is not merely descriptive.

Hoppe & Ozdenoren (2005) provide a formal PBE framework in which an intermediary invests in expertise to distinguish profitable inventions and match creators with firms. They discuss inefficiency, coordination failure, and intermediary competition.

Macho-Stadler et al. (2007) formally explain a TTO through reputation and pooling under asymmetric information.

Calcagnini et al. (2019) embed technology transfer in an endogenous matching model with TTO search and firm advertising; both sides ignore externalities and excessive search can reduce technological progress.

Carayol & Sterzi (2021) make use of the TTO endogenous by allowing inventors to choose direct transfer instead.

**Implication:** `intermediaries lower matching/search/information frictions` and `decentralized intermediary behavior can be inefficient` are already formal prior art.

### 3.4 Public innovation intermediaries / organizational incentives

Russo et al. show that public intermediary behavior responds to measurable performance indicators and can diverge from longer-horizon policy objectives. Fukugawa's Japanese Kohsetsushi studies show that institutional changes in public intermediaries can generate heterogeneous and even negative performance effects. De Silva et al. show that public intermediary functions such as network building and knowledge integration produce different innovation profiles through distinct capabilities.

**Implication:** the legacy `visible short-run activity versus broader welfare` intuition is institutionally credible, but its microfoundation should not be an arbitrary myopic private evaluator. A principal–agent or organizational-design mechanism is one possible Stage 3 candidate, subject to a separate novelty kill against mature incentive theory.

### 3.5 Absorptive capacity

Endogenous capability is not a blank theoretical frontier. Kamien & Zang (2000), Kaiser (2002), Leahy & Neary (2007), and Bae (2016) all formalize absorptive capacity strategically. Bae explicitly derives underinvestment in one R&D margin and overinvestment in another relative to the social optimum and studies subsidy/tax interventions. Leahy & Neary analyze extra-industry knowledge policy.

**Implication:** simply replacing the legacy exogenous `sigma` with endogenous absorptive capacity would not establish novelty.

### 3.6 Regional spillovers / public economics

Cremer et al. directly model two communities investing in a local public good with interjurisdictional spillovers, compare Nash investment with a social optimum, and study corrective grants. Wellisch demonstrates that equilibrium responses such as mobility can overturn the simple spillover-underprovision intuition.

**Implication:** `two strategic regions + spillover + Nash < planner` is not a contribution. The model would require an intermediation-specific strategic margin.

### 3.7 Living labs / co-creation / orchestration

Fauth et al. already frame living labs as regional innovation-ecosystem orchestrators. Gizińska & Lankauskienė (2026) explicitly frame living labs as adaptive intermediary structures embedded in peripheral RIS. Gibert & Plantec (2026) add legitimacy costs and governance tensions.

**Implication:** the institutional label and current-policy relevance remain useful motivation, but institutional novelty is weak.

## 5. Backward / forward / author-neighborhood findings

The audit did not treat a keyword match as the end of the search.

### Hoppe–Ozdenoren neighborhood

The paper itself identifies earlier technology-transfer/intermediation theory and extends to competition among intermediaries and inventor involvement. Related formal TTO work includes Macho-Stadler et al. (2007), later endogenous matching/search models, and endogenous choice between direct and intermediary transfer in Carayol & Sterzi (2021).

### Howells neighborhood

Howells (2006) is followed by extensive literature on intermediary functions. Later work includes Howells & Thomas (2022) on innovation-search processes, Feser (2023) as a systematic review, and public/digital intermediary studies. This confirms that `intermediary = matching device` is too narrow a representation of the field.

### Bathelt neighborhood

Later work tests rather than merely repeats complementarity. Sotarauta et al. distinguish local/digital and global/national patterns; Aarstad et al. identify size-dependent substitution/complementarity; Wang et al. find nonlinear external-linkage effects.

### Absorptive-capacity neighborhood

Formal antecedents span Kamien & Zang, Kaiser, Leahy & Neary, and Bae. The latter papers explicitly add public-policy and social-optimum comparisons.

### Japanese policy neighborhood

Kodama (TAMA), Nishimura & Okamuro (Industrial Cluster Project), and Fukugawa (Kohsetsushi) demonstrate that intermediation/networking, absorptive capacity, institutional form, and heterogeneous outcomes are already empirically studied in Japan.

## 6. Contribution-by-contribution classification

### Claim A — `Public co-creation hubs correct local coordination failure.`

**Decision: KILL AS MAIN CONTRIBUTION.**

- Closest prior art: Hoppe & Ozdenoren; Calcagnini et al.; Howells; Villani et al.
- Primitive difference in legacy model: `m1>m0` directly assumes hub improves matching.
- Strategic difference: none strong enough; legacy has no participant search equilibrium.
- Theorem difference: fixed-cost threshold rather than a new coordination theorem.
- Welfare difference: planner arithmetic, not decentralized equilibrium.
- Without application label: `a costly device raises matching and is adopted if benefit exceeds cost.`

Classification: `STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE / KILL`.

### Claim B — `Thin regions particularly need public hubs.`

**Decision: KILL.**

- Closest prior art: Tödtling & Trippl; Oughton et al.; Feser; Gizińska & Lankauskienė.
- Legacy model problem: holding technology fixed, absolute hub value rises with `N_A` because potential local pairs increase. Thus the model does not itself derive a stronger need for a thinner region.
- Without application label: mostly a scale/fixed-cost effect.

Classification: `EXISTING CONCEPTUAL PRIOR ART + LEGACY MODEL DOES NOT ESTABLISH THE STRONG CLAIM`.

### Claim C — `Local intermediation and external pipelines are complementary.`

**Decision: KILL.**

- Closest prior art: Bathelt et al.; Aarstad et al.; Wang et al.
- Legacy primitive: `q=q0+eta*b*h` makes pipeline effectiveness conditional on hub by definition.
- Legacy theorem: `R_AB>0` adds to hub welfare; there is no endogenous cross-partial generated by behavior.
- Anti-confirmation: empirical work documents substitution and nonlinearities.

Classification: `STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE / ASSUMPTION-DRIVEN / KILL`.

### Claim D — `Decentralized actors underinvest in cross-regional connectivity.`

**Decision: KILL AS GENERIC RESULT.**

- Closest prior art: Cremer et al.; Wellisch.
- Legacy model: no decentralized regional equilibrium exists.
- A future regional Nash-versus-planner model would remain cosmetic unless intermediation creates a distinct choice or spillover structure.

Classification: `EXACTLY THE KIND OF GENERIC RESULT COVERED BY PRIOR PUBLIC-ECONOMICS MODELS`.

### Claim E — `Local interaction creates dynamic redundancy / lock-in.`

**Decision: KILL AS LEGACY THEORETICAL CONTRIBUTION.**

- Closest conceptual prior art: Boschma and proximity/lock-in work.
- Legacy mechanism: `m(1-delta*m)` inserts the loss directly.
- No network state, identity of repeated partners, knowledge stock, or endogenous novelty exists.

Classification: `COMPONENT PRIOR ART + ASSUMPTION-DRIVEN / KILL`.

### Claim F — `Publicly supported intermediaries improve welfare.`

**Decision: KILL.**

- Public status is not itself an efficiency rationale.
- Fukugawa documents adverse institutional effects; Russo et al. document performance-measure distortions; public/private substitutes exist.
- The legacy model contains no private intermediary or decentralized provision benchmark.

Classification: `UNESTABLISHED AND CONTRADICTED AS UNIVERSAL CLAIM / KILL`.

### Claim G — `Optimal policy partitions into hub-only / pipeline-only / bundle / neither depending on parameters.`

**Decision: KILL IN LEGACY FORM.**

The legacy model cannot derive all four regimes because pipeline-only is technologically excluded and, conditional on a hub, a positive pipeline always adds surplus under the maintained restrictions. Its principal thresholds are fixed-cost/scale thresholds.

A four-regime policy partition could become a later result only if a new model endogenously allows both technologies, their costs and interactions, and generates nontrivial ordering. The existence of four labels is not itself a contribution.

Classification: `NOT DELIVERED BY LEGACY MODEL / POTENTIAL FUTURE RESULT ONLY IF NEW INTERACTION EXISTS`.

## 8. Surviving claim / research space

No theorem from the legacy manuscript survives as a main contribution.

The strongest residual object is classified **`POTENTIALLY NOVEL`**, not `novel`:

> **A regional innovation intermediary may supply multiple economically distinct services—local search/matching and extra-regional access—and decentralized institutional/jurisdictional choices may distort not only the level but the composition of those services. The unresolved question is whether an intermediation-specific interaction can make that composition inefficient in a way not reducible to standard matching externalities or generic interjurisdictional spillovers.**

Why this survives Stage 2:

- Hoppe–Ozdenoren and Calcagnini formalize intermediary matching/search but not strategic regional provision of a local/external service portfolio;
- Cremer et al. formalize strategic regional provision and planner comparison but not innovation intermediation or matching technology;
- Bathelt et al. provide the local/external knowledge distinction but not formal decentralized welfare analysis;
- public intermediary studies document multiple functions and governance distortions but generally do not provide this exact regional equilibrium structure.

Why this is **not yet a contribution**:

- all ingredients separately have strong prior art;
- the workflow's combination-novelty rule requires a new strategic interaction/theorem;
- Stage 3 may fail to find one, in which case the theory route should terminate.

## 10. Conditional blocker and resolution

Before the final verdict, Stage 2 was provisionally classified **`CONDITIONAL GO`** with exactly one blocker:

> Does existing formal literature already combine innovation-intermediary matching/search with strategic regional/local-government provision, cross-jurisdiction spillovers, and planner comparison in a single model?

One targeted search round was performed using the intersection queries preserved in `search_queries.md`.

The search located more nearby work—including public/private intermediary distinctions and intermediary competition—but **did not identify an exact joint model** among the strongest accessible records.

This search failure is not treated as proof of novelty. The blocker is considered resolved because the closest formal models could be compared structurally:

- intermediary models lack the strategic-regional service-portfolio problem;
- regional public-good models lack intermediation/search structure;
- local/external knowledge models lack the equilibrium/welfare architecture.

Therefore the final Stage 2 verdict is upgraded from `CONDITIONAL GO` to **`GO`**, with the exceptionally restrictive Stage 3 contract below.

## 12. Stage 3 contract

### Frozen surviving RQ

> **Can decentralized regional/institutional provision of innovation intermediation generate a genuinely intermediation-specific distortion in the composition of local matching and extra-regional knowledge access, and if so, what primitive interaction produces it?**

### Killed mechanisms/claims that may not be revived unchanged

- exogenous private myopia;
- quadratic local redundancy as the headline mechanism;
- technological `b*h` complementarity;
- passive outside region presented as strategic;
- generic interjurisdictional spillover underprovision;
- generic endogenous absorptive-capacity underinvestment;
- generic `intermediary reduces search cost`;
- generic public-good/fixed-cost threshold;
- physical hub as an essential primitive.

### Mechanism families Stage 3 may compare

Stage 3 should generate approximately 8–12 candidates and select a small TOP set. At minimum it should consider, without presuming any survives:

1. **Intermediary-service composition externality:** local matching effort changes the return to external search or vice versa through an endogenous match-quality/selection mechanism, not an assumed cross-partial.
2. **Public-intermediary multitask incentive interaction:** measurable local activity and hard-to-measure external/capability services interact with regional spillovers. Must survive a direct comparison to standard multitask principal-agent theory and Russo et al.
3. **Public/private intermediary entry and crowd-out:** thin versus thick markets change private intermediary entry, while public provision changes search intensity/quality. Must create a theorem beyond standard public/private provision.
4. **Strategic regional funding of a shared intermediary:** jurisdictions finance/use an intermediary that chooses matching/search allocation. Must create an intermediation-specific wedge beyond Cremer et al.
5. **Endogenous participation/selection:** intermediary composition affects which local actors search externally, producing selection or congestion not present in Hoppe/Calcagnini.
6. **Governance/legitimacy trade-off:** broader brokerage reach reduces some friction while changing credibility/participation; only pursue if economically microfoundable and not merely a management label.

### Variables / players that must not yet be accumulated

Stage 3 must obey the workflow's mechanism-search discipline. It may sketch minimal models but must not simultaneously add:

- multiple regions;
- private and public intermediaries;
- dynamics;
- bargaining;
- endogenous networks;
- political economy;
- heterogeneous firms;
- digital platforms;
- endogenous absorptive capacity

into one candidate simply to create novelty.

Each candidate must identify the **smallest model** that generates its feedback loop.

### Required Stage 3 kill standard

A candidate fails if, after removing the words `hub`, `regional`, and `co-creation`, it reduces to:

- Hoppe–Ozdenoren style intermediary screening/matching;
- Calcagnini-style bilateral search externality;
- Cremer-style local-public-good spillover;
- standard multitask moral hazard;
- standard crowd-out;
- standard endogenous absorptive capacity;
- a fixed-cost threshold;
- an assumed complementarity/substitutability cross-partial.

Only a candidate producing a new strategic feedback, threshold ordering, sign reversal, organizational wedge, or welfare result should reach Stage 4.
