## 1. Executive verdict

**Canonical verdict: GO.**

Routing: **GO TO MINIMAL MODEL.**

This is a narrow, falsifiable GO. Stage 3 does **not** certify novelty and does not authorize a full paper model. It authorizes one Stage 4 minimal-model kill test for one mechanism only:

> **Funder-Induced Neutrality Loss / Regional Home-Bias Feedback.** A geographically bounded public sponsor has an incentive to favor local matches; anticipated home bias lowers outside actors' expected matching payoff and therefore outside participation; the thinner outside pool reduces the productivity of extra-regional brokerage, reinforcing the realized local orientation of the intermediary.

The mechanism survives Stage 3 because the proposed wedge can potentially operate through an endogenous participation/market-thickness response rather than through the legacy paper's assumed `hub + pipeline` complementarity, exogenous private myopia, or a generic additive spillover.

However, de Cornière & Taylor (2019), Zennyo (2022), canonical two-sided-market theory, and current platform-bias research are serious prior-art threats. Stage 4 must kill the project if the minimal model is mathematically isomorphic to those models.

## 3. Surviving research question

> **Can decentralized regional or institutional provision of innovation intermediation generate an intermediation-specific distortion in the composition of local matching and extra-regional knowledge access, and if so, what primitive strategic interaction generates that distortion?**

Stage 3 operationalized this as a search for a **composition distortion generated by behavior**, not a simple level distortion or an objective-function coefficient.

## 4. Search logic

Twelve distinct mechanisms were generated from the user-specified seeds and then stripped of institutional labels. Each was tested for:

1. an explicit feedback loop;
2. minimum endogenous margins;
3. minimum new primitive;
4. closest-paper survival;
5. a non-monotone/threshold/reversal or composition theorem;
6. welfare content;
7. Stage 4 tractability;
8. fatal referee attack.

Candidate ranking used the precommitted 100-point score: novelty 25, closest-paper survival 20, mechanism clarity 15, tractability 15, welfare 10, institutional relevance 10, empirical bridge 5. Fatal prior art overrode scores.

Targeted mini-searches were run before keeping candidates involving common agency, two-sided participation, disclosure, network formation, and bias/neutrality. Results are recorded in `MINI_SEARCH_LEDGER.md` and appended to `LITERATURE_LEDGER.csv`.

## 5. Twelve-candidate summary

The full table is in `CANDIDATE_MECHANISM_TABLE.md`.

### Killed in first/deep pass

**M1 — Local-funder additive service externality.** Killed because the clean version collapses to a standard geographically bounded public-good/spillover wedge of the Cremer type.

**M2 — Shared intermediary / strategic regional funding.** Killed after targeted search. Siqueira & Sandler (2004) already analyze many principals funding one agent producing multiple public goods under hidden effort, free riding and competing interests. The proposed task-allocation distortion is a common-agency problem unless an independent innovation-specific mechanism is added, which would violate minimality.

**M4 — Public KPI / multitask intermediation.** Killed. Differential observability across intermediary tasks is standard multitask agency, and Russo et al. (2019) already supplies direct public-innovation-intermediary evidence on performance-measure distortion.

**M5 — Public/private specialization and crowd-out.** Killed. The minimal version is generic entry/crowd-out; making it novel would require additional appropriation/selection mechanisms and feature accumulation.

**M6 — Direct versus intermediated search.** Killed by Carayol & Sterzi and broader TTO/search prior art.

**M9 — Endogenous overembeddedness/network state.** Killed. König and especially Dasaratha (2023) show that endogenous openness/network formation and innovation externalities are already formal research objects; the mechanism is also too complex for a clean Stage 4.

**M11 — Asymmetric regions/intermediary location.** Killed. Asymmetry/location alone is a feature, and the natural game collapses toward standard fiscal/location competition.

### Secondary survivors

**M7 — Scarce boundary-spanning capacity.** Rival capacity can create a service-composition trade-off, but a resource constraint alone is mechanical and the participation feedback is standard platform logic. Retained only as secondary.

**M12 — Service-specific capability formation.** Institutionally plausible but too close to the mature endogenous absorptive-capacity literature and requires dynamics. Retained only as secondary.

## 7. Killed mechanisms and why they fail

The main lesson of the kill pass is that the easiest ways of obtaining a service-composition wedge are already established economics:

- several funders + one multitask agent → common agency;
- measurability differences → multitask moral hazard;
- two participant sides → two-sided platform cross-side externalities;
- disclosure → established disclosure/matching economics;
- direct/intermediated choice → formal TTO theory;
- network state/openness → strategic network-formation theory;
- capability formation → endogenous absorptive-capacity theory;
- regional spillover → fiscal federalism/local public goods.

Therefore the project needs a mechanism tied to a specific productive property of intermediation rather than simply a new sectoral label.

## 9. Targeted mini-literature findings

### Common agency kills shared-funding novelty

Siqueira & Sandler (2004) are especially damaging: many principals condition contributions to one provider producing several collective goods, and task allocation is distorted by hidden effort, free riding and competing interests. M2 is therefore not a credible standalone novelty claim.

### Platform theory kills generic participant-feedback novelty

Rysman (2009) makes cross-side participation externalities canonical. Current 2026 work by Pinarello, Trabucchi & Frattini explicitly describes physical open-innovation intermediaries as two-sided platforms. A co-creation hub cannot gain novelty merely by being called a platform.

### Disclosure economics demotes M8

Kawakami (2024) supplies a formal matching model with professional disclosure services and welfare/regulatory comparisons. Boudreau & Lakhani establish innovation-specific disclosure/reuse trade-offs.

### Strategic innovation networks kill M9

Dasaratha (2023) endogenizes secrecy/openness and learning-network interaction rates, including innovation/welfare consequences and informational intermediary policy implications.

### Bias literature creates the decisive threat to M10

de Cornière & Taylor (2019) formally analyze biased intermediation and welfare. Zennyo (2022) is even more dangerous for the proposed feedback because platform search bias changes consumer participation and thereby seller participation through indirect network effects. Reimers & Waldfogel provide a current welfare framework for platform bias.

Therefore **bias is not the contribution**. M10 is retained only to test whether *geographically bounded public sponsorship interacting with intermediary neutrality* generates a distinct strategic/welfare result.

## 10. Preferred minimal mechanism

### Funder-Induced Neutrality Loss / Regional Home-Bias Feedback

Interpretation:

Publicly or regionally sponsored innovation intermediaries often need outside firms, experts, researchers, solvers, or investors to regard the intermediary as sufficiently neutral to participate. A local sponsor, however, may favor local incumbents/projects or locally captured benefits. If potential outsiders rationally anticipate lower matching probability or lower expected surplus, they may not enter the intermediary's pool. The loss of outside participation then changes the *technology of intermediation itself*: there are fewer valuable outside matches available, so realized brokerage becomes even more local.

The conceptual distinction from a generic spillover is that the sponsor's policy changes an endogenous participant pool before matching occurs.

## 11. Why M10 is not *yet* merely combination novelty — and the remaining burden

M10 is not kept because it combines `regional government + platform + innovation intermediary`. That would fail Stage 2/3.

The only potentially distinct object is:

> a geographically bounded sponsor can damage the intermediary's neutrality/credibility, which changes third-party participation and therefore the productivity and composition of matching services.

This could produce a wedge even in the sponsor's own payoff if excessive local favoritism destroys valuable outside participation. Such a local-welfare reversal would be stronger than a standard planner simply attaching more weight to outsiders.

But this is only a hypothesis. Zennyo already has bias-driven cross-side participation. If the Stage 4 equations are a relabeling of that structure, M10 fails.

## 12. Candidate propositions

Stage 4 may test, not assume:

1. **Home-bias composition wedge:** conditions for `alpha^R > alpha^SP` and lower outsider participation under regional sponsorship.
2. **Participation threshold/tipping:** a parameter region where stronger home bias substantially thins or collapses the outside side of the matching market.
3. **Local-welfare reversal:** beyond some range, greater local priority lowers even regional/local welfare because outside participation becomes too weak.
4. **Neutrality commitment:** only if 1–3 survive, a commitment to a less biased matching rule may improve welfare and possibly the sponsor's own payoff.

The most valuable result is proposition 3. If only proposition 1 survives because the sponsor assigns lower weight to outsiders, the project should be killed as generic public economics.
