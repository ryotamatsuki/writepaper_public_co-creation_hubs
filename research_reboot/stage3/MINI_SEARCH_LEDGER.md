# Stage 3 — Targeted Mini-Search Ledger

Date: 2026-09-01

Purpose: targeted novelty-kill searches for candidate mechanisms that introduced common agency, two-sided participation, disclosure, network formation, or intermediary bias/neutrality. These searches supplement the Stage 2 ledger; they do not convert search failure into novelty evidence.

## A. Shared intermediary / common agency

### Siqueira & Sandler (2004)
**Collective Goods, Common Agency, and Third-Party Intervention**, *Bulletin of Economic Research*, 56(1), 1–20. DOI: `10.1111/j.1467-8586.2004.00185.x`.

Multiple contributors/principals condition payments to one provider/agent supplying multiple public goods. Hidden effort generates agency costs, free riding, and competing interests across tasks. This is a direct structural threat to M2: several regions funding one intermediary whose effort is split across services is not a new mechanism by itself.

**Candidate effect:** M2 `KILL`.

### Galperti (2015)
**Common agency with informed principals: Menus and signals**, *Journal of Economic Theory*, 157, 648–667. DOI: `10.1016/j.jet.2015.01.018`.

Confirms the maturity of strategic multi-principal/common-agent contracting. Used as a structural benchmark rather than innovation-specific prior art.

---

## B. Endogenous participation / two-sided platform feedback

### Rysman (2009)
**The Economics of Two-Sided Markets**, *Journal of Economic Perspectives*, 23(3), 125–143. DOI: `10.1257/jep.23.3.125`.

The canonical two-sided-market architecture already has an intermediary whose value depends on participation on two sides and cross-side externalities. Therefore `service mix → participant entry → return to service mix` is not novel without an additional primitive that changes the strategic theorem.

**Candidate effect:** M3 retained only as `SECONDARY / TOP 3`; M7 demoted to secondary.

### Pinarello, Trabucchi & Frattini (2026)
**From brokerage to orchestration: Physical open innovation intermediaries as platforms for system-level knowledge creation**, *Technology in Society*, 87, 103339. DOI: `10.1016/j.techsoc.2026.103339`.

This 2026 paper explicitly interprets physical open-innovation intermediaries as orchestrative two-sided platforms and links them to absorptive-capacity processes. The project therefore cannot claim that treating a physical co-creation/innovation intermediary as a platform is itself new.

**Candidate effect:** raises the current-frontier bar for M3, M7 and the institutional interpretation of M10.

---

## C. Disclosure-mediated search / matching

### Kawakami (2024)
**Disclosure services and welfare gains in matching markets for indivisible assets**, *Review of Economic Design*, 28, 485–532. DOI: `10.1007/s10058-024-00352-2`.

Competitive matching with professional disclosure services, endogenous paid disclosure, monopoly disclosure and minimum-disclosure alternatives. The paper directly combines intermediary information services, matching and welfare.

**Candidate effect:** M8 cannot be the preferred mechanism merely by adding disclosure to matching; retained only as `SECONDARY / TOP 3`.

### Boudreau & Lakhani (2015)
**“Open” disclosure of innovations, incentives and follow-on reuse: Theory on processes of cumulative innovation and a field experiment in computational biology**, *Research Policy*, 44(1), 4–19. DOI: `10.1016/j.respol.2014.08.001`.

Disclosure timing/open access already generates an innovation-specific incentive-versus-follow-on-use trade-off.

**Candidate effect:** further reduces M8 novelty.

---

## D. Network-state / endogenous openness

### Dasaratha (2023)
**Innovation and Strategic Network Formation**, *Review of Economic Studies*, 90(1), 229–260. DOI: `10.1093/restud/rdac021`.

Firms strategically choose secrecy versus openness; those choices determine interaction rates in an innovation network, with positive learning and negative competition externalities. The paper also discusses informational intermediaries as a policy device.

**Candidate effect:** M9 `KILL`; an endogenous-network/overembeddedness reboot would be both structurally close and too complex for a minimal Stage 4.

---

## E. Bias / neutrality / participation

### de Cornière & Taylor (2019)
**A model of biased intermediation**, *RAND Journal of Economics*, 50(4), 854–882. DOI: `10.1111/1756-2171.12298`.

Formal biased-intermediary model. Welfare consequences depend on congruence/conflict between sellers and consumers; bias is already an endogenous policy object with welfare implications.

**Candidate effect:** strongest structural prior-art threat to M10.

### Zennyo (2022)
**Platform Encroachment and Own-Content Bias**, *Journal of Industrial Economics*, 70(3), 684–710. DOI: `10.1111/joie.12301`.

A platform selects fair or biased search. Bias changes commissions/prices and consumer participation, which then changes seller participation through indirect network externalities.

**Candidate effect:** particularly dangerous because it already contains a `bias → participation → other-side participation` loop. M10 survives Stage 3 only because its proposed source of bias is a geographically bounded public sponsor and its hypothesized distortion is the composition of innovation intermediation; Stage 4 must determine whether that distinction changes the theorem or is merely relabeling.

### Reimers & Waldfogel (2023; revised 2026)
**A Framework for Detection, Measurement, and Welfare Analysis of Platform Bias**, NBER Working Paper 31766. DOI: `10.3386/w31766`.

Provides a current equilibrium/welfare framework for platform ranking bias. The working paper was revised in April 2026.

**Candidate effect:** confirms that `bias + welfare` is a mature object. M10 cannot rely on bias itself as novelty.

### Chiambaretto, Massé & Mirc (2019)
**“All for One and One for All?” — Knowledge broker roles in managing tensions of internal coopetition: The Ubisoft case**, *Research Policy*, 48(3), 584–600. DOI: `10.1016/j.respol.2018.10.009`.

Shows institutionally that a knowledge broker can protect competitive advantage, lower sharing costs, and build trust under coopetition. Used only to support the relevance of neutrality/trust as an intermediary function, not as formal novelty evidence.

---

# Mini-search conclusion

The searches kill the easiest versions of shared funding, multitask incentives, disclosure, network dynamics and generic participant feedback.

The only mechanism retained as preferred for one minimal-model test is:

> **Funder-induced neutrality loss / regional home-bias feedback:** a geographically bounded public sponsor has an incentive to favor local matches; anticipated home bias lowers outside actors' expected payoff and participation; the thinner outside side then lowers the productivity of external brokerage, reinforcing the intermediary's realized local orientation.

This is **not** declared novel. The decisive Stage 4 question is whether the public-regional sponsorship/neutrality mechanism creates a proposition that is not mathematically equivalent to standard biased-intermediation or two-sided-platform models.
