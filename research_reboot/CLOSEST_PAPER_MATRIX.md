## Model-level synthesis

### What is already occupied

The following components are not independently novel:

- an innovation intermediary that improves matching/search under uncertainty;
- intermediary-specific matching externalities and possible over/under-search;
- information-asymmetry and reputation rationales for technology-transfer intermediaries;
- endogenous choice between direct and intermediated transfer;
- local-government investment with interjurisdictional spillovers and Nash-versus-planner comparison;
- organizational thinness/peripheral RIS as a policy problem;
- local knowledge interaction plus external pipelines;
- absorptive capacity as an endogenous strategic variable;
- public intermediary performance-measure distortions;
- living labs/co-creation platforms as regional innovation intermediaries/orchestrators.

### Strongest potentially surviving distinction

No inspected formal model jointly makes **regional/jurisdictional actors choose an innovation-intermediation service portfolio whose distinct local-search and extra-regional-access margins create an intermediation-specific strategic interaction**, while also allowing decentralized provision to be too low, too high, or compositionally wrong relative to a planner.

This is classified only as **`POTENTIALLY NOVEL` research space**, not as a contribution. Three reasons prevent a stronger claim:

1. Hoppe–Ozdenoren and Calcagnini et al. already establish formal intermediary matching and inefficiency.
2. Cremer et al. already establish strategic jurisdictions and planner comparison.
3. Bathelt and later work already establish the local/external knowledge distinction.

A Stage 3 model must therefore generate a theorem that exists **because these margins interact**, not because three known components are placed in one notation. If the interaction collapses to `standard spillover + standard matching`, the project should be killed.

## Reading-depth limitation

For closest papers with accessible full HTML/PDF or institutional copies, model/results sections were inspected. Several restricted papers were available only through detailed publisher abstracts, RePEc metadata, or institutional summaries. Such papers are not used to support a claim of novelty; they are used conservatively as prior-art threats.
