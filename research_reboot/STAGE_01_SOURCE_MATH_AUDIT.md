## 1. Executive audit verdict

**Canonical verdict: GO.**

The legacy model is largely algebraically correct in its core closed forms, but its main economic interpretation does not survive a destruction test unchanged. The central problems are not hidden algebraic mistakes. They are **assumption-induced results and missing decentralized choice**:

- pipeline policy is effective only when a hub exists by construction;
- pipeline marginal benefit is strictly positive at zero by construction, so a positive pipeline is always chosen conditional on a hub;
- the pipeline surplus is additively separable and does not actually repair the modeled local redundancy process;
- dynamic redundancy is a reduced-form quadratic penalty rather than an endogenous state generated by repeated network interaction;
- the `private vs social` wedge is produced by defining a private evaluator that ignores period 2 and outside-link value, rather than solving a decentralized equilibrium;
- Region B is passive;
- ecosystem-size and outside-pool comparative statics are driven directly by combinatorial pair counts and linear surplus assumptions;
- the absorptive-capacity extension changes surplus weights but does not model endogenous capability formation.

No correction-grade mathematical error was found in the core formulas tested. The inherited branch therefore remains useful as a **source object**, but its headline theoretical contribution should not be carried into Stage 2 as established economics.

A residual question survives: whether innovation intermediation creates an intermediation-specific decentralized wedge beyond the standard local-public-good spillover problem. Stage 2 should novelty-kill that question without changing the model.

## 2. Canonical legacy model

### Players and decision makers

The baseline contains one effective decision maker: a benevolent local planner in target region A.

- Region A contains `N_A >= 2` participants.
- Region B contains `N_B >= 1` outside participants.
- Region B has no objective, strategy, budget, participation decision, or government choice.

The planner chooses:

1. `h ∈ {0,1}`, whether to establish a public hub;
2. if `h=1`, `b >= 0`, pipeline intensity in period 2.

The model therefore is a two-location accounting environment, not a strategic two-government equilibrium.

### Pair spaces

\[
P_A=\frac{N_A(N_A-1)}{2},
\qquad
Q_{AB}=N_AN_B.
\]

`P_A` is the potential local pair count; `Q_AB` is the potential cross-regional link count.

### Local matching technology

\[
m_A(h)=m_A^0+(m_A^1-m_A^0)h,
\qquad 0\le m_A^0<m_A^1\le1.
\]

The hub necessarily raises the local matching rate.

### Period-1 local surplus

\[
B_1(h)=v_LP_Am_A(h).
\]

### Period-2 local surplus

\[
B_{2L}(h)=
\beta v_LP_Am_A(h)[1-\delta m_A(h)].
\]

with regularity condition

\[
1-\delta m_A^1>0.
\]

### Pipeline technology

\[
q(b)=q_0+\eta b h,
\qquad q_0\ge0,\ \eta>0.
\]

This specification implies that `b` changes connectivity only when `h=1`.

Cross-regional surplus is

\[
B_{2X}(h,b)=\beta v_XQ_{AB}q(b).
\]

### Costs

Hub cost:

\[
Fh,\qquad F>0.
\]

Pipeline cost:

\[
\frac{\kappa}{2}b^2h,
\qquad \kappa>0.
\]

### Welfare

\[
W(h,b)=B_1(h)+B_{2L}(h)+B_{2X}(h,b)-Fh-\frac{\kappa}{2}b^2h.
\]

### Equilibrium concept

There is no decentralized equilibrium in the baseline. The operative concept is planner maximization plus, in Section 5, comparison with a separately defined short-run `private evaluation rule`.

### 3.1 Hub-alone welfare difference

Starting from primitives,

\[
\Delta_H=W(1,0)-W(0,0)
\]

simplifies exactly to

\[
\Delta_H
=v_LP_A(m_A^1-m_A^0)
[(1+\beta)-\beta\delta(m_A^1+m_A^0)]-F.
\]

Define

\[
\Lambda_A=(1+\beta)-\beta\delta(m_A^1+m_A^0).
\]

Then

\[
\Delta_H=v_LP_A(m_A^1-m_A^0)\Lambda_A-F.
\]

**Classification: CORRECT.**

Economic interpretation is considered separately below.

### 3.2 Hub fixed-cost / ecosystem-size threshold

If `Lambda_A > 0`, the condition `Delta_H >= 0` is equivalent to

\[
P_A\ge
\frac{F}{v_L(m_A^1-m_A^0)\Lambda_A}.
\]

Solving `N_A(N_A-1)/2 = RHS` gives the stated positive root threshold.

**Classification: CORRECT, but the existence of a threshold is mechanically generated by a fixed cost against a pair-count benefit.**

### 3.3 Comparative statics of hub value

The reported derivatives with respect to `N_A`, `F`, `delta`, `m_A^1`, and `m_A^0` are reproduced.

Under `Lambda_A > 0`, `N_A>=2`, `m1>m0`, the `N_A` derivative is positive. Under `1-delta*m1>0` and `0<beta<=1`, the reported signs for `m1` and `m0` are also valid.

**Classification: CORRECT.**

### 3.4 Short-run/private decomposition

The model defines

\[
\Pi_H=v_LP_A(m_A^1-m_A^0)-F
\]

and

\[
D_A=\beta v_LP_A(m_A^1-m_A^0)
[1-\delta(m_A^1+m_A^0)].
\]

Then

\[
\Delta_H=\Pi_H+D_A
\]

is an exact identity.

**Classification: CORRECT identity; economic interpretation is ad hoc.**

### 3.5 Optimal pipeline policy

Conditional on `h=1`, incremental pipeline welfare is

\[
G(b)=\beta\eta v_XQ_{AB}b-\frac{\kappa}{2}b^2.
\]

Therefore

\[
b^*=\frac{\beta\eta v_XQ_{AB}}{\kappa},
\]

with SOC `-kappa<0`, and maximized renewal surplus

\[
R_{AB}=\frac{(\beta\eta v_XQ_{AB})^2}{2\kappa}.
\]

**Classification: CORRECT.**

### 3.6 Bundle threshold

Because the pipeline term is additively separable,

\[
\Delta^*_{H+b}=\Delta_H+R_{AB}.
\]

Thus

\[
F^*_{H+b}=F^*_H+R_{AB}
\]

and, under the positive-parameter restrictions,

\[
F^*_{H+b}-F^*_H=R_{AB}>0.
\]

**Classification: CORRECT algebraically; economically highly mechanical.**

## 4. SOC / feasibility / participation audit

### Hub choice

Hub choice is discrete and requires no SOC. Its welfare comparison is well-defined given the model.

### Pipeline choice

The pipeline objective is strictly concave because

\[
\frac{\partial^2G}{\partial b^2}=-\kappa<0.
\]

Because

\[
\left.\frac{\partial G}{\partial b}\right|_{b=0}
=\beta\eta v_XQ_{AB}>0,
\]

the solution is strictly positive whenever a hub exists. There is no parameter region with a hub and optimally zero pipeline, except degenerate excluded primitives.

### Connection intensity

The manuscript interprets `q(b)` as an intensity, not probability, so no upper bound is imposed. This removes a mathematical feasibility issue but also makes the linear benefit globally available regardless of how large `b` becomes. Convex cost ensures a finite optimum.

### Participation constraints

There are no participant-level payoffs or participation/individual-rationality constraints in the baseline. Local and external actors do not decide whether to join a hub, collaborate, reveal information, or supply knowledge.

### Region B

There is no Region-B participation or budget constraint. `N_B` is a scale parameter.

## 5. Economic microfoundation audit

### Test 1 — Pipeline complementarity by assumption

**Finding: CONFIRMED.**

The hub–pipeline relationship is not generated by optimization over two independently available technologies. The product `b*h` in

\[
q(b)=q_0+\eta bh
\]

makes the marginal productivity of `b` exactly zero without a hub. Simultaneously, pipeline cost is also multiplied by `h`.

Therefore a pipeline-only regime is not merely suboptimal; it is absent from the feasible economic choice set.

No baseline primitive explains why a visiting expert programme, interregional brokerage, digital connector, joint regional programme, or other external-link policy technologically requires the physical/local hub.

**Verdict:** the claimed complementarity is **imposed**, not discovered.

### Test 2 — Dynamic redundancy

**Finding: CONFIRMED as reduced form; not microfounded.**

The term

\[
m[1-\delta m]
\]

contains the entire non-monotone dynamic mechanism. The model has no identities of counterparties, network state, repeated-pair probability, knowledge stock, or endogenous novelty.

A higher matching rate therefore increases the redundancy penalty because the functional form says it does. `delta` controls the sign of the dynamic correction.

This is a legitimate reduced form for illustration but is insufficient to claim that the model derives regional lock-in from first principles.

### Test 3 — Public-intervention wedge

**Finding: CONFIRMED.**

The legacy paper explicitly states that it does not build a full decentralized equilibrium. It defines a private/local criterion using period-1 visible activity only:

\[
\Pi_H=v_LP_A(m_1-m_0)-F.
\]

It then compares this criterion with full social welfare.

This comparison can show disagreement between two objective functions. It does **not** establish why rational private firms, a private hub operator, regional government, or coalition would ignore period-2 returns or outside-link benefits.

Hence the public-intervention rationale is not an equilibrium welfare theorem.

### Test 4 — Passive Region B

**Finding: CONFIRMED.**

Region B has no strategic variable and no welfare trade-off. It is an outside pool of `N_B` potential links. Consequently the phrase `two-region model` is geographically descriptive but does not yet imply interregional strategic interaction.

### Test 5 — Mechanical thresholds

**Finding: CONFIRMED in the relevant sense.**

There is no algebraic defect, but several thresholds/comparative statics arise directly from primitives:

- hub value increases with `N_A` because `P_A=N_A(N_A-1)/2` multiplies a fixed per-pair net surplus;
- pipeline value increases with `N_A N_B` because the benefit is linear in `Q_AB` before optimization;
- a fixed-cost threshold exists because a positive gross-benefit term is compared with `F`;
- optimal pipeline intensity is positive because its linear marginal benefit at zero is assumed positive.

A theorem based only on these features has limited mechanism content.

### Test 6 — Welfare consistency

**Finding: largely PASS.**

Within the reduced-form planner model:

- the same participant populations are used across regimes;
- the same surplus units are used;
- baseline cross-regional access `q0` is present in both no-hub and hub-alone welfare and correctly cancels in `Delta_H`;
- discounting uses the same `beta`;
- hub and pipeline resource costs are subtracted.

No cross-regime accounting inconsistency was found in the audited core formulas.

The more fundamental issue is not accounting consistency but missing individual objectives and decentralized welfare accounting.

## 6. Welfare audit

The legacy model is a planner model of gross social surplus, not a model with separately derived consumer/producer surplus. This is internally coherent as a reduced-form welfare exercise.

However, three limitations are material:

1. **No ownership/appropriability:** it is not specified which benefits are privately appropriated and which are external.
2. **No fiscal incidence:** public funding is a resource cost `F`, without taxation/deadweight cost or intergovernmental finance.
3. **No decentralized benchmark:** without private/regional payoff functions, the welfare optimum cannot be compared to an equilibrium allocation.

Therefore statements such as `public intervention is justified` exceed what the baseline welfare exercise proves.

## 9. Legacy contribution decomposition and publishability audit

### 1. Hub-alone efficiency threshold

- Mathematically true: yes.
- Endogenous economic mechanism: weak.
- Assumption-driven: substantially.
- Stand-alone publishable contribution: no.
- Stage 2 target: only as background / prior-art comparator.

### 2. Short-run vs long-run divergence

- Mathematically true: yes.
- Endogenous: no; criterion difference is specified.
- Potentially useful economic intuition: yes.
- Stage 2 target: performance-measure/principal-agent and dynamic-policy evaluation literature.

### 3. Dynamic redundancy

- Mathematically true conditional on functional form.
- Endogenous: no.
- Potential research direction: only if a later mechanism derives repetition/lock-in from a state/network process.
- Stage 2 target: proximity/lock-in/network literature.

### 4. Pipeline optimum

- Mathematically true.
- Endogenous choice of intensity: yes, conditional on the technology.
- Endogenous choice of whether pipeline exists: effectively no under positive primitives.
- Stage 2 target: external knowledge search/linkage intensity literature.

### 5. Hub + pipeline rescue region

- Mathematically true.
- Genuine complementarity: no. The benefit of `b` is independent of `m(h)` once `h=1`; only availability is tied to `h`.
- Stage 2 target: buzz/pipelines complementarity/substitution literature.

### 6. Ecosystem thickness

- Mathematically true.
- Economic novelty: low.
- Important conceptual warning: the baseline actually makes a hub **more** attractive as the local ecosystem becomes thicker, holding matching technology fixed. This is not itself a theory that `thin regions need hubs`.

### 7. Cross-regional connectivity

- Mathematically true.
- Economic novelty: low in current reduced form.

### 8. Absorptive capacity

- Mathematically true.
- Endogenous capability: absent.
- Stage 2 target: intermediary/absorptive-capacity literature.

### 9. Public intervention

- Not proven as a decentralized welfare wedge.
- Stage 2 target: fiscal federalism/local public goods and public-intermediary incentive literature.

## 10. Surviving economic questions

The following questions survive Stage 1, but none is yet a contribution claim:

1. Does an innovation intermediary create a **distinct strategic margin** beyond generic local-public-good spillovers or ordinary bilateral search?
2. Can local intermediation alter the productivity of later extra-regional search in a way that is endogenous rather than imposed by `b*h`?
3. Can regional actors choose too little, too much, or the wrong mix of local and external intermediation in a decentralized equilibrium?
4. Is the empirically relevant distortion better understood as interjurisdictional spillover, capability formation, performance-measure distortion, private-intermediary entry/crowding out, or another mechanism?
5. Under what circumstances is public support actually required rather than private/mixed intermediation?
