## 1. Journal of Public Economic Theory — Wiley / Association for Public Economic Theory

- Scope: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/page/journal/14679779/homepage/productinformation.html
- Current author guidelines: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/page/journal/14679779/homepage/forauthors.html
- Journal home/current content: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/journal/14679779
- Current submission system: Wiley Research Exchange / Wiley Authors (`submission.wiley.com/journal/JPET`).
- Scope summary: theoretical public economics/public finance; local public goods, externalities, public/social decision making, market failure and regulation; explicitly welcomes game theory, simulation, first-order and comparative-static methods and imposes no upper/lower technique-complexity boundary.
- Fees: current author page explicitly says **no submission fees**; journal is subscription/hybrid and OA is optional after acceptance, so standard publication has **no mandatory APC**.
- Optional OA: Wiley OnlineOpen APC applies only if selected. The current journal-specific official page does not expose a fixed currency amount in the crawled text; this amount is non-binding for the user's zero-cost traditional route and must be rechecked in Wiley's APC calculator if OA is later considered.
- Format: Free Format; LaTeX + review PDF accepted; current page calls for Data Availability Statement and other standard declarations. Current page requests a structured abstract; Stage 13 should follow the live portal without forcing substantive rewriting.
- Preprints: accepted.
- AI: Wiley policy embedded in current author guidance: AI/LLM cannot be an author; generative use beyond copyediting must be transparently disclosed; human authors remain accountable.
- Recent fit evidence:
  - Boadway & Cuff (2026), “Global Warming Policy in a Federation,” https://doi.org/10.1111/jpet.70111 — formal federal/regional public-policy theory.
  - Faria & Silva (2026), “Dynamics of Fiscal Habits and Soft Budgets in Federations,” https://doi.org/10.1111/jpet.70110 — dynamic public game with benchmark equilibria.
  - Figueroa & Skreta (2026), “Efficient Mechanisms for Multi-Asset Bilateral Trading,” https://doi.org/10.1111/jpet.70109 — pure theory built around a sharp complements/substitutes qualitative distinction.
  - Lee (2026), “Knowledge Spillover Network and Optimal R&D Policy in Oligopoly,” https://doi.org/10.1111/jpet.70128 — network-based strategic investment/public policy theory.
  - Zimmermann (2024), “Public infrastructure provision in the presence of terms-of-trade effects and tax competition,” https://doi.org/10.1111/jpet.12689 — strategic public investment/tax-competition theory.
- Uncertainty: no official journal-specific optional-OA amount was visible in the checked author page. This does **not** affect the hard gate because OA is optional.

## 2. Journal of Regional Science — Wiley

- Scope: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/page/journal/14679787/homepage/productinformation.html
- Author guide: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/page/journal/14679787/homepage/forauthors.html
- OA/APC: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/page/journal/14679787/homepage/fundedaccess.html
- Current journal: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/journal/14679787
- Scope summary: original analytical work at the intersection of economics and quantitative geography; geographic dimensions of urban/regional economies and policy must matter.
- Fees: **no submission fee**. Subscription journal with optional OA. Standard copyright/subscription route is zero-APC. Optional OA APC currently **USD 3,770 / GBP 2,550 / EUR 3,130**, exclusive of tax.
- Data: data sharing mandated; Data Availability Statement required.
- Review: double blind.
- Recent fit signal: Wang, Zeng & Zhu (2025), “The Core-Periphery Model Under Additively Separable Preferences” — rigorous spatial pure theory; current 2026 content remains strongly geographic/urban.
- Fit implication: pure theory is real, but the current paper lacks an explicit metric/location-choice geography primitive. This is why JRS is Stretch, not Primary.

## 3. Journal of Economics — Springer Nature

- Current journal/scope: https://link.springer.com/journal/712
- Submission guidelines: https://link.springer.com/journal/712/submission-guidelines
- Publishing options/fees: https://link.springer.com/journal/712/how-to-publish-with-us
- Submission system: Editorial Manager from the journal page.
- Scope summary: all fields of economics; micro/macro papers often have theoretical/mathematical focus.
- Fees: hybrid. Official page states subscription publication has **no APC charges**; optional OA APC currently **GBP 2,490 / USD 3,390 / EUR 2,790**. No journal-specific submission fee is listed in the current submission flow/guidelines; Springer Nature states subscription journals have no charge in the majority of cases and journal-specific charges are listed on individual pages. No such charge is listed for Journal of Economics. Hard-gate status: PASS, with final portal check required immediately before any future submission.
- Format: abstract 150–250 words; JEL codes; LaTeX source and PDF accepted; declarations required.
- AI: LLMs cannot be authors; generative LLM use must be documented in Methods or another suitable section; AI-assisted copyediting alone need not be declared.
- Recent fit evidence: Angelini, Castellani & Zirulia (2025), “Platform investment and seller competition in two-sided markets,” https://doi.org/10.1007/s00712-024-00874-x. This is especially relevant because it is formal two-sided-platform investment/pricing theory and explicitly uses numerical simulations where IFT comparative statics are not clear-cut.
- Current 2026 issue also contains theoretical/mathematical work on technology licensing, product-market competition/fiscal policy/growth, emission taxes/standards and dynamic price competition.
- Uncertainty: because the platform-investment literature is unusually close here, novelty scrutiny is stronger than at JPET.

## 4. Review of Regional Studies — Southern Regional Science Association

- Author guide: https://rrs.scholasticahq.com/for-authors
- Journal/about/current articles: https://rrs.scholasticahq.com/
- Scope: theoretical, empirical and methodological regional science; spatial context/place/region must be central.
- Fees: fully OA and explicitly **no subscription, submission, or publication fees**.
- Review: double blind; Scholastica.
- Format: free format first submission; accepted papers LaTeX; no general word limit.
- Required cover letter: 1–3 sentence spatial-element statement.
- Required disclosure: Conflict of Interest and **AI Usage Disclosure**. Data/code statement optional.
- Recent fit evidence:
  - Batabyal & Beladi (2025), “On Unitization as a Way of Addressing Water Pollution in the Ganges in Kanpur, India,” https://rrs.scholasticahq.com/article/153843-on-unitization-as-a-way-of-addressing-water-pollution-in-the-ganges-in-kanpur-india — concise Cournot regional-policy theory.
  - Batabyal (2026), “Unitization of Tanneries and Water Pollution in the Ganges in Kanpur, India: The Salience of Fixed Costs,” current 2026 RRS issue — concise Nash/Cournot pure theory.
  - Batabyal & Yoo (2023), “Using Taxes to Attract the Creative Class in the Presence of a Region-Specific Rent,” https://doi.org/10.52324/001c.87679 — explicit two-region strategic policy model.
- Uncertainty: submission must state truthfully why the two-region/local-welfare structure is economically spatial; no artificial geographic primitive may be invented.
