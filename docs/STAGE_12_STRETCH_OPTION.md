# Stage 12 Stretch Option — Journal of Regional Science

## Stretch candidate

**Journal of Regional Science (JRS).**

## Why it is not absurd

JRS explicitly publishes original analytical work at the intersection of economics and quantitative geography and has a history of rigorous theoretical regional science. The current paper has two genuine regional governments with distinct local-welfare objectives, interregional strategic interaction, and a shared metropolitan outside option. This is not a purely cosmetic regional label.

A recent pure-theory signal is Wang, Zeng & Zhu (2025), *The Core-Periphery Model Under Additively Separable Preferences*, showing that JRS still publishes standalone formal theory.

## Why it is not Primary

The frozen model contains no transport cost, distance, location choice, spatial equilibrium, agglomeration mechanism, or geographic network. JRS's natural referee tradition is therefore likely to ask whether the regional labels matter enough to justify the journal, and its recent pure theory tends to have stronger analytical/spatial characterization than a local computational existence certificate.

Most likely rejection reason: **insufficient spatial/geographic structure and theorem breadth**, not lack of economic coherence.

Desk simulation: **BORDERLINE**.

## Is trying it first rational?

**No, not under the current objective.** The expected information value of a JRS rejection is low: a desk rejection may simply say that the model is not spatial enough, which would not tell us whether the public-economic mechanism is publishable. A referee rejection may demand exactly the Class T spatial extension that Stage 12 forbids. The time cost is therefore not justified relative to JPET.

Trying JRS first would be rational only if the author explicitly valued an ambitious regional-science shot enough to accept this predictable mismatch risk. It is not the recommended sequence.

## Journal-appropriate positioning paragraph

> We study strategic investment by two regional governments that provide public innovation intermediation while sharing a priced metropolitan commercial alternative. The result identifies a locally robust mechanism through which a common metropolitan outside option changes interregional policy interaction, while the institutional application remains deliberately reduced-form.
