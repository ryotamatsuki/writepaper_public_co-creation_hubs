# Stage 12 Fallback Strategy

## Ordered path

**Recommended:** Journal of Public Economic Theory → Journal of Economics → Review of Regional Studies.

JRS is an optional Stretch, not part of the recommended first-to-last sequence.

## Trigger logic

### Primary rejection: JPET

- **Desk reject for contribution breadth / proof ceiling:** submit next to **Journal of Economics**. Do not add a theorem. Tighten the presentation around the sequential game, private repricing and qualitative strategic reversal.
- **Referee reject for López–Vives/component novelty:** still move to **Journal of Economics**, but preserve the explicit concession and sharpen only the distinction between a known follower-price term and the public-public nested sign reversal.
- **Referee reject for institutional mapping:** Journal of Economics is preferable to moving immediately to a regional outlet because it can evaluate the generic platform/public-private game without demanding causal hub evidence.

### Fallback rejection: Journal of Economics

- **Reject because platform investment/pricing is too crowded:** move to **Review of Regional Studies** and foreground the two-region decentralized public-policy object and shared metropolitan outside option.
- **Reject because local numerical existence is too narrow:** move to RRS without adding analytical/global results; RRS has recent concise pure theory and a lower theorem breadth requirement.

### Safety net: Review of Regional Studies

Submit essentially the same frozen model. The cover letter must truthfully state the spatial element: two regional governments maximize distinct local welfare and are strategically linked through a shared metropolitan alternative. Do not invent distance/location primitives.

## Reserve alternate

**REGION** passes the zero-fee/OA gate and regional-science scope and can replace RRS if Stage 14 discovers a new RRS policy issue. It is not ahead of RRS because the checked recent record provides a weaker direct signal for concise standalone theory.

## Journal-specific positioning paragraphs

**Journal of Economics:**

> We analyze a sequential three-player game in which two welfare-maximizing public investors interact through a two-sided participation environment and a shared profit-maximizing intermediary that reprices after public investment. A matched fixed-price benchmark yields public-investment complementarity, whereas restoring optimal private repricing can make the same public investments strategic substitutes. The paper isolates this qualitative strategic reversal and establishes it by a regular computational certificate plus local persistence.

**Review of Regional Studies:**

> We study two regional governments whose local innovation-intermediation investments are strategically linked by a shared metropolitan commercial alternative. In a matched-price diagnostic the regional investments reinforce one another, but endogenous repricing by the shared alternative can reverse their interaction from complements to substitutes. The result provides a concise theory of interregional policy interaction with a secondary local coordination-welfare diagnostic and a documented innovation-hub analogue class.

## Model-change rule

No rejection on this ladder authorizes Class T changes. New theorems, spatial primitives, empirics, robustness models, welfare optimization or policy design require reopening earlier stages and are outside the submission ladder.
