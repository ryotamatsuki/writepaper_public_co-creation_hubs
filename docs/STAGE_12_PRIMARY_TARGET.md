# Stage 12 Primary Target — Journal of Public Economic Theory

## Decision

**Primary journal: Journal of Public Economic Theory (JPET), Wiley / Association for Public Economic Theory.**

The paper belongs there because its label-stripped object is a public-economics game: two decentralized public investors choose costly facilitation, cross-side participation transmits their actions, and a shared commercial alternative observes those public choices and optimally reprices. The headline contribution is not “innovation hubs” or generic platform theory; it is the qualitative reversal in the **public-public** strategic relation when the third actor's price policy is restored.

JPET's current scope explicitly covers local public goods, externalities, public/social decision making, market failure and regulation, and welcomes game theory, simulation, first-order and comparative-static methods. This is unusually well aligned with the paper's exact mechanism and proof architecture.

## Why stronger journals are not preferred

- **Journal of Regional Science** has a real send-out case but normally places greater weight on explicit geographic structure and analytical spatial characterization. The frozen model has regional objectives but no metric geography; adding one would be a prohibited Class T change.
- **IJIO / RIO** are not preferred because their natural platform/IO referee pool makes López–Vives-type follower-price overlap and crowded component novelty maximally salient. The same narrow combination novelty is more naturally evaluated as a public-strategic result at JPET.

## Why weaker journals are not first choice

- **Journal of Economics** clearly publishes pure platform theory and numerical simulation, but its general/IO-oriented referee pool offers less protection against component-overlap attacks and less direct value for the decentralized-public-investment object.
- **Review of Regional Studies** is a credible zero-cost safety outlet with recent pure theory, but JPET offers a substantially better audience for the generic mechanism without requiring stronger claims.

## Likely editor interpretation

**Subjective desk outcome: LIKELY SEND OUT.** A plausible editor reads the paper as a focused public-economic theory contribution about how an endogenous private response changes strategic interaction among decentralized public investors. The paper is concise, transparent about its numerical/local proof ceiling, and does not ask the editor to accept known components as novel.

Main desk risk: the editor may judge that a computationally certified local existence result is too narrow once the component mechanisms are conceded. This is a real risk, not a formatting problem.

## Likely referee attacks

1. **Combination novelty:** known network complementarity plus known follower-price response may be viewed as insufficiently new.
2. **Proof ceiling:** no global parameter characterization; headline existence is computationally certified and locally persistent.
3. **Artificial B3:** a referee may ask whether fixing the private price at the full-game equilibrium value manufactures the result.
4. **López & Vives:** follower-price-response logic is known; the paper must keep the distinction at the public-public nested sign reversal.
5. **Institutional abstraction:** focal-project single-homing and commercial profit maximization are reduced-form.
6. **Incomplete planner problem:** welfare is only a secondary local coordination diagnostic.

These attacks are manageable at JPET if Stage 13 preserves the Stage-11 scope controls.

## Stage-13 presentation direction

Journal-appropriate positioning paragraph:

> We study a decentralized public-investment game in which two regional governments are linked through cross-side participation and share a commercial alternative that sets its price after observing public investment. Holding the private price at the full-game equilibrium value provides a matched diagnostic in which the public investments are strategic complements. Restoring the private operator's optimal repricing adds a negative strategic channel that can dominate the network effect, yielding strategic substitutes at a regular interior configuration. The result is a computationally certified local existence result with local persistence, not a global sign theorem. The contribution is the qualitative change in the public-public strategic relation, not the component network or follower-price mechanisms themselves.

Stage 13 should: lead with the generic public-economics problem; move the nested B3/G identification logic earlier; concede López–Vives/component overlap early; keep welfare secondary; compress institutional catalogue and computational robustness detail without removing their grounding/certificate.

## What must not change
