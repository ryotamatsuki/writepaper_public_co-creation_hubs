# Stage 10 v2.1 — Literature Description Check

Date: 2026-09-10 JST

Purpose: verify that reader-facing descriptions in `sections/07_related_literature.tex` do not attribute a stronger or different mechanism to cited papers than their published/official abstracts and model descriptions support. This is an exposition check; it does not reopen the Stage-6 novelty decision.

## Verified items

### López and Vives (2019)

`Overlapping Ownership, R&D Spillovers, and Antitrust Policy`, *Journal of Political Economy* 127(5), 2394–2437, DOI `10.1086/701811`.

Publisher text confirms that the benchmark studies cost-reducing R&D with spillovers in Cournot oligopoly and overlapping ownership. The paper also contains a two-stage strategic-commitment extension in which firms choose R&D first and then compete in the product market. It is therefore appropriate evidence for the generic proposition that downstream equilibrium responses can enter first-stage investment incentives. It is **not** a direct private-follower-pricing model.

Stage-10 action: corrected `sections/07_related_literature.tex` to remove the earlier over-specific follower-pricing characterization.

### Liu, Rivadeneyra, and Reshidi (2026)

`Public vs. Private Payment Platforms: Market Impacts and Optimal Policy`, Bank of Canada Staff Working Paper 2026-10, DOI `10.34989/swp-2026-10`.

Official Bank of Canada materials describe competition between a welfare-maximizing public platform and a profit-maximizing private platform in a two-sided payment market and report that the private platform may change its fees in response to public-platform competition.

Stage-10 use: precedent for public/private platform competition and endogenous private fee response, not for the paper's two-public-investor matched-price sign reversal.

### Bontems, Hamilton, and Lepore (2025)

`Sequential Pricing on Multisided Platforms`, *Economics Letters* 255, 112543, DOI `10.1016/j.econlet.2025.112543`.

Publisher materials confirm sequential price setting by platforms and qualitative effects on pricing and cross-platform network effects.

Stage-10 use: precedent for sequential platform pricing and timing effects, not for public investment.

### Sánchez-Cartas (2026)

`The Stackelberg–Armstrong Model`, *Economics Letters* 265, 113019, DOI `10.1016/j.econlet.2026.113019`.

Publisher materials confirm that follower best responses in sequential two-sided platform pricing can overturn the simultaneous benchmark's price-skewness ranking.

Stage-10 use: close qualitative precedent for follower-response-induced reversal in a platform-pricing object; it does not contain the present public-public investment object.

### Zogheib and Bourreau (2021)

`Public vs. Private Investments in Network Industries`, EconomiX Working Paper 2021-4 / HAL `hal-04159754`.

The abstract confirms profit-maximizing private and welfare-oriented public firms, including local public firms, with endogenous prices and infrastructure investment.

Stage-10 use: mixed public/private objectives and investment competition are treated as known ingredients.

### Mun and Nakagawa (2010); Mun (2019)

`Pricing and Investment of Cross-Border Transport Infrastructure`, *Regional Science and Urban Economics* 40(4), 228–240, DOI `10.1016/j.regsciurbeco.2010.03.008`; and `Joint Provision of Transportation Infrastructure`, *Economics of Transportation* 19, 100118, DOI `10.1016/j.ecotra.2019.06.001`.

Publisher abstracts confirm strategic regional/government pricing and investment in shared or cross-border infrastructure settings.

Stage-10 use: decentralized regional strategic objectives and infrastructure investment are not claimed as novel.

### Farhi and Hagiu (2008), Hahn and Kim (2026), He et al. (2026)

The available working-paper/publisher records support the limited uses made in the manuscript: two-sided platform strategic interaction; platform investment under price-parity clauses and cross-platform investment externalities; and pricing/service investment under mixed network externalities.

## Result

**PASS — literature descriptions are aligned with verified source content after one Stage-10 wording repair.**

No literature check in Stage 10 expands the Stage-8 novelty envelope. The surviving contribution remains the matched-price public-public local strategic sign reversal when the shared private price policy is activated.
