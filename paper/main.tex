\title{Strategic Interaction among Public Innovation Hubs under Private Repricing}

\begin{abstract}
We study two regional governments that invest in public innovation intermediation while sharing a price-setting metropolitan private alternative. Cross-side participation creates a positive link between public investments when the private access price is held fixed: stronger facilitation in one region weakens the shared outside option and raises the other region's marginal return to investment. When the private intermediary optimally reprices, however, a demand loss induces a price cut that restores the private route's attractiveness. At a regular interior configuration, this additional channel dominates the fixed-price network effect, so public investments switch from strategic complements to strategic substitutes. The result is a numerically certified existence statement with local open-set persistence, not a global sign theorem. Aggregate welfare accounting separates the strategic reversal from welfare: private access fees are transfers, while decentralized public investment can generate a real cross-regional coordination wedge. Innovation-hub institutions in several countries provide plausible analogues for the model's primitives; the private price response remains a testable prediction.
\end{abstract}
