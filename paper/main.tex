\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Introduction.} We study two regional governments that invest in public innovation intermediation while sharing a price-setting metropolitan private alternative. Cross-side participation can make public investments strategic complements when the private access price is held fixed, whereas endogenous private repricing adds an opposing strategic channel. \textbf{Methods.} We compare a fixed-price benchmark with the full sequential game, derive the public cross effects analytically around the zero-network benchmark, and use exact rational interval methods to certify the canonical interior equilibrium pair. \textbf{Results.} On regular interior equilibrium branches, the fixed-price public cross effect is zero at $\beta=0$ but has a strictly positive first derivative in $\beta$, while the full-game cross effect is already strictly negative at $\beta=0$. Hence there exists $\varepsilon>0$ such that, for sufficiently small positive network interaction, the fixed-price best-response slope is positive while the full-game slope is negative. At the canonical parameter vector, an exact rational Krawczyk certificate and interval implicit differentiation verify the matched but distinct fixed-price and full-game equilibria, strict second-order conditions, and opposite best-response slopes. \textbf{Conclusion.} Endogenous commercial repricing can change the sign of decentralized public strategic interaction. The result is local rather than a global primitive-space classification, and welfare accounting is kept separate from the strategic reversal.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} public investment; two-sided platforms; strategic interaction; local public economics; endogenous pricing; innovation intermediation; network effects.\\
