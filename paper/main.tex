\begin{abstract}
We study two regional governments that invest in public innovation intermediation while sharing a price-setting metropolitan private alternative. Cross-side participation can make the public investments strategic complements when the private access price is held fixed, whereas endogenous private repricing adds an opposing strategic channel. Our main result is analytic: on regular interior equilibrium branches around the zero-network benchmark, the fixed-price public cross effect is zero at $\beta=0$ but has a strictly positive first derivative in $\beta$, while the full-game cross effect is already strictly negative at $\beta=0$. Hence there exists $\varepsilon>0$ such that sufficiently small positive network interaction yields $\BR_i^{B3\prime}>0>\BR_i^{G\prime}$. At the canonical interior parameter vector, an exact rational Krawczyk certificate and interval implicit differentiation jointly verify the matched but distinct B3 and G equilibria, strict second-order conditions, and opposite best-response slopes. The canonical vector is a constructive benchmark, not an empirical calibration. We do not provide a global sign theorem or a primitive necessary-and-sufficient classification. Welfare accounting further separates the strategic reversal from the cross-regional coordination externality.
\end{abstract}
