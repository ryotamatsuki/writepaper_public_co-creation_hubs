# Spray (2021) Deep Dive

Target: John A. Spray, *Search Externalities in Firm-to-Firm Trade*, IMF Working Paper 2021/091, DOI 10.5089/9781513572611.001.

## Relevance
Spray explicitly separates **domestic** and **international** firm-to-firm search and matching. The impact of lower trade costs depends on the relative size and sign of search externalities in those two markets. Search externalities may be positive through information sharing or negative through congestion.

## Mapping to the project
- domestic search market -> local analogue;
- international search market -> external analogue;
- trade/access costs -> P-adjacent access friction;
- market-specific search externality -> thickness/congestion component.

## What Spray already kills
- novelty from merely having local and external matching markets;
- novelty from letting congestion/thickness differ across the two markets;
- novelty from external-market access costs affecting sourcing and welfare;
- novelty from firm-to-firm search as the application domain.

## What Spray does not by itself contain
The paper does not appear to pose the exact planner problem `raise matching productivity in domestic market vs open international market` as two discrete public technologies. It therefore remained `STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE`, not the decisive exact kill.

## Why the branch nevertheless dies
The gap left by Spray is covered at a more general level by matching-platform design. Immorlica et al. jointly choose pair-specific meeting rates for welfare, while Teh/Mekonnen endogenize accessibility/segmentation. Combining these references is not a new synthesis for the project; rather, it shows the candidate controls are already elements of general search design.

**Classification: STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE.**
