# Export Promotion / Trade-Fair Deep Dive

## Core 2026 evidence
Gábor Békés, Mátyás Molnar & Claudia Steinwender, *What Happens in Paris, Does Not Stay in Paris: Trade Fairs and Search and Matching Frictions*, CEPR DP 21574 / CESifo WP 12714 (2026).

The study treats international trade fairs as a commonly subsidized intervention intended to overcome search-and-matching frictions that impede international business links. Using Hungarian participation in the 1900 Paris World Exhibition, it finds persistent increases in export participation and related outcomes. Effects are stronger when there are more potential international buyers and fewer domestic rivals, which the authors interpret as matching gains combined with congestion/rivalry effects.

## Relevance to P
This provides unusually direct institutional evidence for the proposed P interpretation:

`publicly supported external exposure -> access to international counterpart pool -> new buyer-seller links`.

It also shows that the gains from opening access are state-dependent: they depend on the abundance of counterparts and competition among same-side participants.

## What it kills
The project cannot claim as a theoretical novelty that:
- public support can lower external partner-search frictions;
- external-market access can be subsidized;
- more external counterpart abundance raises matching gains;
- congestion/rivalry can attenuate those gains.

## What it does not kill by itself
The 2026 study is primarily causal/empirical rather than the exact formal planner model sought in Stage 0R-S. It therefore supports the economic realism of P but is not the decisive formal prior-art kill.
