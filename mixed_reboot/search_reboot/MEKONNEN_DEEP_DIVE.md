# Mekonnen (2026) Deep Dive

Target: Teddy Mekonnen, *How to segment a search market: Information design and directed search*, Theoretical Economics (2026), DOI 10.3982/TE5350.

## Model structure
- two-sided buyer-seller frictional search;
- sellers are allocated across submarkets through a market segmentation;
- buyers direct their search by choosing submarkets;
- bilateral meeting probabilities depend on submarket tightness;
- tighter markets create thick-market gains for sellers and congestion for buyers;
- trade surplus is divided after meetings.

## Planner problems
The paper solves both:
1. a first-best problem in which the planner chooses segmentation and buyer allocation/tightness; and
2. a constrained-efficient segmentation problem in which segmentation is chosen anticipating buyers' endogenous submarket choices.

Thus segmentation is not a passive label. It changes beliefs, submarket participation, tightness, meeting volumes and welfare.

## Relevance to P
The candidate `P` is an external-market access/segmentation intervention. Mekonnen establishes that segmentation and accessibility of search submarkets are welfare-relevant design variables under directed search. It also already contains the proposed internal mechanism:

`segmentation -> buyer reallocation -> submarket tightness -> congestion/thick-market effects -> welfare`.

## Missing element relative to the candidate
Mekonnen does not separately introduce a public `H` instrument that directly raises local matching productivity while `P` changes segmentation.

## Classification
**STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE.**

It is not the sole fatal prior art, but after Immorlica et al. show that a designer can choose pair-specific meeting rates, the remaining distinction between local productivity and external access is no longer a protected theoretical margin.
