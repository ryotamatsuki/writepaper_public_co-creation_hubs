# Gehrig Intermediary Boundary

Target: Thomas Gehrig (1993), *Intermediation in Search Markets*, Journal of Economics & Management Strategy 2(1):97-120, DOI 10.1111/j.1430-9134.1993.00097.x.

## What is already established
Gehrig models heterogeneous agents in a market where exchange requires costly partner search. Agents can use an intermediary or engage in private search and bargaining. The intermediary reduces trading/search frictions; equilibrium clientele depends on intermediary terms versus delay/search costs.

## Binding implication
The project cannot claim novelty from:
- a hub/intermediary reducing partner-search costs;
- a centralized matchmaker substituting for decentralized search;
- heterogeneous agents selecting into intermediation;
- welfare or participation consequences of costly search intermediation.

## What Gehrig does not contain
It does not provide the project's proposed local-vs-external submarket architecture or a welfare-maximizing designer choosing pair-specific access and meeting intensities.

## Boundary conclusion
Gehrig kills the **generic intermediary** contribution. The later platform-design literature then kills the attempted residual distinction. Therefore the project cannot fall back from Stage 0R-S to a simpler claim that `public hubs reduce search costs`.

**Classification: COMPONENT OVERLAP / BINDING NEGATIVE KNOWLEDGE.**
