# Label-Stripping Test

## Candidate before stripping
A prefectural public co-creation policy chooses among:
- `0`: neither local hub nor external pipeline;
- `H`: improve local partner matching;
- `P`: open access to extra-regional partners;
- `HP`: do both.

## Remove application labels
Delete:
- Ehime;
- region;
- public;
- innovation;
- co-creation hub;
- local/external terminology.

The problem becomes:

> A designer of a two-sided search market decides whether to increase meeting opportunities for one subset of cross-side type pairs and whether to enable meeting opportunities for another subset, anticipating equilibrium acceptance/search responses and social welfare.

## Comparison with Immorlica et al.
Immorlica et al. allow the designer to choose the meeting rate for **every** cross-side pair of types. The candidate's two policies are therefore coarse restrictions:
- H = an intensive change in selected already-positive rates;
- P = an extensive zero-to-positive change in selected rates.

The candidate's `0/H/P/HP` set is a four-element subset of the general meeting-rate design space.

## Comparison with visibility/segmentation models
Banerjee et al. and Teh-Wang-Watanabe make the access interpretation even more direct: platform discovery/meeting design controls which market participants are visible/accessible to one another. Mekonnen makes the endogenous allocation response to segmentation explicit.

## Worker-vacancy relabeling
If seeker/provider are renamed worker/vacancy, the public-employment-service literature provides public and private search channels and public matching-effectiveness changes. The regional innovation labels are not necessary for the basic economics.

## Result
The H/P distinction survives as a useful **policy taxonomy** but not as a distinct theoretical control space.

**LABEL-STRIPPING VERDICT: FAIL — GENERAL MATCHING-PLATFORM DESIGN ABSORBS THE STRUCTURE.**
