# Public Intermediation Test

## Question
Does the public nature of the proposed H/P architecture create a distinct theoretical object after the search-design margins are stripped of regional labels?

## Result
**No at the current social-architecture stage.**

The proposed government actor is a welfare-oriented designer choosing intermediation technology. Modern matching-platform theory already studies designers optimizing matching/meeting structures for social welfare. Replacing `platform` with `prefecture` does not change the strategic problem.

## Public-employment-agency boundary
The labor-search literature also prevents claiming that public matching is an unusual institution:
- Pissarides (1979): state employment agency plus random search;
- Fougère-Pradel-Roger (2009): public job-contact arrival plus endogenous private search effort;
- Plesca (2010): public labor exchange as a directed-search channel alongside all other search;
- Jahn-Wagner (2008): public employment service effectiveness and random search;
- Holzner-Watanabe: public agency and decentralized search channels;
- Merkl-Sauerbier (2024): public and private search channels, agency matching efficiency and activation.

These papers show that a public intermediary can change meeting efficiency and interact with alternative search channels inside standard search theory.

## Could a substantive public wedge exist?
Possibly, but it would require a new primitive such as:
- private appropriability versus social matching surplus;
- fiscal/public-good financing;
- public information advantage;
- political or mandate constraints;
- inability of private platforms to internalize regional externalities;
- distributional objectives.

None of these is authorized in Stage 0R-S. Adding one now would be a rescue pivot, not a consequence of the current technology.

## Conclusion
For the present research question, `public` is an institutional application label rather than a theorem-essential primitive.

**Result: public status cannot rescue Search from prior-art absorption.**
