## Executive verdict

**NO-GO — SEARCH ABSORBED.**

The Stage 0R-T residue was deliberately narrow: `H` raises matching productivity inside an incumbent local two-sided search market, whereas `P` opens or lowers the access/segmentation barrier to a distinct extra-regional matching market. The exact-prior-art round searched beyond regional innovation terminology into matching-platform design, labor intermediation, market segmentation, trade search, assortment/visibility design and public employment agencies.

The decisive result is that the stripped H/P problem is already contained in a more general formal design problem. Immorlica, Lucier, Manshadi & Wei, *Designing Approximately Optimal Search on Matching Platforms* (Management Science 69(8), 2023; DOI 10.1287/mnsc.2022.4601), study a decentralized two-sided matching market with finitely many types on both sides. The platform chooses the meeting rate for every cross-side type pair and optimizes those meeting rates to maximize equilibrium social welfare. A zero meeting rate removes a type pair from effective access; a positive rate makes the pair accessible; increasing an already-positive rate intensifies matching opportunities. Therefore, after representing local and extra-regional actors as type subsets, the proposed `P` extensive access margin and `H` intensive productivity margin are both restrictions of the platform's pair-specific meeting-rate design space.

This is not merely a semantic resemblance. The model already contains strategic acceptance, congestion/cannibalization, endogenous stationary market composition and a welfare-maximizing search-design problem. The candidate regional `0/H/P/HP` menu would therefore be a low-dimensional constrained version of a more general directed-search design problem rather than a new economic technology.

Two additional papers close the remaining gaps. Teh, Wang & Watanabe (2024, JET, DOI 10.1016/j.jet.2024.105798) show a one-to-one relationship between meeting technology and market accessibility in an intermediated directed-search market and characterize efficient accessibility. Mekonnen (2026, Theoretical Economics, DOI 10.3982/TE5350) lets a planner design market segmentation and submarket tightness/allocation, explicitly balancing thick-market benefits and congestion and then solving a constrained-efficient segmentation problem under endogenous buyer search. These results make `market access / segmentation` itself an established endogenous search-design margin.

The public-intermediation label also does not rescue the theory. The public employment service literature, starting from Pissarides (1979) and continuing through Plesca (2010), Fougère-Pradel-Roger (2009), Jahn-Wagner (2008), Holzner-Watanabe, and Merkl-Sauerbier (2024), already models public matching channels alongside alternative search channels and changes in public matching effectiveness. At the social-architecture stage, calling the platform a prefectural government rather than a welfare-maximizing platform does not create a new strategic margin.

Accordingly, **no Technology Minimal Model — Search is authorized.**

## Closest prior art

### 1. Immorlica–Lucier–Manshadi–Wei — decisive general-model absorption
- two-sided dynamic matching market;
- multiple types on both sides;
- platform sets pair-specific meeting rates;
- feasible meeting-rate matrix determines which type pairs can meet and how intensively;
- agents strategically accept/reject matches;
- congestion and cannibalization matter;
- platform solves a social-welfare search-design problem.

Mapping:
- local actors = type subset `L`;
- external actors = type subset `E`;
- `H` = raise selected meeting rates within `L`;
- `P` = switch selected L–E meeting rates from zero to positive / increase external rates;
- `0/H/P/HP` = four coarse restrictions on the admissible meeting-rate matrix.

Classification: **EXACT PRIOR ART — GENERAL-MODEL SUPERSET** for the stripped search-design object.

### 2. Teh–Wang–Watanabe (2024)
The platform chooses meeting technology/accessibility in directed search, while a social planner chooses efficient market accessibility. It establishes that more accessibility need not be efficient and that meeting technology and accessibility can be equivalent choice representations.

Classification: **STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE**.

### 3. Mekonnen (2026)
A planner chooses segmentation and buyer allocation/tightness; in the constrained problem buyers choose submarkets endogenously. Thick-market and congestion externalities determine welfare and allocation.

Classification: **STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE**.

### 4. Public-employment-agency search models
They demonstrate that a public intermediary's matching efficiency can coexist with another search channel and induce crowd-out/reallocation effects. Merkl-Sauerbier explicitly uses private and public channels; Plesca treats the public labor exchange as a directed-search channel alongside all other search; Fougère et al. combine an exogenous public-contact arrival rate with endogenous private search intensity.

Classification: collectively **STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE / COMPONENT OVERLAP**.

## Spray comparison
Spray (2021) formally separates domestic and international firm-to-firm search and allows their search externalities to differ. Falling trade costs change sourcing decisions and welfare. It does not give a planner the exact H-vs-P discrete intervention pair, so it is not the decisive kill by itself. It nevertheless removes novelty from `local vs external search market`, `different congestion/thickness across markets`, and `access costs affect external search`.

## Mekonnen comparison
Mekonnen is closer than Stage 0R-T initially recognized. The planner controls segmentation and, in the first-best benchmark, buyer allocation across submarkets; in the constrained-efficient problem, segmentation changes buyers' endogenous submarket choices. Thus segmentation affects who can effectively meet whom and where tightness is allocated. The candidate P mechanism is therefore an established search-market-design margin.

## Export-promotion comparison
Trade-fair/export-promotion work provides institutional and empirical evidence that public programs can lower foreign partner-search frictions and that gains depend on buyer abundance and rivalry/congestion. This is not the decisive formal kill, but it prevents claiming public facilitation of extra-regional matches as a new mechanism.

## H productivity margin
**Already known.** Search/matching models routinely contain meeting/matching productivity or contact-arrival parameters. Public-employment-service reforms explicitly vary public matching effectiveness; platform-design models directly choose pairwise meeting rates.

## P access margin
**Already known.** Visibility/segmentation/accessibility is controlled in Banerjee et al. (2017), Teh et al. (2024), Mekonnen (2026), and related platform-design work. Setting meeting rates to zero or positive in Immorlica et al. also implements exclusion/access at the type-pair level.

## Joint H/P public choice
No paper located uses the literal binary labels `H` and `P` or a prefectural government's exact four-element menu. That is not the relevant novelty test. The joint control problem is nested in Immorlica et al.'s more general meeting-rate-matrix optimization. A model that restricts those controls to `local intensity` and `external access` would be a special case.

## Endogenous allocation
Yes. Endogenous responses link the two margins in the closest literature. Immorlica et al. obtain strategic stationary equilibrium under designed meeting rates. Mekonnen's segmentation induces endogenous buyer allocation across submarkets, and congestion/tightness respond. Therefore `P -> search reallocation -> tightness -> marginal value of H` is not, by itself, a new interaction.

## Local/external distinction
It is economically useful for the Ehime application but **not theorem-essential** to the existing design theory. Local/external can be represented as two subsets of types or submarkets in general matching-platform models. The regional interpretation is an application restriction, not a new endogenous margin.

## Public role
At this stage the public actor is largely a welfare-maximizing designer label. A genuine public/private wedge would require a new primitive—financing, political objectives, mandate, information, externality ownership, or another institutional friction—which is explicitly outside this stage and cannot be added to rescue the search branch.

## Label-stripping result
Strip `Ehime`, `region`, `public`, `hub`, `local`, `external`, and `innovation`:

> A designer of a two-sided search market chooses whether to intensify meeting opportunities for one subset of type pairs and whether to enable meeting opportunities for another subset, anticipating equilibrium search/matching responses and welfare.

This is directly inside modern matching-platform search design.

## Routing
Do not construct a formal `0/H/P/HP` Search model as a theory contribution. Do not add information asymmetry, network payoffs, club utility, fixed costs, public/private objective differences or arbitrary H×P complementarity to rescue it.

The policy question remains usable in two ways:
1. **application/measurement route** — use established matching/search theory to diagnose whether a thin region needs better local matching or better external access;
2. **distinct evidence-based theory pivot** — only if new institutional evidence identifies a primitive not contained in general matching-platform design.

Neither route is authorized as an automatic continuation of this branch.
