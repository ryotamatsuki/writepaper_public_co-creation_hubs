## 1. Executive verdict

**GO TO STAGE 3R — EVIDENCE-CONSTRAINED MECHANISM SEARCH.**

This is a narrow search authorization, not a novelty certification and not permission to build a formal model. Stage 2R substantially reduces the available contribution space: regional local/external linkage heterogeneity, policy mixes, governance, delegation and modular programme design are all well established in prior literature. Stage 3R may ask only whether the evidence-supported combination of a two-dimensional local/external regional state and bounded portfolio decision scope yields a genuinely distinct architecture-misselection mechanism.

## 2. Stage 1R-M binding inputs
Stage 1R-M replaced the invalid prefecture-year architecture union with `principal × portfolio/commissioning episode` choice units. The core sample is frozen at 35 valid Japanese decisions: `0=5`, `H=15`, `P=7`, `HP=8`. Principal and portfolio boundaries are identified for all units. Architecture coding agreement was 94.3% (kappa 0.917).

## 3. Sample freeze
The 35 decision units are the core Japanese sample. New Japanese cases were not added to improve signs or counts. Stage 2R uses targeted source checks only for anti-confirmation, rationale clarification and transitions.

## 4. Cluster / pseudo-replication control
The sample contains 24 principals and 22 regions. `PRINCIPAL_YEAR_CLUSTER_MAP.csv` records principal, principal-year, region-year and portfolio-family clusters. Results are not interpreted as 35 independent observations. The main evidence is structural counts and within-principal comparisons rather than p-values.

Seven P-only units belong to seven different principals and seven different regions. Seven principals show more than one architecture in the core sample: Tokyo Metropolitan Government, Osaka City, Fukuoka City, Okayama City, Saga Prefecture, Akita Prefecture and Hiroshima Prefecture. Removing any one does not eliminate either P-only support or within-principal architecture variation.

## 5. Zero sensitivity
All bounded zeros: Z01–Z05 (`N=5`). Strong-zero set: Z01 Tokyo deep-tech grant, Z02 Okayama incorporation grant and Z05 Saga Startup Promote. Z03 (joint Tokyo/SME subsidy) and Z04 (Hiroshima PR procurement) have weaker comparison boundaries.

The main conclusions — P-only feasibility, within-principal architecture variation, governance non-determinism and P modularity — do not depend on zeros. The statement that `0` is an observable bounded instrument choice is retained but weakened to the strong-zero interpretation.

## 6. Boundary-sensitive cases
U09 Setouchi-i-Base and U11 K-NIC required adjudication of the P threshold. Dropping both leaves `0=5`, `H=15`, `P=7`, `HP=6`; all four regimes remain represented and all central Stage 2R-A findings survive.

## 7. Japanese architecture distribution
Core distribution: `0=5`, `H=15`, `P=7`, `HP=8`. P-only is not a Tokyo artifact: it also appears in Osaka, Saga, Aomori, Fukuoka, Akita and Okayama. HP is also dispersed across metropolitan and regional cases.

## 8. SF1 — H/P separability
**SURVIVE.** P-only survives strict coding in seven distinct principals/regions. NEXs remains a clean example of cross-regional brokerage; Aomori Blue Ocean explicitly matches local firms with outside-prefecture firms; Saga Connect+ and OIH GLOBAL BRIDGE are dedicated external modules. The result is institutional feasibility only: it rejects `P requires H` and does not prove P has higher social value in any region.

## 9. SF2 — Same-principal variation
**SURVIVE.** Public principal identity does not mechanically determine architecture. Tokyo contains P/HP/0; Okayama City H/P/0; Saga H/P/0. Osaka and Fukuoka contain HP/P; Akita H/P; Hiroshima H/0. This is one of the strongest constraints on the next model.

## 10. SF3 — Bounded policy task / portfolio variation
**SURVIVE.** Within the same principal, architecture changes with the bounded policy problem and programme scope. External-market/partner mandates produce P modules; local ecosystem/community mandates produce H modules; integrated ecosystem platforms produce HP; finance/communication-only instruments can be zero under the strict functional threshold. These task labels are evidence categories, not model primitives.

## 11. SF4 — P modularity
**SURVIVE.** OIH GLOBAL BRIDGE and Saga Connect+ provide strong same-principal evidence that extra-regional connectivity can be commissioned as a separate module. Fukuoka–CIC is additional supportive evidence with a looser partnership boundary. Modularity is not complementarity.

## 12. SF5 — Multidimensional regional state
**SURVIVE WITH CHANNEL BOUNDARY.** NISTEP separately records university–industry links with same-prefecture and outside-prefecture firms and documents heterogeneous regional profiles. Stage 1R-M avoids the old complementary-share problem by defining separate activity intensities. This supports treating local and external science–industry linkages as distinct dimensions. It does not establish a general two-dimensional ecosystem network and does not yet populate a complete numeric four-quadrant matrix for every historical decision episode.

## 13. SF6 — Differential H/P associations with regional state
**UNRESOLVED.** The project does not have sufficiently harmonised numeric pre-decision regional-state observations for all 35 historical decisions to state a credible directional association between H/P choice and local/external linkages. No regression or desired sign is reported. This negative result is binding.

## 14. SF7 — Governance variation
**SURVIVE as a descriptive constraint.** HP appears under concession/PFI, public-private operation, designated management, committee/coalition governance and other forms. Public-principal-strong units include 0, H, P and HP. Therefore broad legal/contract labels cannot be used as deterministic architecture primitives.

## 15. SF8 — Bounded zero
**WEAKEN — USABLE.** Strong-zero cases show that identifiable principals can choose finance/other support without commissioning H/P in the same bounded policy domain. Zero must never be interpreted as absence of regional intermediation.

## 16. Program-rationale coding
`PROGRAM_RATIONALE_CODING.csv` records the observed programme scope or stated purpose and its evidence maturity. It is used only as `POLICYMAKER-PERCEIVED NEED / PROGRAM SCOPE`, not as social-welfare truth. The most informative comparisons are same-principal menus in Tokyo, Okayama and Saga.

## 17. Near-match cases
A numerical cross-principal near-match exercise is **not validated** because the committed panel does not yet contain harmonised pre-decision numeric `z` for every historical episode. Stage 2R refuses researcher-selected pairs. Same-principal menu comparisons are used instead because they hold principal and regional environment more tightly.

## 18. Transition evidence
OIH GLOBAL BRIDGE (2026) and Saga Connect+ (2024) are strong modular P additions. Fukuoka–CIC (2025) is supportive. These observations establish an institutional choice margin, not a causal reason for adding P.

## 19. Japanese anti-confirmation
Anti-confirmation directly rejects simple stories: local/public sponsorship does not imply H; delegated governance does not imply P; long or integrated governance does not uniquely imply HP; regional state alone cannot explain within-principal programme variation. SF6 is left unresolved rather than fitted to the data.

## 20. Leave-one-out results
The central qualitative facts are distributed: P-only uses seven distinct principals/regions; within-principal variation uses seven principals; strong zeros use at least three; modular P has at least two strong same-principal cases. No central Stage 2R-A conclusion is dependent on Tokyo or Saga alone.

## 21. Stage 2R-A verdict

**PASS TO INTERNATIONAL PORTABILITY.**

The Japanese evidence constrains theory even though it does not identify causal effects. In particular, it kills region-wide aggregation, mandatory H→P sequencing, sponsor-type determinism and one-dimensional thinness stories.

## 22. Candidate primitive extraction
Five candidates entered the portability gate: local science–industry linkage state; external science–industry linkage state; bounded portfolio decision scope; task-specific finance; residual operator control/delegation.

## 23. International sample
Eight purposefully selected comparators cover Singapore, South Korea, the UK, Germany, Spain/Basque Country, Finland, Canada and Australia. The same strict H/P functional language is used. The purpose is portability, not an international representative dataset.

## 24. Primitive label stripping
Japanese legal forms are removed. PFI/designated-management labels become authority/delegation margins; dedicated procurement/subsidy becomes task-specific finance; separate H/P programmes become bounded decision scope; regional linkage measures become local/external science–industry states.

## 25. International portability
Enterprise Singapore's Global Innovation Alliance is the strongest confirming case for a public principal maintaining a P-specific external-network portfolio. BIND provides a regional-government P case importing worldwide startups for local venture clients. Catapult, Bayern Innovativ, Seoul Startup Hub, Maria 01, MaRS and LaunchVic show integrated/local-global architectures under very different ownership and governance arrangements.

## 26. International counterexamples
MaRS and Maria 01 are strong counterexamples to a direct-public-control explanation for HP. BIND is a counterexample to `regional public sponsor -> H`; it is deliberately outward-sourcing. EnterpriseSG is a counterexample to the idea that P must be embedded in H. Conversely, Catapult/Bayern/Seoul demonstrate that broad integrated portfolios remain common.

## 27. Boundary conditions
Portable decision-scope primitive requires: (i) an identifiable actor/coalition; (ii) a bounded decision opportunity; and (iii) genuine discretion over which intermediation functions sit inside that portfolio. It is not applicable to a researcher-created union of unrelated programs. Regional linkage primitives remain channel-specific unless broader network data are later validated.

## 28. Closest-theory re-kill
Prior art sharply narrows the contribution space. Hoppe–Ozdenoren and Calcagnini et al. kill generic innovation-intermediary search/matching. Grillitsch–Asheim, OECD regional policy-mix work and Boschma–Iammarino–Olechnicka (2026) establish that regional capabilities and external connections are place-specific policy concerns. Flanagan–Uyarra–Laranja, Magro–Wilson and Henderson establish policy mixes, layering, boundaries and governance. Aghion–Tirole kills residual control as a standalone novelty primitive; Holmstrom–Milgrom and Siqueira–Sandler are binding threats to multitask/earmark and multi-principal stories.

Therefore the project may not claim novelty for `different regions need different mixes`, `external linkages matter`, `policy portfolios matter`, `governance matters`, or `delegation matters`.

## 29. Final primitive shortlist
Three core primitives remain:
1. **P1 — local science–industry linkage state / local-linkage gap** (`SOCIAL-VALUE`; non-novel state input; channel boundary).
2. **P2 — external science–industry linkage state / external-linkage gap** (`SOCIAL-VALUE`; non-novel state input; very strong Boschma-2026 threat).
3. **P3 — scope of portfolio authority: integrated H/P composition choice versus bounded task-specific module** (`DECENTRALIZED-CHOICE`; empirically strong/portable; major policy-mix and governance prior-art threat).

Residual control is killed as standalone. Earmarked finance is demoted and is not part of the frozen Stage 3R core.

## 30. Theory-paper viability
**Yes, narrowly.** Evidence and prior art leave a specific theoretical question: can a regional local/external state combined with bounded portfolio decision scope produce a discrete `0/H/P/HP` architecture-misselection mechanism that is not merely standard policy mix, spillover, delegation, multitask or common agency? This has not been established; Stage 3R is explicitly authorized to kill it.

## 31. Empirical/mixed-paper viability
**Yes.** The decision-unit dataset, same-principal programme menus, modular transitions and international institutional mapping are independently useful. Causal claims remain unauthorized; a stronger numeric pre-decision regional-state panel would be required for econometric work.

## 32. Stage 2R final verdict

**GO**

Routing: `GO TO STAGE 3R — EVIDENCE-CONSTRAINED MECHANISM SEARCH`.

The GO is conservative: the three primitives are inputs, not contributions. The strongest threat is that regional policy-mix/place-based literature already contains the economics in conceptual form and that any formal model will merely translate it into notation.

## 33. Stage 3R contract
Stage 3R may use **only P1, P2 and P3** as core primitives. It may generate at most six candidate mechanisms, preferably by pairing the two-dimensional regional state with alternative interpretations of bounded portfolio scope. It may not add lobbying, generic fiscal spillovers, dynamics, endogenous network formation, absorptive capacity, multitask incentives, common agency, platform pricing or new players to rescue a candidate. Stage 3R must select at most one candidate for a Stage 4R minimal-model kill test.
