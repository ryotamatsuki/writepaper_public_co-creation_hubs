# Label-Stripping Test

## Search
Original labels removed:
- no region;
- no public hub;
- no innovation;
- no local/external language.

Stripped model family:
`A two-sided frictional matching environment has an incumbent submarket A and a segmented/inaccessible submarket B. One intervention increases matching productivity in A; another reduces the access/segmentation barrier to B. Agents may reallocate search effort across accessible submarkets, changing tightness and congestion.`

### Result
This is **not obviously identical** to Gehrig's one-market intermediary or Spray's exogenous domestic/international search setting, but it lies very close to targeted/directed-search and market-segmentation design theory, especially Mekonnen (2026). The only defensible residue is the asymmetric policy technology: productivity improvement in A versus access creation to B.

**Verdict: CONDITIONAL / unresolved exact structural prior art.**

## Network
Stripped family:
`An intervention can increase links within a group or add links from that group to another group. Internal and bridging links can have different informational values and redundancy.`

### Result
This is standard network formation/brokerage/closure-versus-bridge theory. Jackson-Wolinsky, Burt/Ahuja and subsequent network literature absorb it; the regional literature makes the mapping even closer.

**Verdict: KILL.**

## Club
Stripped family:
`An authority may provide a congestible shared service to a group and separately provide access to outside resources.`

### Result
The first technology is standard club theory. The second is not generated by the family. Combining them is a hybrid by construction.

**Verdict: KILL.**

## Strongest label-stripping lesson
The labels `hub` and `pipeline` do not rescue any family. Search survives only if H and P remain technologically asymmetric after stripping all institutional labels. If the next audit shows that this intensive-versus-extensive intervention pair is already a standard submarket-design problem, Search must be killed as well.
