## Executive verdict

**CONDITIONAL GO — SEARCH FAMILY ONLY.**

Network/Brokerage is killed by close prior art on local closure/buzz versus external brokerage/pipelines. Club/Congestion is killed as a complete H/P foundation because H is naturally a congestible club/agglomeration service but P has to be appended as an external-access benefit rather than arising from club theory itself.

Search/Matching is the only family that survives the family-level gate, but only narrowly. Generic `intermediary reduces search costs` is already known; local versus distant innovation search is known; domestic versus international search externalities are known; public information can optimally segment frictional search markets; and subsidized trade fairs can reduce international search/matching frictions while displaying congestion. The surviving residue is therefore not generic search intermediation. It is the asymmetric policy-technology distinction between (i) raising matching productivity inside an existing thin local market and (ii) reducing segmentation/access barriers to a distinct external matching market, with endogenous market tightness/search allocation potentially linking the two.

Because this residue is very close to multiple literatures but no exact formal paper was located in this audit that jointly lets a public actor choose those two asymmetric interventions, the correct verdict is **CONDITIONAL GO**, not GO. One exact-prior-art resolution round is authorized before any formal model.

## Search family
### Best H definition
`H = intervention that raises effective meeting/matching productivity inside the existing local partner-search market.`

### Best P definition
`P = intervention that lowers the segmentation/access barrier to a distinct extra-regional partner-search market, thereby making an outside matching pool operationally reachable.`

This definition is deliberately stronger than `P = a larger pool`. The latter is killed by the search-family gate.

### What is already known
- Search intermediaries lower costly partner-search frictions (Gehrig 1993; middlemen literature).
- Innovation intermediaries conduct local/distant search and partner search (Lopez-Vega et al. 2016; Meulman et al. 2018; Howells 2022).
- Matching functions naturally contain thick-market and congestion externalities.
- Domestic and international firm-to-firm search can have different search externalities (Spray 2021).
- Public information/segmentation can alter directed-search allocation and welfare (Mekonnen 2026).
- Export-promotion/trade-fair interventions reduce foreign matching frictions; the 2026 trade-fair evidence explicitly finds matching gains that depend on buyer abundance and domestic rivalry/congestion.

### Narrow distinct residue
A thin-region authority deciding between two technologically asymmetric margins: improve local matching productivity versus open/reduce segmentation to an external matching market. Search allocation across the two markets can make local and external policy margins interact through tightness/congestion without adding a separate Club primitive.

### Verdict
**CONDITIONAL GO — one exact-prior-art blocker.**

## Network family
The strongest structural threat is direct. Bathelt, Malmberg & Maskell (2004) distinguish local buzz from deliberately constructed global pipelines. Morrison (2013) formalizes when global pipelines increase cluster knowledge as a function of cluster size, knowledge endowment and local diffusion. Operti & Kumar (2023) distinguish internal regional brokerage from external boundary-spanning and find that balanced configurations can outperform simultaneously high brokerage on both dimensions.

After label stripping, `H = strengthen internal links` and `P = add bridging links` is already a mature network/brokerage problem. Making links endogenous moves the model toward Jackson-Wolinsky-style network formation rather than creating a hub-specific technology.

**Verdict: KILL — NETWORK ABSORBED.**

## Club family
Buchanan club theory and its extensions already derive optimal membership/provision under congestion. Spatial-club and agglomeration literatures already combine scale/agglomeration with crowding. Coworking evidence supports proximity-induced knowledge spillovers, including randomized spatial assignment evidence.

H is therefore natural as a congestible shared local interaction service. P is not. To obtain P one must append external variety/access as a separate technology. After label stripping this is `congestible club + outside option/access technology`, not a unified club mechanism.

**Verdict: KILL — CLUB ABSORBED / P NOT ENDOGENOUS TO FAMILY.**

## Hybridization test
Search matching functions naturally generate thick-market and congestion effects. Retaining those effects inside Search does **not** constitute Search+Club hybridization. By contrast, adding network-specific brokerage payoffs, agglomeration utility, information asymmetry, or club membership utility to rescue Search is prohibited.

## Policy fit for Ehime
The Search family yields the most operational diagnostic. The relevant question is not simply whether Ehime has many or few actors, but whether both sides of a local collaboration market are thick enough to sustain matches, how imbalanced the sides are, how long matching takes, and whether useful outside counterpart pools are inaccessible because of segmentation/search barriers.

Candidate observables include local seeker mass, local potential-partner mass, market tightness, time-to-match, match success, unmatched demand, repeat search, local versus outside match share, and the number/quality of reachable outside counterpart pools. These are measurement targets, not currently validated variables.
