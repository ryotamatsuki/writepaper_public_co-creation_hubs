# Club / Congestion Family Audit

## Family question
Can a congestible shared local interaction platform generate both H and P as distinct technologies, rather than providing only a natural model for H?

## Subfamilies screened
- Buchanan club goods;
- local congestible public facilities;
- agglomeration plus congestion;
- shared intermediary/staff capacity;
- coworking and innovation-hub proximity effects.

## Closest prior art
### Buchanan (1965) and Sandler–Tschirhart (1997)
Club theory already makes membership/provision jointly endogenous under crowding/congestion. Later club models allow heterogeneous membership, transaction costs, uncertainty and other institutional complications.

### Local/spatial clubs
The local-public-good and spatial-club literature already studies facility scale, membership, location and congestion. Hochman (2011) explicitly studies agglomeration of spatial clubs.

### Innovation agglomeration and congestion
Sedgley & Elmslie (2004) study both agglomeration and congestion in innovation. Other regional work finds diminishing/inverted-U effects from concentrating innovation activities.

### Coworking / innovation hubs
Roche, Oettl & Catalini (2022) exploit randomized office assignment in a large technology coworking hub and find strong proximity-driven knowledge spillovers mediated by social interaction. Recent peripheral-coworking research also treats temporary co-location and proximity as mechanisms for knowledge exchange.

## Strength
Club theory provides an excellent microfoundation for H:
`H = an excludable/shared local interaction service whose benefit has scale/thickness economies but whose per-user quality can fall with utilization/capacity congestion.`

This naturally disciplines the claim `thin region => build a hub`: if membership is too small, fixed/shared provision may not pay; if utilization is excessive relative to capacity, congestion lowers value.

## Fatal weakness for the H/P architecture question
P does not arise naturally from club theory. To represent P one must append something like `external variety`, `outside-resource access`, or a second club/network. Once that is done, the model is no longer a one-family Club foundation; it is a congestible local club plus an unrelated external-access technology.

After label stripping:
`congestible shared service + outside-access technology`.

The H side is standard club theory, while the P side has no club-theoretic microfoundation.

## Kill-test results
1. H standard club good? **YES.**
2. Optimal size from congestion? **Known.**
3. P appended external benefit? **YES — fatal.**
4. H/P reducible to club A/B? **Either yes or requires a second unrelated technology.**
5. Four regimes from fixed costs/independent benefits? **Would be mechanical.**
6. External-access gap microfounded inside family? **NO.**
7. Agglomeration+congestion as novelty? **Unavailable.**
8. Hub-specific mechanism? **H application only.**

## Policy usefulness
For Ehime, club measures remain useful diagnostics: active member mass, interaction frequency, repeated participation, occupancy/utilization, staff or mentor capacity, wait times, number of simultaneous projects, staff-hours per project and other congestion proxies.

But policy usefulness does not establish a complete H/P theory.

## Verdict
**KILL — CLUB ABSORBED / P NOT ENDOGENOUS TO FAMILY.**

Club mechanisms may later be used only if the research question narrows to `0 vs H` or if new evidence independently justifies an external-access technology in a distinct pivot. Do not append P to Club merely to recover the four architectures.
