# Search Family Audit

## Family question
Can Search/Matching make H and P economically distinct enough to support a thin-region `0/H/P/HP` policy problem without reducing to generic search-cost reduction?

## Subfamilies screened
1. Random search / matching-function models.
2. Directed or targeted search across submarkets.
3. Search intermediaries / middlemen.
4. Heterogeneous-quality search and screening.
5. Two-market domestic/local versus external/international search.

## Binding prior art
### Intermediation
Gehrig (1993), *Intermediation in Search Markets*, already models heterogeneous agents choosing between costly direct search and an intermediary. Gong, Qiao & Wright (2025), *Middlemen in Search Equilibrium: A Survey*, confirms that search intermediation is a mature theory family covering superior search, bargaining, quality recognition, inventories and other intermediary capabilities.

**Kill:** `H/P = intermediary lowers search costs` is unavailable.

### Thick-market and congestion externalities
Matching-function/search theory already contains the positive thick-market externality and negative congestion externality. Recent generalized Hosios work and spatial transport-search work make this explicit.

**Kill:** adding `agglomeration benefit - congestion cost` separately to a matching model is unnecessary if the same effects arise through market tightness.

### Innovation partner search
Lopez-Vega, Tell & Vanhaverbeke (2016) explicitly distinguish local versus distant innovation search and study an innovation intermediary. Meulman et al. (2018) study intermediaries overcoming local-search constraints to identify distant partners. Howells & Thomas (2022) conceptualize innovation intermediaries as participating across stages of partner/solution search and explicitly discuss local versus distant search.

**Kill:** `innovation intermediary enables distant search` is not a contribution.

### Domestic versus international search markets
Spray (2021) builds firm-to-firm matching with domestic and international markets and allows search externalities to differ by market, including positive information-sharing and negative congestion. The model is quantitative and has welfare counterfactuals.

**Kill:** simply splitting search into local and external markets is not novel.

### Public/collective search-market design
Mekonnen (2026), *How to Segment a Search Market: Information Design and Directed Search*, studies public information that segments a two-sided frictional search market into submarkets. Buyer allocation responds endogenously; thick-market/congestion externalities determine equilibrium and constrained-efficient segmentation.

**Kill:** `publicly designed segmentation changes search allocation and congestion` is already formal theory.

### External matching interventions
Export-promotion evidence and theory already treat government-supported trade missions/fairs and export agencies as interventions that reduce search/matching frictions to foreign buyers. Atkin, Khandelwal & Osman (2017) experimentally study an intervention reducing matching frictions to sophisticated foreign buyers. Makioka (2021) studies Japanese trade-fair support. Békés, Molnar & Steinwender (2026) study subsidized trade fairs as a device reducing international search/matching frictions and find effects varying with potential buyers and domestic competition; the paper explicitly interprets the fair as a congestible platform.

**Kill:** `P = government helps firms find foreign/external partners` is not novel.

## Best surviving formulation
The only version not directly killed is deliberately asymmetric:

- `H`: raise matching productivity within an already-existing local search market.
- `P`: reduce the segmentation/access barrier that prevents local seekers from entering a distinct external search market.

This makes H an **intensive-margin matching-efficiency intervention** and P an **extensive-margin market-access intervention**, rather than two identical search subsidies or two independent pool expansions.

A plausible interaction can arise because agents allocate search effort across accessible submarkets, so changing local matching productivity can alter local tightness/congestion and the attractiveness of the external market, while opening external access reallocates search away from/to the local market. This interaction is internal to directed/two-market search and does not require adding Club congestion separately.

## Why this is not yet a GO
The component literatures are extremely close:
- Spray supplies domestic/international search externalities;
- Mekonnen supplies public submarket design, endogenous directed-search allocation, thickness and congestion;
- export-promotion/trade-fair literature supplies publicly supported external matching;
- Gehrig/middlemen supply intermediation.

No exact formal paper was located in this audit that lets a public actor choose **both** (i) local matching-productivity improvement and (ii) external-market access/segmentation reduction as asymmetric policy technologies and then compares `0/H/P/HP`. But novelty cannot be inferred from that search failure.

## Kill-test results
1. H = generic search-cost reduction? **KILLED; surviving H must be matching productivity/tightness technology.**
2. P = simply larger pool? **KILLED; surviving P must be an extensive access/segmentation margin.**
3. Local/external distinction already known? **YES.**
4. Four regimes from two independent investments? **PROHIBITED.**
5. Fixed-cost thresholds only? **PROHIBITED.**
6. Hosios/thickness/congestion application only? **INSUFFICIENT.**
7. Public hub irrelevant after label stripping? **MAJOR risk.**
8. Generic search technology A/B? **MAJOR risk; asymmetric intensive-vs-extensive margin is the only residue.**

## Family verdict
**CONDITIONAL GO — SEARCH FAMILY.**

### Exact blocker
Determine whether formal prior art already studies public choice between improving matching productivity in one incumbent submarket and reducing access/segmentation to another submarket, with endogenous reallocation/search tightness. One targeted literature round only.

If absorbed: `NO-GO — SEARCH ABSORBED`.
If not absorbed: authorize a minimal **social-architecture-only** Search model. No decentralized wedge.
