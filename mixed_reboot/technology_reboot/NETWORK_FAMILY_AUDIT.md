# Network / Brokerage Family Audit

## Family question
Can Network/Brokerage give a distinct technology foundation in which H strengthens local ties and P creates external bridges, without simply relabeling mature network/cluster theory?

## Subfamilies screened
- strategic network formation;
- information/knowledge diffusion on networks;
- structural holes and brokerage;
- local closure versus external bridging;
- regional innovation networks / local buzz-global pipelines.

## Closest prior art
### Jackson & Wolinsky (1996)
Strategic network formation already compares stable and efficient networks when agents form or sever links. Any endogenous H/P link formation model immediately enters a mature stability/efficiency literature.

### Burt / Ahuja
Structural-hole and brokerage theories already provide the information/non-redundancy rationale for bridging disconnected groups. Ahuja (2000) also shows that structural holes can harm innovation in interfirm collaboration networks, so closure-versus-brokerage is already a non-monotone innovation question.

### Bathelt, Malmberg & Maskell (2004)
This is a direct conceptual threat. The paper explicitly distinguishes local `buzz` generated by local embeddedness from deliberately constructed `global pipelines` to selected outside knowledge providers.

### Morrison (2013)
This is a direct formal threat. A formal cluster knowledge-diffusion model studies when global pipelines increase local knowledge as a function of cluster size, knowledge endowment and internal knowledge transmission.

### Operti & Kumar (2023)
This is the strongest recent empirical structural threat. They distinguish **internal brokerage** connecting otherwise disconnected local inventors from **external boundary-spanning** connecting the local network to other regions, and show that configurations balancing the two can outperform high brokerage on both levels.

## Label stripping
Proposed family becomes:
- H: subsidize/induce internal links or internal brokerage;
- P: subsidize/induce bridging links to another network/community.

This is a standard two-link-class network/brokerage problem. Adding the word `regional` does not create a new mechanism.

## Kill-test results
1. H = add local links, P = add external links? **YES — fatal.**
2. Local closure vs bridging already known? **YES — fatal.**
3. Redundancy/diminishing returns already known? **YES.**
4. Public policy architecture merely network subsidy? **HIGH risk.**
5. Generic link type A/B relabeling? **YES.**
6. Need endogenous network formation for distinct mechanism? **Likely, which increases complexity and prior-art exposure.**
7. Minimality? **WEAK relative to Search/Club.**
8. Four regimes from link-cost thresholds? **Would be mechanical and prohibited.**

## Policy usefulness
Network measures could still be empirically valuable for Ehime: local network density, connected components, brokerage centrality, repeated ties, outside-region bridging ties and redundancy. But measurement usefulness does not rescue theory novelty.

## Verdict
**KILL — NETWORK ABSORBED.**

The family is retained as an empirical measurement language only. Do not use network formation, structural holes or local buzz/global pipelines as the next formal theory foundation unless a genuinely new empirically grounded primitive is discovered later.
