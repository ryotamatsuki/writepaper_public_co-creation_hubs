# Policy Measurement Map — Ehime Use Case

The variables below are **measurement targets**, not claims that the project already possesses valid data for them.

## Search / Matching — preferred family
### Local market
- number of organizations actively seeking collaboration/problem-solving partners;
- number of potential local solution/knowledge providers;
- side-specific composition by firm/university/startup/expert category;
- local market tightness or imbalance between seekers and providers;
- median time from articulated need to first credible counterpart;
- probability of a viable match within a fixed period;
- unmatched/abandoned search rate;
- repeated search rate;
- number of candidate introductions per successful project;
- match quality / progression to project formation.

### External market
- share of searches that require outside-prefecture counterparties;
- number of reachable outside counterpart pools/networks before P;
- outside match share;
- time/cost to obtain an outside introduction;
- external search abandonment rate;
- external-market segmentation/access indicators such as formal network membership, dedicated brokerage route, or reciprocal network access.

### Operational policy question
`Is Ehime's bottleneck low matching productivity inside a viable local market, or insufficient access to a separate external matching market?`

That distinction maps directly to the retained H/P technology interpretation.

## Network / Brokerage — measurement only after theory kill
- local collaboration-network density;
- connected components;
- isolates;
- repeated ties;
- brokerage/betweenness;
- external bridging ties;
- outside-region centrality;
- redundancy / overlap of partner sets.

Policy question: `Are local actors fragmented, or are external bridges missing?`

These variables may be useful empirically, but Network is not authorized as the formal foundation.

## Club / Congestion — measurement only after theory kill
- active membership;
- visit/event participation;
- repeat participation;
- coworking/meeting-space occupancy;
- utilization peaks;
- staff/mentor hours per user/project;
- wait time for mentoring/matching;
- projects per coordinator;
- interaction frequency;
- participant-reported relevance/noise.

Policy question: `Does a shared local platform have sufficient critical mass, and where does service capacity become congested?`

These variables may be useful for facility/operation evaluation even though Club is killed as the complete H/P theory.

## Minimum future data discipline
If Search passes the final prior-art blocker, the next empirical validation should distinguish **pre-policy market conditions** from outcomes generated by H/P. In particular, outside-match share after a P program cannot be used as a baseline external-access state without an explicit treatment/post-choice label.
