## 1. Executive verdict

**NO-GO — THEORY ROUTE EXHAUSTED under the Stage 2R primitive freeze.**

The preserved motivation remains economically meaningful: regional conditions may change the desirable mix of local intermediation H and extra-regional connectivity P, while real public decisions are made through bounded portfolios. But Stage 3R cannot identify a non-tautological strategic mechanism using only the frozen primitives P1 local linkage state, P2 external linkage state, and P3 portfolio decision scope.

The key negative result is a primitive-sufficiency failure. P1/P2 describe the state; P3 describes which H/P composition choices sit inside a decision boundary. With common objectives, common payoff-relevant information and no cross-module externality/resource constraint/incentive wedge, componentwise or bounded decisions can reproduce the integrated optimum whenever H and P payoffs are separable. To generate `R^D != R^SP`, one must either (i) mechanically remove actions from a decision unit, making the result an imposed choice-set restriction, or (ii) introduce an additional economic primitive such as cross-task substitution/complementarity, a shared budget/capacity constraint, heterogeneous objectives, asymmetric information, externalities, or irreversibility. None is authorized by Stage 2R.

Therefore Stage 4R formalization is **not authorized**.

## 2. Binding motivation
The research question is retained as a motivation, not as an achieved theory result: under what regional conditions should realized intermediation be 0/H/P/HP, and why might decentralized institutional choice yield another composition?

## 3. Stage 2R constraints
Frozen primitives only:
- `P1 L`: local science–industry linkage state, channel-specific and non-novel.
- `P2 X`: external science–industry linkage state, channel-specific and non-novel.
- `P3 S`: portfolio decision scope, integrated versus bounded/task-specific authority.

No lobbying, fiscal spillovers, dynamics, endogenous networks, absorptive capacity, multitask incentives, common agency, platform pricing, participant entry, private competition or new funding primitive may be used to rescue a candidate.

## 4. Why model form was reopened
Stage 1R showed that a prefecture is not a unitary decision maker. Stage 1R-M showed that actual observations are principal × bounded portfolio/commissioning episodes. Stage 2R showed P-only, same-principal 0/H/P variation and modular P. Thus the old single four-way choice is only a benchmark, not a canonical decentralized architecture.

## 5. Model-architecture candidates
Six candidates were compared: A integrated single-principal, B componentwise modular, C sequential modular, D bounded portfolio/task-specific, E hierarchical authority and F multiple bounded principals.

D scores highest on evidence fit; B is the strongest minimal benchmark. C is not required by the evidence, E adds unsupported hierarchy and collapses toward delegation, and F overstates the importance of multiple principals because same-principal variation already generates the observed architecture diversity.

## 6. Stage 3R-A shortlist
Shortlist: **D Bounded Portfolio / Task-Specific Choice** and **B Componentwise Modular Choice**. A is retained only as the integrated benchmark/counterfactual.

## 7. Mechanism competition
Six mechanisms were tested: scope-constrained composition, modular duplication/omission, sequential scope dependence, portfolio-level substitution neglect, distributed architecture aggregation and scope-state mismatch.

The strongest conceptual candidate was scope-state mismatch (M-F), followed by portfolio-level substitution neglect (M-D) and sequential scope dependence (M-C). None survives the primitive-essentiality and feature-stripping tests.

## 8. Primitive-sufficiency / two-by-two result
The desired mechanism should occur only with both a multidimensional state `(L,X)` and bounded/split scope. No candidate satisfies that standard without importing another primitive.

- If H and P are separable and all units maximize the same objective with the same relevant information, split scope alone does not create a welfare wedge.
- If a bounded unit is literally prohibited from selecting the socially preferred alternative, misselection is true by construction and does not require multidimensional regional state.
- If H/P interact economically, a substitution/complementarity/externality primitive has been added.
- If one unit ignores X or another ignores L, an information/objective restriction has been added.
- If independent units over- or under-provide modules because they do not internalize each other, this is generic coordination/public-good fragmentation and adds an externality/objective wedge.

Thus neither P1/P2 nor P3 jointly produces a distinct strategic feedback under the authorized search space.

## 9. Closest-theory kill
The model-form route is also crowded. Flanagan, Uyarra & Laranja (2011) explicitly reject a unitary policy-maker view and frame innovation policy as multi-actor, multi-level interacting instruments. Borras & Edquist (2013) treat instrument selection and policy mixes as problem-oriented system design. Magro & Wilson (2019) link regional policy mixes to governance. Sah & Stiglitz (1986) formally show that decision architecture affects selection errors; Csaszar (2012, 2013) develops organization-design consequences for omission/commission and exploration/exploitation; Sharapov & Dahlander (2025) studies evolving innovation-selection regimes. Hegele (2021) directly studies portfolio combinations and interdepartmental coordination. Besanko, Regibeau & Rockett (2005) formalize organizational form with multiple tasks and cross-task interactions.

These papers do not contain the exact H/P regional application, but they make generic claims about task partition, decision architecture, policy integration, and organizational error unavailable as novelty claims.

## 10. Label stripping
Remove region/public/innovation/hub/pipeline/local/external. The strongest candidate becomes: a two-dimensional state and two possible tasks are allocated across bounded decision units; restricted or fragmented task scope may yield a different task portfolio than integrated choice. Without an additional payoff, information or incentive wedge, this is either equivalent to integrated component choice or a restricted-action tautology. With such a wedge added, it maps to mature organization-design, delegation, multitask or coordination literatures.

## 11. Feature stripping
- Remove L/X multidimensionality: M-F can still generate a different action only if scope removes actions; therefore multidimensionality is not essential to the imposed distortion.
- Remove bounded scope: the proposed distortion vanishes, but only because P3 itself defines the action partition; this does not establish a strategic mechanism.
- Merge decision units: M-E disappears into integrated choice; its content was generic fragmentation.
- Remove sequencing: M-C collapses into static scope restriction; sequencing is not evidence-essential.
- Replace H/P with generic tasks A/B: all surviving intuitions remain generic organizational-design or policy-integration problems. Regional labels are not theorem-essential.

## 12. Referee conclusion
The fatal attack is: **“Where does the wedge come from?”** Under P1–P3 alone there is no endogenous reason for bounded decisions to diverge from integrated welfare choice. If the answer is “because the unit is not allowed to choose the other task,” the result is tautological. If the answer invokes objectives, information, resources, cross-task returns or externalities, the model has silently added a new primitive.

## 13. Theory-paper viability
**NO under the current evidence-constrained primitive set.** This is not evidence that the motivating policy question is unimportant. It means the current data/literature sequence has not identified a new economic primitive capable of supporting a distinct formal theory.

## 14. Empirical/mixed-paper viability
**YES.** The decision-unit dataset, same-principal program menus, P-only architecture, modular transitions and international institutional comparison remain viable outputs. Stronger pre-decision regional-state data could support a descriptive or empirical paper without claiming a new formal mechanism.

## 15. Stage verdict

**NO-GO — THEORY ROUTE EXHAUSTED.**

No Stage 4R model is authorized. Reopening a theory branch requires a distinct Stage 0/2 evidence-based pivot that identifies one new primitive rather than adding features to P1–P3 after failure.
