# Regional Variation Matrix

Stage 0R does not estimate causal effects. This matrix records which regional conditions can plausibly be measured comparably and where the pilot sample already falsifies one-dimensional `thinness` stories.

| Regional dimension | Public measurement route | Pilot variation signal | H/P differentiation hypothesis | Stage 0R status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| General actor density | 2021 Economic Census establishment/employment tables | metropolitan and small-prefecture cases coexist in HP class | scale may raise H match opportunities but does not uniquely predict P | measurable |
| Startup/research actor density | official startup databases; MEXT university/research data | TIB/STATION Ai/FGN/OIH are dense; IIB/Setouchi show HP outside top metros | not sufficient as one-dimensional thickness | measurable with harmonization |
| Local network fragmentation | project/co-patent/cooperation data | not yet coded consistently | likely H-specific value primitive | difficult but potentially central |
| Baseline external connectivity | cross-region project/co-patent links; national ecosystem programs | explicit P architectures vary sharply | likely P-specific gap | medium feasibility |
| Private intermediary availability | accelerator/TTO/VC/bank/chamber inventory | clearly richer in metropolitan regions, but not yet normalized | key for real `0` regime and crowd-out baseline | manual but feasible |
| Industrial specialization / related variety | Economic Census industry employment | strong expected cross-region heterogeneity | may move H and P differently | high feasibility |
| University/research capacity | MEXT School Basic Survey and research institution records | strong regional variation | may increase both match opportunities and external knowledge demand | high-medium feasibility |
| Accessibility | official transport/geospatial data | very large variation | changes P cost, but must not become a mechanical distance theorem | high feasibility |
| Existing public support infrastructure | official program inventory | multiple overlapping systems in Tokyo/Aichi/Fukuoka; fewer in smaller regions | essential denominator for architecture coding | manual but feasible |

## Strongest anti-one-dimensionality finding

The observed architecture does not line up with a simple `larger ecosystem -> HP, smaller ecosystem -> H` ordering. Ishikawa explicitly combines local startup exchange with importing Tokyo resources, and Kagawa explicitly positions Setouchi-i-Base as a broad hub attracting people and firms from outside the prefecture. Meanwhile several larger regional-city programs remain H-heavy in their core public descriptions.

This is **not** evidence that HP is socially optimal in small regions. It is evidence that the model must allow regional dimensions other than actor count to change the relative returns to H and P.

## Stage 1R measurement priorities

1. Build region-year actor-density and sector-specialization variables from Economic Census.
2. Construct a baseline external-connectivity proxy independent of the focal hub (e.g., pre-period cross-region collaboration or ecosystem-network measures).
3. Inventory private/public alternative intermediaries to define `0` honestly.
4. Test whether local fragmentation can be measured using project or co-patent networks rather than assumed.
