## Cycle 1 — Architecture and case discovery

Search families covered prefectural/municipal innovation hubs, startup support facilities, regional open-innovation platforms and cross-regional startup programs. Primary-source hits yielded at least 18 usable candidates spanning Ehime, Aichi, Tokyo, Osaka, Fukuoka, Yokohama, Kawasaki, Hiroshima, Kagawa, Ishikawa, Kyoto, Okayama, Kumamoto, Nagasaki, Okinawa, Toyama and Nagano.

Key discovery: NEXs Tokyo is a P-dominant public architecture explicitly built around region-crossing JUMP/DIVE matching. This is direct anti-confirmation against the legacy assumption that P requires H.

## Cycle 2 — Pilot coding and governance/funding feasibility

Deep searches targeted procurement, operator contracts, official facility rules, partner systems and funding/management structures.

Eight cases were pilot-coded from primary sources: E:N BASE, STATION Ai, NEXs Tokyo, Osaka Innovation Hub, Setouchi-i-Base, MomoSta, Ishikawa Innovation Base and Tokyo Innovation Base.

Observed governance forms include:
- short/medium public outsourcing and JV operation;
- PFI BT + long-term concession;
- designated management;
- multi-local-stakeholder operating committee;
- multi-agency joint operation;
- metropolitan platform with multiple contracted modules.

## Cycle 3 — Regional-condition variation

Searches checked standardized regional data availability and national startup-ecosystem context. The 2021 Economic Census supplies comparable prefectural establishment/employment measures. University/research, industrial composition and accessibility variables have standard public data routes. Baseline local fragmentation and extra-regional collaboration are the hardest constructs and should be built from project/co-patent networks rather than assumed.

Anti-one-dimensionality signal: HP is found not only in Tokyo/Osaka/Aichi but also in Ishikawa and Kagawa, while several regional-city hubs are H-heavy. This is not a causal result; it is sufficient to reject actor count as the sole Stage 4R state variable.

## Cycle 4 — Institutional and within-case change

Priority was given to changes that alter H/P composition without rebuilding the whole institution.

Strongest candidate: Osaka Innovation Hub introduced the dedicated `OIH GLOBAL BRIDGE` overseas expansion program in 2026 on top of a hub operating since 2013. This is a clean P-intensification event candidate.

Additional candidates:
- Fukuoka City/CIC MoU in 2025 expanding international support links;
- E:N BASE physical/organizational launch in 2026 after prior digital co-creation activity;
- STATION Ai opening in 2024 and subsequent global-program expansion;
- IIB's FY2025 specialist support and explicit Tokyo-resource-import architecture.

## Cycle 5 — Gap fill: participant geography and architecture validity

Targeted searches tested whether local/outside participant composition is actually observable.

Strong positive cases:
- NEXs Tokyo public member search explicitly filters Hokkaido, Tohoku, Kanto, Tokyo, Hokuriku, Chubu, Kansai, Chugoku, Shikoku, Kyushu/Okinawa and overseas.
- TIB public startup database distinguishes Tokyo-region and outside-Tokyo locations.
- E:N BASE publishes 561 co-creation partner organizations and industries, but not a direct geography variable; location requires entity enrichment.

This confirms useful participant-geography observability for a subset while identifying nonrandom missingness across hub types.
