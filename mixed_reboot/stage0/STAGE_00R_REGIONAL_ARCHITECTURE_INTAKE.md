The failure was not algebra; it was theory selection. This Stage therefore changes research order from `model -> institution` to `institution -> observable variation -> primitive selection -> model`.

The Stage 4 negative result remains binding and is not repaired here.

## 3. Preserved policy motivation

The project continues to target:

`R^SP(z) in {0,H,P,HP}`

versus

`R^D(z,g,f,d,...) in {0,H,P,HP}`.

The eventual contribution target is architecture misselection `R^D != R^SP`, not merely a level distortion such as underinvestment.

## 4. Functional definition of 0/H/P/HP

`H` is dedicated local intermediation: local search, local partner discovery, matching, brokerage, local project formation, community coordination or local knowledge translation.

`P` is dedicated extra-regional connectivity: systematic outside-region partner search, national/global matching, external expert/investor recruitment, overseas support or resource import from another region.

`HP` is co-provision. It does **not** mean complementarity.

`0` means neither dedicated public H nor P after accounting for material alternative intermediation. It must be coded at a region-year system level, not inferred from absence of a building.

## 5. Revised research question

> What regional economic characteristics determine whether the socially optimal innovation-intermediation architecture is `0`, `H`, `P`, or `HP`, and why can decentralized institutional decision making select the wrong architecture?

## 6. Case universe

18 usable current Japanese cases/programs were screened from primary sources across Ehime, Aichi, Tokyo, Osaka, Fukuoka, Yokohama, Kawasaki, Hiroshima, Kagawa, Ishikawa, Kyoto, Okayama, Kumamoto, Nagasaki, Okinawa, Toyama and Nagano.

Provisional facility/program distribution:

- `H`: 11
- `P`: 1
- `HP`: 6
- `0`: 0 by construction of a positive-facility screen

The absence of `0` in this distribution is not evidence that `0` is rare. Stage 1R must construct the region-year denominator.

## 7. Architecture coding result

The functional distinction is feasible. The strongest architecture observation is **NEXs Tokyo as a P-dominant public program**: its JUMP/DIVE structure explicitly intermediates across regional boundaries and overseas. Therefore P can exist as a policy architecture without a technological requirement that H be installed first.

This directly rejects a core legacy assumption.

## 8. Regional-condition measurement

A one-dimensional `thinness = local actor count` variable is not defensible as the sole state variable.

Public measurement routes are strong for:

- business establishment/employment density;
- industrial specialization/related variety;
- university/research capacity;
- geographic accessibility;
- inventory of existing public support.

Measurement is harder but potentially more economically meaningful for:

- local network fragmentation;
- baseline extra-regional collaboration;
- private intermediary availability.

These hard variables deserve priority because they can move H and P values differently.

## 9. Funding measurement

Funding structure is observable in form but not yet consistently in shares.

The pilot identifies:

- ordinary prefectural outsourcing and grant dependence (E:N BASE);
- PFI/BT + concession with private SPC operation through 2034 (STATION Ai);
- public metropolitan program/contracting (TIB, NEXs);
- city grant/program operation through a public development agency (OIH);
- designated management plus user fees (Setouchi-i-Base);
- multi-local-stakeholder operating committee (MomoSta).

Exact annual operating cost, funder shares and H/P earmarks require Stage 1R extraction from budgets/contracts.

## 10. Governance / decision-right measurement

Governance is the strongest institutional dimension discovered in Stage 0R.

Superficially similar public innovation-support organizations differ materially in:

- operator identity;
- contract horizon;
- concession versus ordinary outsourcing;
- designated manager versus committee governance;
- number and level of public principals;
- partner/program rule-setting;
- degree of modular program contracting.

These are observable decision-right structures rather than an arbitrary `governance quality` index.

## 11. Access / neutrality measurement

Open registration, eligibility, geographic scope, selection and matching discretion can be coded separately. Stage 0R explicitly rejects a single neutrality index.

E:N BASE illustrates why: it is free and open to registered members and lists 561 partner organizations, but these facts alone do not establish a dedicated P function or neutral geographic allocation.

## 12. Participant composition

Participant geography is directly usable for an important subset:

- NEXs Tokyo exposes region filters covering all Japanese macro-regions and overseas.
- TIB's public startup database distinguishes Tokyo-region and outside-Tokyo locations.
- E:N BASE publishes partner identities/industries, permitting later address enrichment, but does not directly expose geography.

Thus participant geography is measurable but missingness is nonrandom across architecture types. Stage 1R must explicitly measure this missingness.

## 13. H/P service composition

Service functions can be coded more reliably than expenditure shares at this stage.

Important observation: P is often provided through a **separate program module or network relationship**, not a technologically inseparable feature of H. Examples include OIH GLOBAL BRIDGE, Fukuoka-CIC links, and IIB's explicit use of Tokyo resources.

This makes `H and P are complements` a proposition to derive/test, not a primitive.

## 14. Pilot coding

Eight cases were full-pilot-coded from primary sources:

1. E:N BASE
2. STATION Ai
3. NEXs Tokyo
4. Osaka Innovation Hub
5. Setouchi-i-Base
6. MomoSta
7. Ishikawa Innovation Base
8. Tokyo Innovation Base

The coding confirms practical observability of architecture, operator/governance form and access rules. Funding shares and matched-project geography remain the main missing data.

## 15. Cross-case architecture variation

Two Stage 0R facts are especially useful:

1. P-only is institutionally real (NEXs Tokyo).
2. HP is not confined to the largest ecosystems: Ishikawa and Kagawa explicitly combine local intermediation with external resource attraction/import.

This does **not** show which regime is optimal. It shows why the next theory needs a multidimensional regional state vector.

## 16. Regional variation

The sample spans globally connected metropolitan ecosystems and smaller prefectural economies with different industry structures, research assets and accessibility. Yet architecture is not ordered monotonically by apparent market size.

The most promising social-value primitives are therefore:

- local network fragmentation (H-specific gap candidate);
- baseline external connectivity/search friction (P-specific gap candidate);
- private/existing intermediary availability (critical for `0`);
- industrial specialization/related variety as a moderator.

## 17. Within-case architecture changes

### Strongest candidate: OIH 2026 P-intensification

OIH has existed since 2013 as a global innovation hub. In 2026 Osaka City introduced the dedicated `OIH GLOBAL BRIDGE` program for overseas expansion. This gives a clearly dated increase in P functionality layered on an existing architecture.

### Additional candidates

- 2025 Fukuoka City-CIC MoU expanding international startup-support links;
- 2026 E:N BASE launch after prior digital co-creation platform activity;
- 2024 STATION Ai opening followed by continuing global-program expansion;
- FY2025 IIB specialist consultation and explicit Tokyo-resource-import initiatives.

These are design candidates, not causal identification claims.

## 18. Anti-confirmation findings

The strongest counterexamples are:

- **NEXs:** P without required H.
- **IIB/Setouchi:** smaller regional systems deliberately deploy HP, rejecting simple `thin -> H` logic.
- **MomoSta:** multi-stakeholder local governance does not automatically produce P.
- **E:N BASE:** open/free access and a large partner list do not establish extra-regional architecture.
- **OIH:** P can be a modularly intensified function rather than a fixed complement to H.

## 19. Candidate stylized facts

These remain hypotheses for Stage 2R, not established facts:

### SF-A — Architecture is not one-dimensional in ecosystem size
Similar broad region classes can choose different H/P portfolios, and smaller regions can deploy HP.

### SF-B — P is technologically separable from H
NEXs and modular global programs show that P need not be produced only through an H technology.

### SF-C — Public governance form varies substantially at fixed broad policy purpose
Concession, designated management, JV outsourcing, multi-stakeholder committee and multi-agency arrangements coexist.

### SF-D — P capacity is often a separately commissioned/networked module
OIH GLOBAL BRIDGE, Fukuoka-CIC and IIB-Tokyo resource import are leading examples.

### SF-E — Geographic participant data is more observable in platformized programs than conventional physical hubs
This may matter both for research measurement and, only if contracts use those metrics, for organizational incentives.

## 20. Candidate social-value primitives

Priority order for later evidence testing:

1. **Local network fragmentation** — can raise H value even where actor mass is large.
2. **Baseline external connectivity/search friction** — can raise P value independently of local matching.
3. **Private/existing intermediation availability** — can make `0` socially plausible and prevent automatic public provision.
4. **Industrial specialization / related variety** — may cause H and P to respond differently to the same region size.

Actor mass remains a control/state variable, not the definition of thinness.

## 21. Candidate decentralized-choice primitives

Most promising observed institutional candidates:

1. **Control rights / delegation:** who can choose program portfolio and matching rules?
2. **Contract horizon / residual discretion:** concession versus short-horizon outsourcing may change incentives to build network assets.
3. **Funding earmarks / modular procurement:** H and P may be commissioned by different budgets/programs.
4. **Stakeholder representation:** committee or multi-principal governance may change portfolio choice.

These are not yet theory contributions. Each faces prior art in delegation, incomplete contracts, common agency and public organization theory.

## 22. Candidate regime-misselection mechanism

The strongest evidence-compatible hypothesis is presently:

> Regional social value depends on distinct local-fragmentation and external-connectivity gaps, but the entity controlling the intermediation portfolio has decision rights, contract horizons and funding earmarks that need not align with those gaps. Decentralized institutions may therefore choose the wrong **type** of architecture rather than merely too little total intermediation.

This is deliberately broader than a specific model. Stage 1R/2R must determine whether governance variables actually co-move with H/P allocation before Stage 3R chooses a mechanism.

## 23. Closest theory threats

Any later theory must survive at least:

- standard fiscal/spillover models for decentralized public provision;
- innovation-intermediary/search theory;
- delegation and incomplete-contract/control-rights theory;
- common agency and multi-principal models;
- multitask incentive theory if measurement/KPI asymmetry is invoked;
- two-sided/platform participation theory;
- regional innovation / local-global pipeline literature.

The mere combination of architecture choice and governance is not novelty.

## 24. Theory-paper viability

**Yes, conditionally on measurement.**

The theory route is better justified than at the start of Stage 0R because real architecture and governance variation exists, P-only is observed, and H/P separability is institutionally credible. But there is not yet a selected formal mechanism. Theory re-entry is prohibited until Stage 1R measurement validation and Stage 2R stylized-fact gate.

## 25. Empirical/mixed-paper viability

**Yes.** At minimum Level 0/1 institutional mapping is feasible. Level 2 is plausible using architecture transitions such as OIH's 2026 P-intensification and other program changes. No Level 3 causal design is claimed yet.
