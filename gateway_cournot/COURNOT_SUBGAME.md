# Homogeneous Cournot Subgame

Let

`A = a-cbar`, `m=2+beta`, and `n=beta-1`.

Marginal cost is

`c_i = cbar-(1+beta)x_i-beta x_j`.

For fixed engagement `(x_i,x_j)`, firm `i` solves

`max_{q_i} [a-q_i-q_j-c_i]q_i`.

The Cournot FOC is

`a-2q_i-q_j-c_i=0`.

Solving the two FOCs gives

`q_i^* = [A + (2+beta)x_i + (beta-1)x_j]/3`

and symmetrically for `j`.

Hence

`dq_i^*/dx_i = (2+beta)/3 > 0`,

`dq_i^*/dx_j = (beta-1)/3`.

The rival-engagement effect therefore changes sign exactly at `beta=1` **holding engagement choices fixed**:

- `beta<1`: rival strengthening dominates the common-core benefit for own output;
- `beta>1`: common-core thickening dominates the rival-strengthening effect for own output;
- `beta=1`: the two direct effects cancel in `q_i`.

This `beta=1` diagnostic is not the government gateway threshold after engagement is endogenized.

Using the Cournot FOC,

`P^*-c_i=q_i^*`,

so equilibrium operating profit is

`pi_i^{OP,*}=(q_i^*)^2`.

Total firm profit entering Stage 2 is therefore

`pi_i(x_i,x_j;E)=(q_i^*)^2-(K_i(E)/2)x_i^2`.

The absolute levels `a` and `cbar` affect the strategic solution only through `A=a-cbar`. For any solved `(A,x,q)` one can raise both `a` and `cbar` by the same level amount to ensure nonnegative marginal costs and positive prices without changing quantities, engagement or the strategic sign results.
