# Comparison with Gateway Stage 4R

Classification of the previous participation-payoff gateway model:

**PARTIAL DIAGNOSTIC ONLY.**

## What survives

The earlier model correctly identified two institutionally important primitives:

1. an own regional gateway improves access more than another region's gateway;
2. gateways feed users/firms into a common network-valued ecosystem rather than depleting the core.

The notions of gateway overlap and common-core thickening therefore remain useful.

## What changes under explicit firm competition

The Cournot model adds a genuinely distinct channel: rival engagement changes rival marginal cost and therefore equilibrium quantities, market shares, price, profits and consumer surplus.

The exact firm-level diagnostic is

`dq_i/dx_j=(beta-1)/3`.

Thus rival engagement is not simply a positive common-network spillover. It also strengthens a competitor.

## Old baseline-attractiveness threshold does not survive

In Stage 4R, the strategic sign switched with baseline core attractiveness `B`.

In the homogeneous-Cournot model, `A=a-cbar` scales equilibrium engagement/output by `A` and all gross gateway payoff differences by `A^2`. Therefore `A` does not change the sign of the government cross-entry effect.

The old `B*` theorem is not a reduced-form theorem of the Cournot model.

## Welfare conclusion changes

Stage 4R generated a particularly clean positive cross-region gateway effect and decentralized under-provision. With Cournot rivalry, the cross-region welfare effects can change sign. Both under- and over-provision occur.

## Bottom line

The Stage 4R model remains useful only as a diagnostic decomposition of access/network primitives. It is not the canonical model once the motivating requirement of explicit firm competition is imposed.
