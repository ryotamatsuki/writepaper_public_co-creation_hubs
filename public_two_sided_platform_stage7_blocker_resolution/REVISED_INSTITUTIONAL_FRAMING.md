# Revised Institutional Framing

## Preferred canonical phrase

**regional public innovation hubs and a shared metropolitan private innovation hub**

This phrase is preferred over the more abstract “decentralized public innovation intermediation with a shared metropolitan private outside option” because it retains the institutional structure that distinguishes the application from generic sequential-pricing theory.

## Candidate contribution wording

> We study decentralized regional public innovation hubs that compete for focal projects while sharing a metropolitan private innovation hub as an outside option. Cross-side network feedback can make regional public investments strategic complements when the private hub's access price is fixed, yet strategic substitutes when the private operator optimally reprices.

Welfare qualifier:

> Exact welfare accounting separates the strategic-interaction reversal from welfare rankings: the private access fee is a transfer at the aggregate level, while public investment generates real cross-regional allocation and network externalities.

International qualifier:

> The institutional primitives are not unique to Japan: municipal innovation-intermediation programmes coexist with priced metropolitan innovation campuses across multiple countries, although the paper does not claim that any one observed city reproduces every timing assumption of the model.

## Prohibited wording

Do not claim that all metropolitan hubs are private/profit-maximizing, that all projects are contractually single-homing, that public and private hubs are ecosystem-wide pure substitutes, or that private hubs are empirically known to cut fees after regional public expansion.
