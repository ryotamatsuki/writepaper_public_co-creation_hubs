# Substitute vs Complement Relationship

Real innovation institutions can cooperate even when their services are substitutes for one focal project's primary route.

CIC works with governments and other ecosystem organisations. Public programmes can refer participants to private facilities; private hubs can host public programmes; the same support organisation can interact with several ecosystems. Therefore the paper must not claim that regional public hubs and metropolitan private hubs are globally or institutionally pure competitors.

The frozen choice structure requires only a narrower margin:

> They may be substitutes for the primary intermediation route of a focal project, even when the institutions are complementary at the ecosystem level.

This statement is consistent with project-level single-homing and support-side multi-homing. It also protects the theory from an unnecessary empirical claim that every interaction between the institutions is competitive.

Referee attack “the hubs are complements, not substitutes”: **SURVIVES BUT NONFATAL**. Complementarity at ecosystem level does not logically eliminate substitution on the focal route margin modeled here.
