# Three-Layer Game

## Layer 1 — Regional government policy competition

Two regional governments simultaneously choose binary hub provision `E_i in {0,1}` and pay the public fixed cost of any hub they open.

Government objectives are regional welfare objectives. They do not maximize Hub fees or platform profit.

## Layer 2 — Explicit hub intermediation competition and cooperation

Each region-specific beneficiary firm has a collaboration project.

Conditional on open hubs:

1. the project observes its need/type and broad hub specialization;
2. if multiple hubs are feasible, it chooses the hub with the highest expected direct productive-match value generated by that hub's partner set;
3. the selected hub searches its partner pool and partner-specific compatibility is realized;
4. if no productive direct match is obtained and the rival hub is open, referral to the rival hub's non-overlapping partner capabilities may occur;
5. a successful match creates beneficiary-firm capability gain and complementary-partner surplus.

Competition and referral occur at this layer.

## Layer 3 — Downstream product-market competition

After productive collaborations are realized, beneficiary firms remain in their home regions and compete in the downstream product market with capabilities determined by their collaboration outcomes.

## Key separations

- Government competition: strategic provision of `E_i`.
- Hub competition: endogenous allocation of overlapping collaboration projects.
- Hub cooperation: referral to non-overlapping partner capabilities.
- Firm competition: downstream strategic interaction after matching.

None of these layers may be silently collapsed into another in Stage 4M-R.
