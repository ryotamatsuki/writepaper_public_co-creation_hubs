# Prior-Art Hard Kill

Search was updated through September 2026.

## Closest matching/intermediary threats

- Gehrig (1993), *Intermediation in Search Markets*, JEMS, DOI `10.1111/j.1430-9134.1993.00097.x`: heterogeneous agents choose intermediation versus search; competing intermediaries are already standard.
- Caillaud & Jullien (2003), *Chicken & Egg*, RAND, DOI `10.2307/1593720`: competing intermediation service providers with indirect network effects and nonexclusive use.
- Rochet & Tirole (2003), *Platform Competition in Two-Sided Markets*, JEEA, DOI `10.1162/154247603322493212`: generic platform competition and alternative governance structures.
- Damiano & Li (2008), *Competing Matchmaking*, JEEA, DOI `10.1162/JEEA.2008.6.4.789`: competing matchmakers sort heterogeneous participants when match value is complementary. This is the strongest direct threat to endogenous hub choice by productive fit.
- Sülzle (2009), *Duopolistic competition between independent and collaborative B2B marketplaces*, IJIO, DOI `10.1016/j.ijindorg.2009.02.004`: competing matching intermediaries and collaborative ownership.
- Gal-Or (2020), *Market segmentation on dating platforms*, IJIO, DOI `10.1016/j.ijindorg.2019.102558`: competing matching websites with heterogeneous users and compatibility.

## Productive / innovation matching

- Hoppe & Ozdenoren (2005), *Intermediation in Innovation*, IJIO, DOI `10.1016/j.ijindorg.2005.03.003`: innovation intermediaries economize on expertise under uncertainty.
- Banal-Estañol, Macho-Stadler & Pérez-Castrillo (2018), *Endogenous Matching in University-Industry Collaboration*, Management Science, DOI `10.1287/mnsc.2016.2680`: heterogeneous firm-academic collaboration and endogenous matching.
- Calcagnini et al. (2019), *Technology transfer with search intensity and project advertising*, JTT, DOI `10.1007/s10961-018-9667-3`: endogenous TTO-firm matching, search and externalities.

## Public/regional competition

- Walz / Walz & Wellisch (1996), strategic public provision of local inputs: regional governments compete using local public inputs for oligopolistic firms; decentralized provision can be inefficient.
- Takahashi (2004), *Spatial competition of governments in the investment on public facilities*, RSUE, DOI `10.1016/S0166-0462(03)00070-X`: governments invest in excludable public facilities, users choose spatially, and discrete public decisions are analyzed.

## Updated referral / competition threats

- Zhang, Cao & He, *Competitor referral by platforms*, Annals of Operations Research, DOI `10.1007/s10479-021-04020-4`: competing platforms can explicitly refer customers to direct competitors. Thus “competition plus competitor referral” is not novel as a broad mechanism.
- Kanoria, Min & Qian (2024/2025), *The Competition for Partners in Matching Markets*, Management Science, DOI `10.1287/mnsc.2023.00064`: modern matching theory directly studies competition for partners under heterogeneous preferences/connectivity.
- Kamada & Kojima (2026), *Choice or competition: does integration benefit everyone?*, Theoretical Economics 21(1): fragmented matching-market integration can expand choice while intensifying competition for resources.

## One-page absorption verdict

**NEAR, not an exact single-paper specialization.**

No inspected single paper simultaneously supplies binary regional public provision, immobile beneficiary firms, split regional match incidence, endogenous matchmaker choice and post-failure competitor referral.

However, the surviving Stage 4M-R theorem is only global substitution with referral providing an insufficient fallback. Damiano-Li absorbs the choice/sorting core, Zhang-Cao-He absorbs competitor referral at the platform level, and Takahashi/Walz absorb the strategic public-facility/public-input layer. The remaining combination does not generate a new sign theorem.

Therefore prior art reinforces, but is not the sole basis for, the NO-GO.
