# Stage 3M-R Minimality Audit

## Proposed primitives and why each is necessary

### 1. Two regional governments with binary hub provision

Necessary for decentralized public policy competition. Continuous quality is not needed at this stage.

### 2. Region-specific beneficiary firms/projects

Necessary to preserve downstream regional incidence while allowing a project to use either hub without firm relocation.

### 3. Partner pools `S_1,S_2`

Necessary to give hub differentiation productive-matching content and to derive both overlap and referral opportunities from one structure.

### 4. Heterogeneous project need/type `theta`

Necessary because without heterogeneity one hub would weakly dominate or projects would tie. Heterogeneity is economic compatibility heterogeneity, not arbitrary horizontal taste.

### 5. Productive fit `v(theta,s)`

Necessary to distinguish productive collaboration from mere contact. No specific functional form is frozen.

### 6. Endogenous first-hub choice

Necessary to create literal hub competition for intermediation demand.

### 7. Referral after failed direct matching

Necessary to represent cooperation using the rival's non-overlapping partner capabilities. It is the only authorized cooperative routing margin.

### 8. Split regional incidence of collaboration surplus

Necessary to make project allocation economically meaningful to public principals without hub fees or firm relocation. The beneficiary-firm component accrues where the firm resides; the complementary-partner component accrues where the partner resides.

### 9. Downstream strategic interaction

Retained from the research question but not used to create Hub choice. Stage 4M-R must hard-kill whether it materially changes hub competition or only welfare magnitudes.

## Explicitly unnecessary / excluded

- Hotelling distance;
- logit taste shocks;
- private hub prices;
- multihoming;
- capacity/congestion;
- hub quality investment;
- R&D spillovers;
- absorptive capacity;
- generic network utility;
- arbitrary overlap/referral payoff terms;
- endogenous firm location.

## Competing architecture hard kill

### H1 — Project Choice × Overlapping Partner Sets

**SELECTED — PRIMARY CANDIDATE.** It is the only candidate that creates literal project allocation, matching-content differentiation, overlap-based stealing and referral-based cooperation with the same primitive pair `S_1,S_2` and without adding pricing, network utility or spatial taste differentiation.

### H2 — Standard Two-Sided Competing Matchmakers

**REJECTED AS BASELINE; RETAINED AS ABSORPTION BENCHMARK.** It can generate intermediary choice and matching competition, but ordinarily makes both participant sides endogenous and relies on price/network-participation mechanisms that are unnecessary here. The project-choice submodule of H1 is nevertheless strongly threatened by this literature, so Stage 4M-R must show that the decentralized public-incidence results do not collapse to a relabeling.

### H3 — Hub Quality Competition

**REJECTED.** Binary provision plus partner-set composition is sufficient to generate meaningful competition. Endogenous or continuous hub quality would add a policy margin not authorized by the rollback and is not needed to prevent winner-take-all.

### H4 — Hotelling Hub Competition

**REJECTED AS CANONICAL; RETAINED AS BENCHMARK.** Spatial differentiation can mechanically sustain coexistence and has public-facility precedents, but it would make project shares depend on horizontal distance rather than productive matching. The baseline therefore does not use geography to manufacture interior allocation.

## Stage 3M-R hard-kill questions

### RQ1 — What exactly do hubs compete for?

**Productive collaboration projects attached to region-specific beneficiary firms.** The project, not the firm as a whole and not the complementary partner side, is the single baseline competitive object.

### RQ2 — Why can multiple hubs coexist instead of one capturing everything?

Because heterogeneous projects have different productive fit with heterogeneous partner pools. For some `theta`, `V_1(theta)>V_2(theta)`; for others the inequality reverses. Coexistence is therefore generated by matching heterogeneity rather than an arbitrary taste or distance term.

### RQ3 — What creates literal business/project stealing?

For project needs feasible through both partner ecosystems, opening the rival hub changes the feasible intermediary set. A project switches when the rival pool offers a higher expected productive-match value. The stealing effect is an allocation consequence of overlapping feasible matching opportunities, not a negative cross term.

### RQ4 — What creates cooperation?

After unsuccessful direct matching, the selected hub may refer the project to complementary capabilities available only through the rival pool. Cooperation is sequential routing to non-overlapping matching opportunities, not generic network utility.

### RQ5 — Can competition and cooperation be generated from the same structure?

**YES.** Common feasible capability coverage induced by `S_1,S_2` creates alternative mediators and project competition; non-overlapping capabilities in the same pools create gains from referral toward the effective union.

### RQ6 — Does endogenous Hub choice alter government incentives?

**YES at the architecture level, subject to formal hard kill in Stage 4M-R.** The beneficiary-firm gain follows the firm's home region while complementary-partner surplus follows the matched partner's region. Project allocation therefore changes the regional incidence of collaboration value and, once downstream rivalry is attached, can also change rival-firm and consumer outcomes. No hub fee or project-attraction rent is required.

### RQ7 — Would the architecture remain meaningful without downstream product-market competition?

**YES.** Endogenous intermediary choice, project stealing, referral and split collaboration incidence remain meaningful without downstream rivalry. Downstream competition is retained because the research question asks whether post-match rivalry changes the public provision/referral wedge; it is not needed to manufacture Hub competition.

### RQ8 — Is the architecture already completely absorbed by competing-matchmaker theory?

**NOT YET SHOWN TO BE COMPLETELY ABSORBED, BUT THE RISK IS HIGH.** Standard matchmaker theory strongly absorbs endogenous intermediary choice and heterogeneous matching. What remains to be hard-killed is the combination of region-anchored beneficiary firms that do not relocate, partner-side regional incidence, decentralized public provision, cross-hub referral and downstream strategic consequences. Stage 3M-R makes no novelty claim; failure of this distinction in Stage 4M-R requires NO-GO.

## Final referee test

Referee objection: “This is simply standard competition between two matching platforms. Calling the intermediaries public co-creation hubs adds no economics.”

Response without institutional labels: two jurisdictions finance binary matching infrastructures; a project remains attached to an immobile producer in its home jurisdiction but chooses which intermediary searches for a complementary partner; the intermediary choice can change the jurisdiction in which the partner-side part of collaboration surplus is realized; unsuccessful search can be routed to the other intermediary's non-overlapping pool; and the strengthened producer later competes with the other jurisdiction's producer. Each public principal internalizes only resident-agent welfare rather than intermediary profit or total match surplus. Standard private-platform pricing models absorb the intermediary-choice subgame, but do not by themselves establish the resulting decentralized regional incidence and policy wedge. Whether that distinction survives formal specialization is exactly the Stage 4M-R kill test.

## Ten-question minimality check

1. Projects allocated between hubs? **YES**.
2. Rival opening can reduce focal hub project share? **YES**, for overlapping feasible projects.
3. Overlap comes from productive matching opportunities? **YES**.
4. Referral comes from partner complementarity? **YES**, via non-overlapping pools.
5. Competition and cooperation share one structure? **YES**, `S_1,S_2`.
6. Allocation matters for regional government incentives? **YES**, through split agent incidence.
7. Downstream competition attaches without changing hub-choice logic? **YES**.
8. Private pricing avoided? **YES**.
9. Generic network utility avoided? **YES**.
10. Solvable in a subsequent minimal hard kill? **YES**, subject to Stage 4M-R choosing a finite tractable specialization of the frozen sets/fit process.

## Verdict

**MINIMALITY PASS.**
