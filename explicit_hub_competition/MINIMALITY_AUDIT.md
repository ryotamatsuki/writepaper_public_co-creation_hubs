# Stage 3M-R Minimality Audit

## Proposed primitives and why each is necessary

### 1. Two regional governments with binary hub provision

Necessary for decentralized public policy competition. Continuous quality is not needed at this stage.

### 2. Region-specific beneficiary firms/projects

Necessary to preserve downstream regional incidence while allowing a project to use either hub without firm relocation.

### 3. Partner pools `S_1,S_2`

Necessary to give hub differentiation productive-matching content and to derive both overlap and referral opportunities from one structure.

### 4. Heterogeneous project need/type `theta`

Necessary because without heterogeneity one hub would weakly dominate or projects would tie. Heterogeneity is economic compatibility heterogeneity, not arbitrary horizontal taste.

### 5. Productive fit `v(theta,s)`

Necessary to distinguish productive collaboration from mere contact. No specific functional form is frozen.

### 6. Endogenous first-hub choice

Necessary to create literal hub competition for intermediation demand.

### 7. Referral after failed direct matching

Necessary to represent cooperation using the rival's non-overlapping partner capabilities. It is the only authorized cooperative routing margin.

### 8. Split regional incidence of collaboration surplus

Necessary to make project allocation economically meaningful to public principals without hub fees or firm relocation. The beneficiary-firm component accrues where the firm resides; the complementary-partner component accrues where the partner resides.

### 9. Downstream strategic interaction

Retained from the research question but not used to create Hub choice. Stage 4M-R must hard-kill whether it materially changes hub competition or only welfare magnitudes.

## Explicitly unnecessary / excluded

- Hotelling distance;
- logit taste shocks;
- private hub prices;
- multihoming;
- capacity/congestion;
- hub quality investment;
- R&D spillovers;
- absorptive capacity;
- generic network utility;
- arbitrary overlap/referral payoff terms;
- endogenous firm location.

## Ten-question minimality check

1. Projects allocated between hubs? **YES**.
2. Rival opening can reduce focal hub project share? **YES**, for overlapping feasible projects.
3. Overlap comes from productive matching opportunities? **YES**.
4. Referral comes from partner complementarity? **YES**, via non-overlapping pools.
5. Competition and cooperation share one structure? **YES**, `S_1,S_2`.
6. Allocation matters for regional government incentives? **YES**, through split agent incidence.
7. Downstream competition attaches without changing hub-choice logic? **YES**.
8. Private pricing avoided? **YES**.
9. Generic network utility avoided? **YES**.
10. Solvable in a subsequent minimal hard kill? **YES**, subject to Stage 4M-R choosing a finite tractable specialization of the frozen sets/fit process.

## Verdict

**MINIMALITY PASS.**
