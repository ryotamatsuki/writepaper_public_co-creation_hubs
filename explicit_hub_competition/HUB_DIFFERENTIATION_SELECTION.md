# Hub Differentiation Selection

## D1 — Partner-Set Differentiation

**SELECTED.** Hub `i` has access to a region-anchored pool `S_i` of heterogeneous complementary partners/resources. Projects differ in what capability they need and in fit with available partners. Expected match value therefore differs across hubs because `S_1` and `S_2` differ in composition.

This is matching-content differentiation, not horizontal taste differentiation.

## D2 — Match-Quality Differentiation

Deferred as a separate primitive. Any hub-specific expected quality difference used in the baseline should be derived from the composition of `S_i`, not imposed directly as a quality parameter.

## D3 — Information/Specialization Differentiation

Subsumed under D1 where specialization means different partner capabilities are represented in `S_i`.

## D4 — Geographic/Hotelling Differentiation

Rejected for the baseline. Distance could guarantee interior shares, but it would make coexistence depend on an unrelated horizontal-differentiation primitive rather than productive matching.

## Minimal representation

For project need/type `theta`, let the productive value of pairing with partner `s` be `v(theta,s)`, with nonproductive contacts normalized to zero. The hub-specific expected direct match value is conceptually:

`V_i(theta) = E[max_{s in S_i} v(theta,s)]`.

No final distribution or functional form is frozen in Stage 3M-R.

## Why multiple hubs can coexist

Different project needs are best served by different partner-pool compositions. Coexistence follows from heterogeneity in productive fit, not from an artificial Hotelling term, logit noise, or exogenous 50–50 splitting.
