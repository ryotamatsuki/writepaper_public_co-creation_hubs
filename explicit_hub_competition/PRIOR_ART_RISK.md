# Prior-Art Absorption Risk

## Status

**HIGH ABSORPTION RISK — NO NOVELTY CLAIM.**

Stage 3M-R asks only whether a coherent explicit-hub-competition architecture exists. It does not establish theorem-level novelty.

## Threat 1 — Damiano & Li (2008), Competing Matchmaking

Strongest threat to endogenous hub choice with heterogeneous productive fit. Competing matchmakers sort heterogeneous agents and match value depends on complementary quality.

Threat: user/project choice plus partner-fit differentiation may already be generic competing-matchmaker structure.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1162/JEEA.2008.6.4.789

## Threat 2 — Caillaud & Jullien (2001, 2003)

Competing intermediation service providers with endogenous platform choice, indirect network effects and multihoming.

Threat: explicit intermediation competition is mature theory; adding the label 'public hub' is not a contribution.

DOI: 10.2307/1593720

## Threat 3 — Gal-Or (2020), Market Segmentation on Dating Platforms

Competing matching websites serve heterogeneous users; compatibility and platform population determine matching value and endogenous segmentation.

Threat: partner-set/compatibility differentiation and coexistence of competing matching platforms have direct formal precedents.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijindorg.2019.102558

## Threat 4 — Regional public-input / infrastructure competition

Walz (1996) and subsequent regional-infrastructure models analyze decentralized governments providing local inputs/infrastructure when heterogeneous firms choose jurisdictions. Related work allows infrastructure spillovers and compares decentralized and cooperative provision.

Threat: the public-government layer plus endogenous user allocation may collapse to standard jurisdictional competition.

Relevant records:
- https://cepr.org/publications/dp1324
- https://doi.org/10.1016/j.regsciurbeco.2009.02.007

## Threat 5 — Banal-Estañol, Macho-Stadler & Pérez-Castrillo (2018)

University-industry collaboration is modeled as endogenous matching between heterogeneous firms and academics with complementarity.

Threat: productive collaboration among complementary economic agents is itself established matching theory.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2016.2680

## Additional institutional evidence relevant to architecture

Science-park choice evidence shows firms trade off university proximity, R&D facilities, accessibility, shared facilities and technological focus; the literature explicitly recognizes competition between science parks for firms. This supports economically meaningful intermediary/facility choice, but it is empirical rather than an absorption-proof theorem.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techfore.2022.121796

Business-incubator review evidence also recognizes that startups first choose which incubator to apply to and emphasizes fit, services and partner networks.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.3390/su17146255

## Structural distinction to hard-kill next

The candidate architecture is not firm-location competition. A beneficiary firm remains in its home region while the **collaboration link** and complementary partner are allocated through one of two public hubs.

A single successful collaboration may therefore have split geographic incidence:

- beneficiary-firm capability/profit accrues in the firm's home region;
- complementary-partner surplus accrues in the partner's region.

The public principal of each region internalizes different components of the same match surplus. Cross-hub referral can improve a home firm's match while exporting partner-side surplus, and downstream rivalry can create further cross-region effects.

Stage 4M-R must test whether this split-incidence structure creates a proposition that cannot be obtained by relabeling a standard competing-matchmaker model or a standard public-input/location model. If not, the branch must be killed.
