## I3 targeted resolution

A dedicated final evidence round examined E:N BASE/Ehime, NEXs Tokyo, Japanese regional startup ecosystems, startup-program participation constraints and international evidence on local/external collaboration.

Final I3 classification:

**I3 PLAUSIBLE BUT INDIRECT**.

Evidence supporting a generic diversion margin:

- JETRO's 2026 J-StarX materials explicitly warn that participation in multiple acceleration programs can disperse valuable startup time and resources.
- J-StarX Silicon Valley Extended Program requires at least 50% of one executive's time.
- some local accelerator programs prohibit concurrent accelerator participation;
- peer-reviewed evidence (Aarstad, Kvitastein & Jakobsen, 2016) finds regional and international innovation collaboration can substitute for medium-sized firms.

However, no primary evidence demonstrates that E:N BASE participation itself reduces direct participation in NEXs Tokyo or another common Tokyo/national ecosystem. The strongest Ehime source points the other way institutionally: its 2026 metropolitan co-creation project explicitly aims to strengthen networks between E:N BASE members and metropolitan firms and create mutual collaboration. NEXs Tokyo likewise positions its DIVE program as helping regional startups use the metropolitan ecosystem.

The E:N BASE-specific counterfactual — absent the regional hub, would marginal users participate more directly in the common core? — therefore remains **UNKNOWN** rather than evidenced.
