# Stage 7 — Hostile Referee Simulation

## Attack 1 — “The theorem is technically distinct but has no welfare content.”

**Severity: MAJOR BUT ANSWERABLE.**

Response: Stage 7 derives a nontrivial welfare identity,

\[
G_1-G_0=2\Gamma,
\]

so the private strategic threshold is exactly the threshold at which social marginal entry benefits change from decreasing to increasing. In the coordination region,

\[
\bar G=(G_0+G_1)/2=D_0+X_1<D_0<D_1,
\]

which implies that full-entry coordination can be socially excessive and that any coordination-multiplicity region ranks the no-entry equilibrium above full entry. This is more than transfer accounting.

Caution: excessive entry itself is standard and must remain a corollary, not a novelty claim.

## Attack 2 — “The regional-hub application does not satisfy the shared-residual-core assumption.”

**Severity: MAJOR AND ONLY PARTLY ANSWERABLE.**

Response: NEXs Tokyo and the Cabinet Office ecosystem architecture support the existence of shared metropolitan/national network resources used by regional startups. However, direct evidence that E:N BASE or another regional hub thins participation in that core is absent. The official policy architecture emphasizes linkage and mutual collaboration.

Required manuscript consequence if the project later freezes: co-creation hubs cannot be presented as the theorem's literal established empirical case. They should be a qualified possible application, or the main application should be replaced by a stronger environment such as retail agglomeration or hub airports.

## Attack 3 — “Regional and metropolitan hubs are complements, not substitutes, so user diversion is unrealistic.”

**Severity: MAJOR.**

The primary-source evidence makes this a serious objection. Ehime explicitly seeks stronger E:N BASE–metropolitan networks, and NEXs Tokyo helps regional startups use Tokyo resources.

A limited response is possible: institutional complementarity does not rule out partial substitution in scarce participant time, attention, local event attendance or direct central participation. But Stage 7 found no direct crowd-out estimate.

Therefore the project must not rely on the regional-hub application to establish the mechanism. This attack is not fatal to the general theorem because retail and airport environments provide stronger diversion mappings.

## Attack 4 — “The result only applies to platforms and is not general.”

**Severity: MAJOR BUT ANSWERABLE.**

Retail agglomerations and airport hubs provide economically distinct mappings without changing the theoretical structure:

- peripheral malls divert shoppers from a downtown agglomeration whose attractiveness rises with variety/footfall;
- regional airport bypass diverts traffic from a common hub whose connectivity value rises with concentrated traffic and connection opportunities.

These are not merely public-platform relabelings.

## Attack 5 — “The result is general only because you relabel the same platform structure.”

**Severity: MAJOR BUT ANSWERABLE.**

The generality claim should be restricted to environments satisfying the six residual-core conditions. It is not a universal entry theorem. Retail and airport examples have separate institutional microfoundations—agglomeration/footfall and transport connectivity—yet generate the same reduced-form causal chain.

Generality is therefore structural, not semantic, but it is deliberately narrow.

## Attack 6 — “The policy implication is just standard excessive entry.”

**Severity: MINOR TO MAJOR, depending on exposition.**

Concede that excessive entry itself is standard. The useful welfare result is the linkage between the residual-core strategic threshold and the ordering of social marginal entry benefits, plus the sharp ranking of coordination equilibria. Do not market over-entry as a separate contribution.

## Attack 7 — “Single-homing drives the result.”

**Severity: MAJOR UNRESOLVED SCOPE LIMITATION.**

No Stage-7 theory extension is authorized. Existing robustness checks cover network curvature, asymmetry and cost distributions, not multi-homing. For co-creation hubs and labor search, multi-channel participation is institutionally common, which weakens literal application fit.

For retail shopping occasions and airport route/airport choice, an episode-level single-homing interpretation is more defensible.

This issue must be carried to Stage 7.5 as a scope question. It is not currently evidence that the theorem fails, but no robustness claim to multi-homing is permitted.

## Attack 8 — “Why should an economist care if the main public-hub application is weak?”

**Severity: MAJOR BUT ANSWERABLE.**

The economic object is decentralized entry around a network-dependent residual core, not public hubs. The mechanism changes whether peripheral entry is self-limiting or self-reinforcing and changes both equilibrium multiplicity and the ordering of social marginal entry benefits. Retail and transport provide independent economic settings where the mechanism is institutionally recognizable.

If Stage 7.5 concludes that an IO paper needs a stronger headline application, the project should pivot its motivation rather than alter the theory.

## Referee summary

No welfare or generality attack is fatal. The two serious constraints are:

1. the original co-creation-hub application is weak on the theorem-essential diversion margin;
2. multi-homing robustness is untested and cannot be claimed.

These are appropriate Stage-7.5 freeze questions. They do not authorize model expansion at Stage 7.
