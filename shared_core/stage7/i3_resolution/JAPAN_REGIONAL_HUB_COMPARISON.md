# Japan Regional Hub Comparison

The comparison asks whether regional startup/co-creation infrastructure is institutionally organized as a substitute for a common metropolitan/national ecosystem or as a gateway/complement to it.

| Region / program | Primary-source pattern | Classification |
|---|---|---|
| Ehime / E:N BASE | Local networking and project formation are paired with a 2026 metropolitan co-creation project explicitly designed to strengthen networks between E:N BASE members and metropolitan firms and create mutual collaboration. | **COMPLEMENT / GATEWAY** |
| Hokkaido / J-Startup HOKKAIDO | Regional ecosystem formation is combined with Tokyo meetups and exchanges with metropolitan firms/supporters; selected startups also connect into national J-Startup. | **COMPLEMENT / GATEWAY** |
| Sendai / research-development startup support | Six-month local hands-on support culminates in Demo Days, including a Tokyo event explicitly designed to create matching opportunities with metropolitan supporters. | **COMPLEMENT** |
| Osaka/Kansai startup ecosystem | Strong local accelerator, mentoring and investor functions coexist with Tokyo-Kansai matching pitches, Tokyo deep-tech meetups and international matching. | **MIXED, PREDOMINANTLY COMPLEMENT** |
| Hiroshima / J-Startup WEST | Regional support includes mentor/investor functions, but the architecture explicitly links regional firms horizontally and connects them with other-region investors, national supporters and Tokyo events. | **COMPLEMENT / GATEWAY** |
| Kagawa startup ecosystem | Local startup support is supplemented by Tokyo Innovation Base pitches intended to connect Kagawa startups with metropolitan corporations and venture capital. | **COMPLEMENT / GATEWAY** |
| Fukuoka startup ecosystem | Strong local startup support is paired with inter-municipal procurement collaboration led with Tokyo and use of metropolitan public infrastructure as a market/access channel. | **COMPLEMENT** |

## Cross-case conclusion

No audited Japanese regional ecosystem provided direct evidence that creation/use of a regional hub reduces participation in a common metropolitan/national startup ecosystem. The dominant official architecture is network extension: local ecosystems develop firms and then connect them to metropolitan, national or global investors, corporations, programs and markets.

This does not logically rule out partial crowd-out of founder time or search effort, but it means the canonical residual-core depletion channel is not the stated institutional design of these regional programs.

## Evidence relevant to substitution

Japan-wide accelerator rules nevertheless show that startup-support participation can compete for scarce organizational capacity:

- JETRO's 2026 J-StarX documentation warns that purposeless participation in multiple acceleration programs has dispersed startups' valuable time and resources.
- J-StarX Silicon Valley Extended Program expects at least 50% of one executive's time over the program.
- Sagamihara's acceleration program has required participants not to join another accelerator during implementation.
- Other Japanese programs explicitly permit simultaneous participation, confirming that parallel-program choice is a real design margin rather than a purely theoretical possibility.

These facts support partial substitution as a generic support-program mechanism, but not E:N BASE-specific core depletion.
