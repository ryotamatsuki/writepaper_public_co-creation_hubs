## Executive blocker verdict

**I3 PLAUSIBLE BUT INDIRECT**

The institutional claim tested was whether regional innovation/co-creation-hub participation displaces, at a meaningful margin, direct participation, attention, search effort, or relationship formation in a shared metropolitan/national innovation ecosystem.

The evidence establishes two things. First, regional and central programs can perform materially overlapping functions and participation in intensive startup-support programs consumes scarce managerial time and organizational resources. JETRO explicitly warns in its 2026 J-StarX materials that purposeless participation in multiple acceleration programs has dispersed startups' valuable time and resources, and its Silicon Valley Extended Program expects at least 50% of one executive's time. Some local accelerator programs prohibit simultaneous participation. This makes partial diversion institutionally plausible in the broader startup-support environment.

Second, no primary evidence was found that E:N BASE itself reduces direct participation in Tokyo/NEXs or a national startup ecosystem. The strongest Ehime evidence runs in the opposite institutional direction: the prefecture's 2026 metropolitan co-creation project explicitly aims to strengthen networks between E:N BASE members and metropolitan firms and create mutual collaboration. NEXs Tokyo similarly describes regional startups as using the Tokyo ecosystem to accelerate growth.

Accordingly, the blocker cannot be upgraded to SUPPORTED for the co-creation-hub application. The broader resource-diversion margin is real, but the E:N BASE-to-Tokyo counterfactual is not evidenced.
