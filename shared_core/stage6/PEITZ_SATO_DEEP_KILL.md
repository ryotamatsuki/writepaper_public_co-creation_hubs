# Peitz & Sato (2026) — Deep Kill

Paper: Martin Peitz and Susumu Sato, **Asymmetric Platform Oligopoly**, *RAND Journal of Economics* 57(1), 60–77. DOI: `10.1111/1756-2171.70008`.

## Why this is the strongest platform-theory threat

The paper already combines:

- asymmetric platforms;
- two user groups;
- endogenous single-homing participation;
- within- and cross-group network effects;
- participation fees;
- exogenous platform entry;
- free entry of a symmetric fringe;
- changes in incumbent platform quality;
- compatibility and partial participation.

Therefore none of those ingredients is available as a contribution claim.

## Entry results

Their Proposition 7 states that there are pre-entry conditions and entrant types under which entry **increases incumbent platform profit**. This directly demonstrates that platform entry under network effects need not have the conventional sign.

Their free-entry section defines the number of active entrants `M_E` through a post-entry zero-profit condition. Entrants sequentially enter while post-entry profit exceeds the fixed entry cost. Proposition 8 studies how a shock to incumbent characteristics changes free-entry outcomes and user-group surplus.

These results make it untenable to claim that the candidate is novel merely because incumbent strength or network effects alter entry outcomes.

## Why the hardened Proposition 2 is not a short specialization

The free-entry entrants in Peitz–Sato are an anonymous symmetric fringe. Their core analytical objects are aggregate action variables, platform market shares, fees, and entrant profit under a given active-platform count. The model does not define two named binary entrants `E_1,E_2` whose cross-entry effect is

`Gamma = [payoff_i(E_j=1)-payoff_i(E_j=0)]`.

More importantly, the network effects attach to users on the platforms themselves (within and across user groups). There is no separate residual incumbent/core whose **remaining** user mass is jointly reduced by entry from two peripheral alternatives and whose network-dependent utility serves as the common outside option of those entrants.

To obtain the candidate Proposition 2 from Peitz–Sato, a referee would need to:

1. replace the anonymous free-entry fringe with two named binary entrants;
2. define the entrant-to-entrant cross-entry payoff rather than entrant zero profit;
3. create a distinct residual incumbent option shared by the two entrants' user populations;
4. make the value of that residual option depend on the user mass left after both entrants draw users away;
5. separately shut down core-side and entrant-side network feedback;
6. prove that entrant-side feedback alone leaves the bilateral cross-entry effect negative in the candidate class while core-side feedback admits a sign reversal.

Steps 3–6 change the strategic interaction that produces the theorem; they are not parameter substitutions or notation changes.

## Explicit less-than-one-page test

**Question:** Could an informed referee derive the hardened Proposition 2 from Peitz–Sato in less than one page?

**Answer:** **NO.**

The paper is a very high structural threat and must be treated as the closest platform benchmark, but the residual-core/shared-outside-option network-location theorem is not an immediate corollary of their free-entry condition or their entry propositions.

## What Peitz–Sato does kill

The candidate must concede all of the following as prior art:

- asymmetric platform competition;
- endogenous participation;
- network effects and entry;
- incumbent quality shocks under free entry;
- network effects that make entry benefit an incumbent;
- compatibility as a determinant of platform competition.

## What survives after stripping labels

The only defensible distinction is the bilateral residual-core object:

> entrant `j` changes entrant `i`'s incentive because `j` diverts users from a **third residual network**, changing the value of the common outside option faced by `i`'s users; and the sign result depends on whether feedback is located at that residual core rather than only at entrants.

## Stage-6 disposition

`STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE / VERY HIGH RISK`, but **not exact prior art and not an immediate specialization**.
