## 2. Exact propositions re-killed

### Proposition 1 — Core-induced strategic reversal

In the frozen canonical model,

`u_M = A_M + beta n_M`,

`u_i(c)=A_L-c`,

`u_j(c)=A_L-c-tau`,

and `Gamma(A_M)=D1(A_M)-D0(A_M)` satisfies

`Gamma(A_M) = [Omega^2-(A_L-A_M-mu)^2] / [2(1-2 beta)^2]`,

with

`mu=tau+4 beta(1-beta)`

and

`Omega^2=2 beta[2 beta(1-2 beta)^2+(1-beta)tau^2]`.

A nonempty open parameter set admits a unique interior negative-to-positive crossing in `Gamma` as core attractiveness rises.

### Proposition 2 — Network-location result

In the diagnostic two-beta model,

`u_M=A_M+beta_M n_M`,

`u_i=A_L+beta_L n_i-c`,

- if `beta_M=0` and `0<beta_L<1/2`, `Gamma<0` throughout the canonical interior domain;
- if `beta_M>0` and `beta_L=0`, a nonempty open parameter set supports a negative-to-positive crossing.

The candidate contribution is therefore not `network effects matter`, but the narrower result that feedback through a shared residual core can generate bilateral strategic complementarity that entrant-side feedback alone does not generate in the canonical class.

### Proposition 3 — Game-form implication

The `Gamma` sign change changes intermediate-fixed-cost best responses from anti-coordination (`(1,0),(0,1)`) to coordination (`(0,0),(1,1)`). Stage 6 classifies this as an equilibrium implication/corollary of Proposition 1 rather than a separate novelty claim.

## 3. Strongest proposition-level prior art

### 3.1 Amir, Evstigneev & Gama (2021) — strongest new threat to the broad claim

Their *Economic Theory* paper compares oligopoly with proprietary firm-specific networks against a compatible single industry network. Proposition 3 and the surrounding text establish that, from a monopoly benchmark, increasing the number of firms increases viability in the single-network model but decreases viability in the firm-specific-network model.

This is decisive prior art for the broad statement that **network architecture/location can reverse how additional firms affect market outcomes**.

It is not the candidate theorem. In their compatible model, firms jointly contribute to and benefit from the same industry-wide network: additional firms expand the common network. In the candidate, a peripheral entrant removes users from a separate residual incumbent core; the incumbent core's network value therefore falls, weakening a shared outside option faced by the other peripheral entrant. Their theorem compares industry viability as `n` changes and does not define a named entrant-to-entrant cross-entry payoff.

**Disposition:** broad network-location novelty killed; residual-core bilateral theorem survives.

### 3.2 Peitz & Sato (2026) — strongest platform-theory threat

Their model contains asymmetric platforms, endogenous user choices, within/cross-group network effects, exogenous platform entry, free entry, incumbent-quality shocks, compatibility and partial participation. Proposition 7 explicitly allows entry to increase incumbent profit, and the free-entry section endogenizes a symmetric fringe entrant count through zero profit.

The model nevertheless lacks the Stage-6 strategic object:

- entrants are an anonymous symmetric fringe in free entry rather than two named binary entrants;
- each platform's network benefit is generated by participation on that platform/group, rather than by two peripheral entrants sharing the residual network size of a separate incumbent outside option;
- there is no theorem comparing core-only versus entrant-only network feedback for the sign of a bilateral entry cross-effect.

**Less-than-one-page test:** **NO.** Obtaining Proposition 2 requires changing the entrant structure, constructing a bilateral cross-entry payoff, imposing a residual common incumbent outside option, separating its network feedback from entrant-side feedback, and proving the location necessity result. Those are not parameter substitutions into their free-entry condition.

### 3.3 Tan & Zhou (2021)

Tan–Zhou establishes that externalities can reverse standard competition effects in multi-sided markets and that free entry may be socially excessive or insufficient. This is binding prior art against any claim that `network effects can overturn conventional entry logic`.

Their endogenous margin is the symmetric number of competing platforms. The audited model does not contain a separate residual incumbent whose installed base is the shared outside option of two named entrants, nor a core-only-versus-entrant-only network-location necessity theorem.

**Less-than-one-page test:** **NO.** A named bilateral `Gamma` requires substantive restructuring rather than specialization.

### 3.4 Liu, Rivadeneyra & Reshidi (2026)

Public-platform introduction can divert participants from a private platform and reduce that platform's network benefits. This is close to the core-fragmentation channel. But the model intentionally contains a single public and a single private platform, so it does not define the bilateral public/peripheral entry cross-effect required for Proposition 2. The authors explicitly leave multiple-platform public/private configurations to future research.

### 3.5 Economides (1996) and installed-base literature

A strong common network can make an incumbent invite or subsidize competitor entry. Church–Gandal and related installed-base models show that an incumbent's installed base can materially change entry and standardization. These papers establish that installed bases can invert ordinary incumbent/entrant incentives. They do not compare the effect of entrant `j` on entrant `i` when both compete against a separate residual core, and they do not establish entrant-side-network insufficiency versus core-side-network sufficiency.

## 4. Peitz–Sato deep-kill answer

**Can an informed referee derive Proposition 2 from Peitz–Sato in less than one page? — NO.**

A candidate derivation would require at least:

1. replacing their symmetric fringe free-entry count with two named binary entrants;
2. defining entrant-specific payoff changes conditional on the other entrant's presence;
3. replacing platform-specific network benefits with a third residual incumbent/core whose participation is jointly displaced by both entrants;
4. separately switching off network feedback at the core and at entrants;
5. proving the sign restriction in the local-network-only case and the open-set crossing in the core-network-only case.

Steps 2–5 are economically substantive to the candidate theorem, not notation changes.

---

## 5. Tan–Zhou deep-kill answer

**Can Proposition 2 be obtained from Tan–Zhou by a short specialization? — NO.**

Their result already absorbs generic reversal of entry effects due to externalities. The candidate survives only because its sign comparison is between two specific locations of feedback around a residual third option. No corresponding `beta_core=0` versus `beta_entrant=0` bilateral theorem was found in the published result set or the closest descendants inspected here.

## 6. Installed-base / network-location kill

The installed-base and compatibility literature substantially narrows the available novelty claim:

- Economides (1996): network effects can make an incumbent welcome entry;
- Church & Gandal (1996): installed complementary products can deter entry and affect standardization;
- Amir & Lazzati (2011): entry improves viability in a single compatible network under their conditions;
- Amir–Evstigneev–Gama (2021): entry can have opposite viability effects under single versus firm-specific networks;
- Gama (2019), Gama et al. (2020), Gama–Samano (2021), Basak (2021): compatibility/network structure affects free entry and welfare;
- Feng–Hu (2024), Campbell–Ushchev–Zenou (2024), Zhang (2026): current models show network structure can generate phase changes in entry/competition.

**Kill result:** the paper cannot claim a general theorem that network-effect location or compatibility changes entry. That family is mature.

**Survival result:** none of the inspected papers contains the candidate configuration in which entry by one peripheral option **shrinks** a distinct incumbent network that acts as the other entrant's shared outside option, together with the core-only/local-only necessity comparison.

---

## 7. Generic shared-outside-option kill

The candidate mechanism is also stripped of platform labels. Morales & Rodero-Cosano (2023) explicitly studies market entry with an endogenous outside option and shows that the size of that option affects coordination/pricing. This demonstrates that `endogenous outside options affect entry games` is not novel.

But their outside option is a rent-seeking activity whose payoff is linked to market profits. It is not a shared third option whose value is endogenously reduced by the combined allocation of users away from it. The two entrants do not modify a common residual installed base.

No exact general theorem was identified in which one entrant's entry endogenously weakens a **shared residual outside option** and thereby reverses the bilateral entry externality, with a corresponding no-reversal result when network feedback is confined to the entrants.

---

## 8. Common-rival kill

The common-enemy literature explicitly shows that an external threat can raise cooperation and can change the underlying reduced-form game (including transitions to Stag Hunt structures). Therefore `a stronger common rival induces coordination` is not a defensible contribution statement.

The candidate differs because no direct defense, coalition, common-threat payoff, or reduced-form complementarity is introduced. The only cross-entry channel is user reallocation: entrant `j` reduces residual core participation; a smaller residual network lowers the core's value; that changes the outside option faced by entrant `i`'s users.

**Verdict:** common-rival language is application-level intuition only. The narrow residual-network channel is not absorbed by the common-enemy models inspected.

---

## 9. Network-location artifact test

The exact `beta_M=0 => Gamma<0` necessity statement is scoped to the canonical class. Stage 6 does not promote it to an unrestricted theorem for arbitrary demand/network functions.

Authorized nearby checks from the hardening stage do not overturn the mechanism: the reversal survived concave core network effects, small regional asymmetry and nearby nonuniform participation-cost distributions. These checks support local robustness of Proposition 1. They do not justify claiming a global necessity theorem outside the canonical/two-beta class.

This scope restriction avoids turning a model-class result into an unsupported universal claim.

## 12. Surviving contribution

> In a bilateral entry game against a residual incumbent network that functions as a shared endogenous outside option, entry by one peripheral option can weaken the outside option faced by the other through user diversion from the core. In the canonical class this permits a core-side network feedback to reverse the bilateral entry externality, while entrant-side network feedback alone leaves entry strategically substitutable.

No public, regional, metropolitan or co-creation labels are needed for this statement.

## 14. Referee simulation

### Attack 1 — `This is just Peitz–Sato with two entrants.`

**MAJOR BUT ANSWERABLE.** Their framework is exceptionally close at the platform-entry level, but the free-entry object and network structure do not contain a shared residual incumbent outside option or the location-necessity theorem.

### Attack 2 — `Tan–Zhou already shows network effects reverse entry effects.`

**MAJOR BUT ANSWERABLE.** Concede the generic reversal. The contribution must be phrased as a location/strategic-object result about residual-core feedback, not generic reversal.

### Attack 3 — `The location result is obvious once you write an endogenous outside option.`

**MAJOR BUT ANSWERABLE, highest residual risk.** Endogenous outside-option entry models exist, but the targeted search found no bilateral residual-network necessity theorem. Stage 7 should test generality and economic content rather than add primitives.

### Attack 4 — `The common-rival mechanism is old.`

**MAJOR BUT ANSWERABLE.** Concede common-enemy effects; avoid this headline. The candidate has no direct common-enemy payoff and works through endogenous residual network value.

### Attack 5 — `Public and regional interpretations do no theoretical work.`

**MINOR / CORRECT.** Treat them as application. Do not claim ownership novelty.

### Attack 6 — `Amir–Evstigneev–Gama already proves network location changes entry.`

**MAJOR BUT ANSWERABLE.** This attack kills the broad statement, which is removed. Their common network is jointly enlarged by firms; the candidate's residual core is shrunk by entrant diversion, and their object is industry viability rather than bilateral entry cross-payoff.
