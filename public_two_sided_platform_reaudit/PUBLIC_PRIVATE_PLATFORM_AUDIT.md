# Public–Private Two-Sided Platform Audit

## Kim (2024)

Jeong-Yoo Kim, “Mixed duopoly in two-sided markets,” The Manchester School 92(3), 281–295, DOI `10.1111/manc.12467`.

Core architecture:

- one welfare-maximizing public platform;
- one profit-maximizing private platform;
- two-sided network effects;
- linear-city differentiation;
- full coverage in the baseline;
- platform price competition;
- an investment/quality stage.

Kim shows that the private platform's prices under mixed competition can be above or below the corresponding private-duopoly prices depending on public-platform quality, and that the private platform invests more than the public platform in the investment extension.

Classification: **STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE for B1**, but not the candidate G. It contains neither a second regional public platform nor separate regional welfare objectives, and its baseline suppresses non-participation.

## Liu, Reshidi & Rivadeneyra (2026)

“Public vs. Private Payment Platforms: Market Impacts and Optimal Policy,” Bank of Canada Staff Working Paper 2026-10, DOI `10.34989/swp-2026-10`.

This is the strongest structural prior located.

It contains:

- one welfare-maximizing public platform;
- one profit-maximizing private platform;
- a two-sided payment market;
- endogenous consumer/merchant participation;
- heterogeneous platform preferences;
- network fragmentation;
- private fee responses to public competition;
- public pricing constrained by the fact that its action alters the rival network.

The paper explicitly distinguishes an extensive participation margin from switching/diversion toward the public platform. It therefore already contains the core economic trade-off behind generic P3: public competition can expand participation while inefficiently diverting participants from a rival and eroding that rival's network benefits.

Crucially, the paper states in its discussion that it considers only two platforms and identifies analysis of multiple platforms—including coexistence of multiple public platforms alongside private ones—as future research. Thus it does not itself solve the candidate three-platform decentralized-public game.

Classification: **STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE; exact B1 threat; not exact whole-game prior art**.

## Abe & Zennyo (2023)

“Welfare Effects of Socially Conscious Platforms in Two-Sided Markets,” B.E. Journal of Economic Analysis & Policy 23(1), 243–251, DOI `10.1515/bejeap-2022-0379`.

One competing platform internalizes consumer surplus in addition to profit. Stronger indirect network externalities can make the socially conscious platform welfare-improving whereas weak network effects can reverse this.

Classification: **STRUCTURALLY CLOSE COMPONENT** and a warning that welfare sign changes with network strength are not novel by themselves.

## Bottom line

B1 is heavily occupied. Any Stage 3 contribution must require the second regional public platform and/or regional incidence, not merely public/private platform competition.
