# Candidate Full Game

## Strategic actors

- Regional government `G_1` controls public platform `H_1` and maximizes own regional welfare `W_1`.
- Regional government `G_2` controls public platform `H_2` and maximizes own regional welfare `W_2`.
- National/metropolitan private platform `H_T` maximizes profit `Pi_T`.

Candidate strategic controls are

`G_i: x_i`, for `i=1,2`,

and

`H_T: p_T`.

The exact economic interpretation of `x_i` is left to Stage 3. It must be a defensible platform-building instrument rather than a label for arbitrary quality.

## Two sides

For each platform `h in {1,2,T}` define endogenous masses

`n_h^F` = firm/project-side participation,

`n_h^P` = partner/support-side participation.

Participation generates positive cross-side externalities. Non-participation is available.

## Candidate timing

1. `G_1,G_2` choose `(x_1,x_2)`.
2. `H_T` observes public choices and chooses `p_T`.
3. Both sides make platform participation/homing choices.
4. Participation, platform surplus, regional welfare and private profit are realized.

This timing is not yet frozen; Stage 3 must compare it with simultaneous-pricing/investment alternatives found in the closest literature.

## Distinctive full-game feedback under audit

`x_1 -> n_1^F -> n_1^P -> n_1^F`,

combined with network fragmentation across `H_1,H_2,H_T`, and

`x_1 -> (n_T^F,n_T^P) -> p_T* -> (n_2^F,n_2^P) -> BR_2`.

The Stage 2 survival question is whether this full chain is already a solved corollary of prior theory.
