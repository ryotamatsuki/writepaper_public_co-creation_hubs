# Interregional Mixed Oligopoly Audit

## Inoue, Kamijo & Tomaru (2009)

“Interregional mixed duopoly,” Regional Science and Urban Economics 39(2), 233–242, DOI `10.1016/j.regsciurbeco.2008.10.001`.

Model features:

- a local public firm owned by Region 1;
- the public firm maximizes Region 1 welfare rather than national welfare;
- a private rival;
- spatial differentiation;
- location followed by price competition;
- the local public firm may serve consumers outside its own region.

This is a direct prior for the idea that local welfare incidence changes a public competitor's behavior. It kills any claim that “regional public objective” alone is new.

It has only one public firm and no two-sided participation/network structure.

Classification: **STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE COMPONENT**.

## Dadpay & Heywood (2006)

“Mixed Oligopoly in a Single International Market,” Australian Economic Papers 45(4), 269–280, DOI `10.1111/j.1467-8454.2006.00292.x`.

Two countries each possess public and private firms competing in one market. The public firms act in the interests of their respective countries, and the strategic interaction between the two public firms typically reduces welfare.

This is the strongest prior-art threat to any claim that decentralized public objectives create a novel welfare wedge by themselves.

Classification: **STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE to the public-public regional-objective layer, but one-sided and without a national private platform with endogenous two-sided pricing**.

## Implication

The candidate must use two-sided participation and private platform response to change the regional-public strategic game. Merely re-deriving welfare loss from competing parochial public entities would be an immediate literature extension and should be killed.
