# Local-Government / Public-Competition Network Audit

The search found substantial adjacent theory combining public/private mixed competition with network effects, although not the candidate full architecture.

Examples include Yasuhiko Nakamura's mixed-duopoly work on network effects, capacity choice and endogenous strategic variables. These models feature one welfare-maximizing public firm and one profit-maximizing private firm with direct network effects. They demonstrate that network strength can change equilibrium strategic choices and welfare rankings.

Belleflamme & Peitz (2010), “Platform competition and seller investment incentives,” European Economic Review 54(8), 1059–1076, DOI `10.1016/j.euroecorev.2010.03.001`, is an especially important P1 threat. In competing for-profit two-sided platforms, for-profit intermediation can produce overinvestment where free access produces underinvestment because investment changes indirect network effects and access prices.

Consequences for Stage 2:

- a sign change in investment distortion as network effects change is not automatically novel;
- public/private network competition is not automatically novel;
- the surviving P1 must be explicitly a **decentralized regional-public wedge** and must be unavailable in B1 and B2 separately.

Targeted searches did not locate a standard local-government model with the exact two regional public two-sided platforms plus national private price-response architecture. This absence remains provisional and must be re-killed after actual propositions are known.
