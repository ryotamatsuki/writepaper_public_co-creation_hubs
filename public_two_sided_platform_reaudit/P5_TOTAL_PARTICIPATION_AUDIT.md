# P5 — More Public Platforms Can Reduce Participation/Welfare

Candidate intuition: adding a zero-price public platform may split network participants sufficiently that total platform participation or welfare falls.

## Prior-art threat

The generic mechanism is established.

Tremblay, Adachi & Sato (2023) study N-platform two-sided competition and show that welfare can fall with the number of platforms because network fragmentation can outweigh competition-induced price benefits.

Ding, Ko & Shen (2022) uses a three-platform model to show that platform configuration/compatibility materially changes network welfare.

Liu et al. (2026) likewise notes that welfare effects of an additional platform are ambiguous under network effects and, absent differentiation, a single platform can be optimal.

The exact claim that **total participation** rather than welfare falls is not identified here as a canonical theorem, but it is too close to the established fragmentation mechanism to carry the paper absent a distinctive regional-public threshold.

## Classification

**LIKELY COROLLARY / KILL AS GENERIC HEADLINE.**
