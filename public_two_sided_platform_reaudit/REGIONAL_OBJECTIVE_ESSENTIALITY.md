# Regional Welfare Objective Essentiality

Regional welfare is economically plausible but not intrinsically novel.

Inoue, Kamijo & Tomaru (2009) directly models a local public firm maximizing only its own region's welfare while competing with a private firm.

Dadpay & Heywood (2006) goes further: public firms representing separate countries compete in a common market, and their strategic interaction can reduce welfare because each pursues its own national interest.

Therefore:

`regional objective -> strategic distortion`

is existing economics.

The regional objective can become essential to the present route only if it determines **which cross-side network benefits and fragmentation losses each government internalizes**, thereby changing public best responses and the effect of private pricing.

Stage 3 must build regional incidence from real participant surplus rather than add a reduced-form local-market-share payoff.

Stage 2 status: **ECONOMICALLY ESSENTIAL CANDIDATE; NOVELTY BY ITSELF KILLED**.
