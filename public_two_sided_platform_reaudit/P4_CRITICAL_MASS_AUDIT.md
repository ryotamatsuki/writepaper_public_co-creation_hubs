# P4 — Critical Mass / Coordination Audit

Cross-side externalities naturally generate coordination and critical-mass issues in platform theory.

Government-platform research also explicitly studies critical mass. Aslan et al. (2026) develops a public-administration two-sided platform model in which initial network size, capacity and within-group effects determine whether public platforms achieve sustainable growth.

Canonical platform theory also treats participation coordination and multiplicity under network effects.

Therefore a result such as

`x_i > x_i^crit -> high-participation equilibrium`

is not a contribution by itself.

A future result could become relevant only if **decentralized competing public platforms alter equilibrium selection in a way unavailable in a single-platform coordination model**, but that is not established at Stage 2.

## Classification

**KILL P4 as currently formulated.**
