# Government / Public Platform Audit

## Aslan, Nemeslaki, Somogyi et al. (2026)

“Dynamics of two-sided platforms in public administration,” Operational Research 26, article 12, DOI `10.1007/s12351-025-01001-4`.

The paper explicitly models public-administration platforms with two-sided-market logic. It emphasizes critical mass, cross-group externalities, within-group externalities, capacity constraints and dynamic growth.

Its baseline is a **monopoly public platform**, with exogenous membership and transaction fees and a managerial objective focused on public-platform participation/growth rather than the candidate decentralized regional-welfare competition problem.

Classification: **MERELY RELATED / INSTITUTIONALLY CLOSE** for the Stage 2 static game, but fatal to any claim that “government services as a two-sided platform” or public-platform critical mass is itself new.

## Broader government-platform literature

Much of the “government as platform,” digital public infrastructure and innovation-platform literature is conceptual, managerial or institutional rather than a formal strategic model. Such papers can later support institutional interpretation, but they are not substitutes for the formal whole-game novelty audit.

## Implication

P4—public platform as a critical-mass coordination device—is not a viable headline without a distinct decentralized-competition theorem.
