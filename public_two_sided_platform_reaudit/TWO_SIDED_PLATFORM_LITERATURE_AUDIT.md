# Canonical Two-Sided Platform Literature Audit

## Rochet & Tirole (2003)

“Platform Competition in Two-Sided Markets,” JEEA 1(4), 990–1029, DOI `10.1162/154247603322493212`.

Establishes platform competition with two-sided network externalities, price allocation across sides and different governance structures, including profit-maximizing platforms and not-for-profit joint undertakings, with planner comparisons.

Classification: **COMPONENT OVERLAP / FOUNDATIONAL PRIOR**. It kills any claim that cross-side participation or non-profit governance is new. It does not reproduce two regional welfare objectives plus a national private platform.

## Caillaud & Jullien (2003)

“Chicken & Egg: Competition among Intermediation Service Providers,” RAND Journal of Economics 34(2), 309–328, DOI `10.2307/1593720`.

Analyzes imperfect price competition between intermediaries with indirect network externalities, nonexclusive use/multihoming and discriminatory pricing.

Classification: **COMPONENT OVERLAP / FOUNDATIONAL PRIOR**.

## Armstrong (2006)

“Competition in Two-Sided Markets,” RAND Journal of Economics 37(3), 668–691, DOI `10.1111/j.1756-2171.2006.tb00037.x`.

Provides monopoly, competing-single-home and competitive-bottleneck models. Equilibrium pricing depends on cross-side externalities, tariff form and homing structure.

Classification: **COMPONENT OVERLAP / FOUNDATIONAL PRIOR**. Stage 3 cannot claim novelty from the cross-side multiplier, single-homing, competitive bottlenecks or standard platform differentiation.

## Hagiu (2006)

“Pricing and Commitment by Two-Sided Platforms,” RAND Journal of Economics 37(3), 720–737, DOI `10.1111/j.1756-2171.2006.tb00039.x`.

Studies commitment, fixed and variable fees, and competing-platform exclusive/multihoming outcomes.

Classification: **COMPONENT OVERLAP**.

## Weyl (2010)

“A Price Theory of Multi-Sided Platforms,” AER 100(4), 1642–1672, DOI `10.1257/aer.100.4.1642`.

Provides a general network-pricing framework with heterogeneous users and coordination considerations, mainly for a monopoly platform.

Classification: **COMPONENT OVERLAP**.

## Implication

The platform family is mature. A Stage 3 model survives only if decentralized regional public objectives and the national private response create a strategic object not reducible to ordinary platform pricing/investment theory.
