# Cooperation Mechanism

## Institutional finding

The cooperation side of the hub duality is not best described as an abstract utility-side network effect. The institutional evidence points more specifically to **cross-hub access to missing partners/resources and expansion of the effective matching pool**.

Examples:

- Aichi explicitly organizes mutual cooperation between STATION Ai and regional partner hubs to create a prefecture-wide startup ecosystem and co-creation projects.
- NEXs Tokyo connects regional startups/support organizations to Tokyo's dense human/information resources and nationwide actors.
- Setouchi-i-Base explicitly searches for matching partners and builds organic networks inside and outside the region.
- Forum Virium Helsinki builds bridges across city, business, research and citizens and forms cross-sector consortia.
- Feser's Berlin innovation-lab case finds peer-lab cooperation motivated by access to missing resources/specific knowledge, specialization, learning and collaborative projects.

## Canonical secondary mechanism candidate

**Shared matching-pool / cross-hub referral externality.**

Verbal definition:

> Provision/activity of hub j can increase the effective set or expected quality of complementary partners accessible through hub i, because hubs exchange referrals, connect distinct partner pools, or contribute specialized resources to a broader innovation ecosystem.

This is a positive cross-hub effect on the **matching technology**, not a direct addition to firm utility.

## Why it is not the direct primitive

A hub can provide productive matching even in isolation. Shared-pool/referral effects arise only when multiple hubs or networks interact. Therefore the direct primitive remains M2 productive matching, while this is the sole retained secondary cross-hub mechanism.

## Necessity status

**ESSENTIAL CANDIDATE FOR THE COOPERATION SIDE.**

If two otherwise identical hubs merely duplicate matching services over the same fixed partner pool, the natural cross-hub force is substitution. A positive cross-hub channel requires an institutionally grounded reason why one hub improves another's effective matching opportunities. Shared-pool/referral expansion is the cleanest documented candidate.

The next minimal-model stage must hard-kill this claim: if strategic complementarity can arise without this channel, M6 should be removed as redundant.

## Not retained

- generic network utility;
- automatic R&D spillover;
- absorptive-capacity spillover;
- exogenous synergy between hubs.

Those mechanisms are less directly supported as general hub functions and would increase ad-hoc risk.
