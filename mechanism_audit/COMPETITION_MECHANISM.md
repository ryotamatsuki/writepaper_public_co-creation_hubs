# Competition Mechanism

## Competition between hubs

The institutional evidence supports **overlapping intermediation / duplication** as the cleanest negative cross-hub force.

Two hubs may target overlapping sets of:

- firms/users;
- projects;
- complementary partners;
- experts;
- funding/attention;
- public resources.

When hub j already forms a match or provides access to a partner that hub i could have intermediated, the incremental value of hub i falls. This is **matching-opportunity cannibalization**.

Feser's Berlin labs provide direct qualitative evidence: increasing numbers of similar labs create competition, market-opacity/information costs and pressure to differentiate/specialize; horizontal cooperation is also constrained by unintended knowledge spillovers and competitive markets.

## Why this is not “business stealing” between hubs by assumption

The eventual baseline need not give hubs private revenues. The negative force can exist even for public/nonprofit hubs because duplicated intermediation has lower incremental regional value. A local government may therefore view another region's matching infrastructure as partially substituting for its own.

## Competition between beneficiary firms

Hub competition must be kept conceptually separate from product-market competition.

After a productive match improves a beneficiary firm's capability/productivity/innovation value, firms interact strategically in a downstream market. Improvement of rival firms can therefore reduce a focal firm's equilibrium profit even when collaboration creates real productive value.

The exact downstream conduct model is not frozen at Stage 0M–2M. Requirements for the next stage:

1. firm profits must be endogenous;
2. collaboration outcomes must enter actual competitive capability/cost/demand, not an additive reduced-form utility only;
3. downstream firm rivalry must remain separate from hub service overlap;
4. the model should begin with the simplest standard competition benchmark and test robustness only later.

The prior homogeneous-Cournot diagnostic satisfies these feasibility requirements but is not carried over mechanically.

## Canonical negative channels

1. **Hub-level substitution:** overlap/duplication of productive matching opportunities.
2. **Firm-level rivalry:** successful collaboration can strengthen a downstream competitor.

These channels are economically distinct and must not be collapsed into one parameter.
