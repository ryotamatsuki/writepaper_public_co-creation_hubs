# Canonical Primitive Freeze

## P1 — What does the hub directly change?

**Exactly one primary answer:**

> **The hub directly improves the technology for forming productive matches/collaborations among heterogeneous agents with complementary resources or capabilities.**

No final matching function is specified in this stage.

## P2 — What is “co-creation” economically?

> **A productive collaboration among complementary agents.**

“Productive” means the relationship creates economic value relative to remaining unmatched/uncollaborative. “Complementary” means match value depends on combining different resources/capabilities rather than merely increasing the number of participants.

## P3 — What is the baseline outcome of successful co-creation?

**Firm capability/productivity improvement**, at the verbal architecture level only.

This is chosen because it can encompass technology, knowledge, market access, expertise, complementary business assets and project-specific resources without prematurely restricting the study to formal R&D.

The next stage must choose the smallest formal mapping from productive match value to competitive capability.

## P4 — What can make hubs cooperate / become complements?

**Shared matching-pool / cross-hub referral externality.**

One hub can expand another hub's effective partner set or access to complementary specialized resources. This is the only secondary candidate frozen for testing.

## P5 — What can make hubs compete / become substitutes?

**Overlapping productive-matching opportunities / duplicated intermediation.**

Another hub may already serve the same firms, projects or complementary partners, reducing the focal hub's incremental social value.

## P6 — What creates competition between beneficiary firms?

**Downstream strategic product-market interaction after collaboration changes competitive capability.**

Exact Cournot/Bertrand structure is not frozen here.

## Broad timing freeze

1. decentralized regional/public principals choose/provide hub intermediation;
2. hubs affect productive collaboration formation among heterogeneous complementary agents;
3. successful collaboration changes beneficiary-firm capability/productivity;
4. beneficiary firms compete downstream and earn endogenous profits;
5. regional welfare depends on these outcomes and the cost of hub provision.

Multiple-hub interaction includes at most two cross-hub forces in the minimal candidate:

- positive: shared partner-pool/referral effect;
- negative: overlapping matching-service/cannibalization effect.

## Intended dependent strategic object

Whether decentralized public hub provision is a **strategic complement or strategic substitute** to provision by another jurisdiction.

The next stage must not assume that both signs exist.

## Canonical label-stripped research question

> **When do decentralized providers of productive-matching infrastructure become strategic complements or strategic substitutes when their intermediation opportunities overlap, their partner pools can be shared across providers, and matched beneficiary firms subsequently compete in a downstream market?**

## Frozen exclusions for next-stage entry

Do not begin the minimal model with:

- generic utility-side network effects;
- R&D spillovers;
- absorptive capacity;
- explicit joint-synergy terms beyond the basic fact that productive matches have complementary value;
- differentiated products;
- multiple cores;
- dynamics;
- political economy;
- private intermediary pricing.

Any such feature requires a diagnosed failure and separate authorization.
