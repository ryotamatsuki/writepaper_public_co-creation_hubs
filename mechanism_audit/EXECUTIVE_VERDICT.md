# Stage 0M–2M Executive Verdict

## Institutional definition

**Co-creation** is the formation of productive collaborations among heterogeneous agents whose resources, capabilities, knowledge, or market access are complementary. A **public co-creation hub / innovation intermediary** is not treated as a producer of innovation itself; its primary economic role is to improve the process by which such productive collaborations are formed.

The institutional sample consistently shows matching, connection, brokerage, community formation, and cross-sector/network access as recurring core functions. Experimentation, direct resource provision, knowledge transfer, mentoring, and commercialization appear frequently but are not universal enough to define the general hub.

## Best direct primitive

**M2 — Productive matching technology.**

Canonical verbal freeze:

> The hub directly improves the technology for forming productive matches/collaborations among heterogeneous agents with complementary resources or capabilities.

This is deliberately broader than a pure search-cost reduction but narrower and more observable than an undifferentiated “collaboration friction.” Search, screening/information, brokerage, translation, and coordination are treated as possible microfoundations of the matching technology rather than independent canonical primitives.

## Secondary mechanism

**M6 — shared matching-pool / cross-hub referral externality**, retained as the single secondary candidate required to represent the empirically observed cooperation side of hub coopetition.

Interpretation: one hub can enlarge the effective partner pool or refer users/projects to another hub, increasing the expected quality/probability of productive matching elsewhere. This is a matching externality, not a free-standing utility-side network effect.

## Competition mechanism

Hub competition is generated by **overlapping intermediation opportunities**: two hubs may serve the same firms, projects, partners, or partner pools, so provision by one can reduce the incremental matching value of the other. This is treated as service overlap/cannibalization, not as a separate direct primitive.

## Beneficiary-firm competition

After successful collaboration changes firm capability/productivity/innovation potential, beneficiary firms must interact strategically in a downstream product market. Exact Cournot/Bertrand functional form is **not frozen in this stage**. The prior homogeneous-Cournot model remains a diagnostic proof that explicit downstream competition can be closed, but not the canonical theory.

## Blue-ocean classification

**B2 — STRUCTURAL FORMAL-THEORY GAP.**

The search found mature formal theories for each major component separately: search intermediation, competing matchmakers, two-sided platform competition, innovation intermediation, TTO information/matching, and intermediaries serving downstream firms. The strongest generic absorption threat is Caillaud & Jullien (2003), which already formalizes competition among matchmakers with indirect network externalities and multihoming.

However, no screened model combines the full target architecture:

1. decentralized public/regional provision of multiple intermediaries;
2. productive matching among complementary agents;
3. cross-hub shared-pool/referral benefits and overlapping-service cannibalization;
4. downstream strategic competition among beneficiary firms; and
5. regional-welfare incentives for hub provision.

This is a structural gap, **not B3**: absence of an exact model in the documented search is not proof that none exists.

## Canonical research question

> **When do decentralized providers of productive-matching infrastructure become strategic complements or strategic substitutes when their intermediation opportunities overlap, their partner pools can be shared across providers, and matched beneficiary firms subsequently compete in a downstream market?**

## Anti-ad-hoc test

PASS. Even if the eventual minimal model produced only substitution and no strategic-complementarity region, the institutional evidence would still justify productive matching as the correct direct representation of a co-creation hub. The primitive is therefore not selected to manufacture a desired sign reversal.

## Routing

**GO TO MINIMAL FORMAL MODEL CONSTRUCTION.**

Next-stage discipline: start with the frozen productive-matching primitive; add only the single shared-pool/referral channel if it is shown necessary for the empirically motivated cooperation side. Do not reintroduce generic network effects, R&D spillovers, absorptive capacity, or joint-synergy terms without a separate necessity test.
