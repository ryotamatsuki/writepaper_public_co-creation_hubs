# Current Working-Paper Frontier

Search horizon: through September 2026, emphasizing 2020–2026 publisher pages, working-paper repositories, RePEc/IDEAS, central-bank papers, and current journal articles.

## Current/high-recency items that matter

1. **Liu, Reshidi & Rivadeneyra (2026), Bank of Canada SWP 2026-10.** Strongest current B1 public/private two-sided-platform threat. It establishes one-public/one-private competition and private fee responses, but explicitly does not solve the two-decentralized-public interaction needed for P2-R4.
2. **Sánchez-Cartas (2026), Economics Letters 265, 113019.** A current two-sided follower-response reversal: sequential pricing can invert a side-price-skewness ranking. Different strategic object; no first-stage public-investment BR reversal.
3. **Hahn & Kim (2026), *Price Parity Clauses and Platform Investments*, 26 Feb 2026 WP.** Two platforms choose investments/fees, then a seller chooses retail prices. Eqs. (4)–(13) make the downstream price-response channel explicit; pp.10–12 explain that price adjustment can dampen cross-platform investment effects. No P2-R4 theorem.
4. **He et al. (2026), IJPE 300, 110123.** Two-sided capacity-sharing platform pricing and service investment under mixed network externalities. Current platform investment/pricing frontier, but no public objectives or distinct third-price follower.
5. **Katsamakas & Sánchez-Cartas (2026), Computational Economics.** Competing platforms strategically invest under network effects/tipping. No follower-price benchmark comparison.
6. **Bontems, Hamilton & Lepore (2025; revised 2026), Economics Letters 255, 112543.** Sequential multisided pricing changes the operation of network effects, but has no investment stage.

## Frontier conclusion

Recent work materially strengthens the claim that (i) investment with network externalities, (ii) sequential/follower pricing, and (iii) price-mediated changes in platform investment incentives are already active theory frontiers. It does **not** supply the exact public-public best-response sign comparison `BR_i^{B3′}>0>BR_i^{G′}`.

Accordingly, Stage 6 cannot claim priority for the generic price-response channel. The only surviving current-frontier claim is the benchmark-to-full-game sign reversal.
