# Author-Neighborhood Audit

Author neighborhoods were reopened around the named Stage-6 priors and around newly surfaced adjacent threats.

## Platform / two-sided neighborhood

- **Sánchez-Cartas:** 2026 output includes *The Stackelberg–Armstrong model* and *Platform Competition and Market Tipping with Preference Alignment*, plus adjacent work on entry and multihoming/VAS. These show active work on sequencing, tipping, investment and network effects, but no located P2-R4 theorem.
- **Tremblay / Adachi / Sato:** current work develops N-platform, endogenous-homing and mixed-homing competition. Platform count/homing therefore cannot support novelty.
- **Armstrong / Hagiu / Weyl / Belleflamme / Peitz:** canonical and descendant work establishes two-sided pricing, homing, participation and competition; no located public-public investment sign-reversal theorem.
- **Liu / Reshidi / Rivadeneyra:** current public-payment-platform work remains one-public/one-private in the inspected version; its own multiple-platform discussion does not solve P2-R4.

## Investment / mixed-objective neighborhood

- **López / Vives:** their R&D/common-ownership work explicitly formalizes the strategic effect of later prices on earlier investments and therefore sharply narrows P2-R3.
- **Bourreau / Zogheib:** adjacent network-industry work directly treats national/local public firms against a private firm with investment and pricing; this is now the first architecture-neighborhood source to check if any future version expands the sign-comparison result.
- **Mun:** transport-infrastructure work repeatedly studies regional pricing/investment and joint provision, providing a natural non-platform neighborhood for decentralized-government/charge-response theory.

## Current working-paper neighborhood

Hahn & Kim (2026) was surfaced through a platform-investment/MFN search rather than the original named-author list. It is important because it places two platform investments before a downstream seller's price choice and explicitly discusses how seller-price adjustment changes investment effects. It still does not state P2-R4.

## Conclusion

Author-neighborhood search produces **stronger narrowing**, not absorption: P2-R1, R2 and R3 are plainly crowded; P2-R4 remains the only object not located as a theorem or immediate corollary.
