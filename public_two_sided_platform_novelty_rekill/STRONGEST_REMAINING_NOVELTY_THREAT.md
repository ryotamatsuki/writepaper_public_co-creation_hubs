# Strongest Remaining Novelty Threat

**Farhi & Hagiu (2008)** is the strongest structural threat. It explicitly imports Fudenberg–Tirole/BGK strategic-investment logic into two-sided markets and proves that downstream two-sided pricing can make one platform's cost-reducing investment strategically soft.

Why it does not presently kill P2-R4: only one first-stage investment is chosen; the downstream game is pricing by the two competing platforms; there is no second decentralized public investment, no distinct third private price setter, and no B3-vs-G public-public BR sign comparison.

Second threat: **Sánchez-Cartas (2026)**, which demonstrates a current two-sided follower-response reversal, but for side-price skewness rather than first-stage investment interaction.
