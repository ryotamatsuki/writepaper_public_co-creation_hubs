# Strongest Remaining Novelty Threat

## 1. Strongest mixed-objective architecture threat — Zogheib & Bourreau (2021)

*Public vs. Private Investments In Network Industries* is the most important newly elevated threat because it already combines a profit-maximizing private firm with national/local public firms, strategic prices, and infrastructure investment in a network industry. The associated thesis also contains an appendix in which the private firm leads in investment and separately treats local public firms.

Why it does not presently kill P2-R4:

- the accessible model/result descriptions do not state a fixed-private-price versus endogenous-private-price comparison of local-public investment best-response slopes;
- the model does not use a two-sided participation feedback to generate the positive fixed-price benchmark cross-effect;
- the HAL full theorem PDF was blocked during this audit, so no unobserved proposition is presumed either way.

Risk: if the inaccessible full text contains an explicit theorem that a private price/leadership response flips two local-public investment reactions from complements to substitutes, the Stage-6 verdict would need reopening. Current accessible evidence does not indicate such a theorem.

## 2. Strongest generic sequential-investment theorem threat — López & Vives (2019)

Their JPE paper makes the price-response mechanism mathematically explicit. In the two-stage Bertrand extension, the investment FOC contains an endogenous second-stage price term, and that strategic term can be negative. This means the paper cannot claim novelty for “endogenous price adjustment reduces investment incentives.”

Why it does not kill P2-R4: price is chosen by the investing firms rather than a distinct third actor, objectives are private, and no theorem compares a positive fixed-price first-stage cross derivative with a negative endogenous-price cross derivative.

## 3. Strongest current platform-response threat — Hahn & Kim (2026)

Two platforms choose service investments and fees, after which a seller optimally sets retail prices. The seller's price adjustment dampens investment effects and cross-platform spillovers. This is close to `(x1,x2)->p->D`, but the paper does not establish the target BR sign reversal.

## Net assessment

The remaining novelty risk is no longer “perhaps the negative price channel is known”—that channel **is** known. The precise unresolved priority question is whether the **dominance/sign-reversal theorem** has appeared in a structurally closer mixed-public or intermediary-pricing model. No such theorem was located at Distance 0–1.
