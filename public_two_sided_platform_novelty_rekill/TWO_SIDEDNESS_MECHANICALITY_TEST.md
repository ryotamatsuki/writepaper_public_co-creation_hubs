# Two-Sidedness Mechanicality Test

## Verdict

**Two-sidedness is essential to the stated strong P2-R4 comparison, but is not an independent novelty claim and does not make the generic price-response mechanism new.**

## External-prior evidence

- López & Vives (2019) shows in a standard R&D/product-market setting that an endogenous later price response can enter the first-stage investment FOC with a negative strategic contribution. Thus negative follower-price feedback is generic sequential-game economics.
- Sääskilahti (2006), Shrivastav (2021), and Knauff (2021/22) establish that network effects/compatibility can make investment relations complements or substitutes. Thus network-generated investment complementarity is also known.
- Hahn & Kim (2026) shows that downstream seller price adjustment can attenuate cross-platform investment effects even outside the candidate public-platform architecture.

## Frozen-model essentiality

What remains specific to the Stage-4 theorem is the **comparison of those known forces**. In B3 with `beta>0`, cross-side participation can generate a positive public-public cross effect. With `beta=0`, the relevant B3 cross effect collapses to zero. Endogenous private pricing can still re-couple governments at `beta=0`, but then there is no strong complements-to-substitutes comparison because the positive fixed-price side has disappeared.

Therefore Stage 6 does **not** claim:

- that one-sided models cannot have follower-price effects;
- that network effects as a source of complementarity are new; or
- that two-sidedness is universally necessary for strategic sign reversals.

It claims only that the surviving P2-R4 theorem uses two-sided feedback to create the positive benchmark term that the already-familiar endogenous-price strategic effect can dominate.
