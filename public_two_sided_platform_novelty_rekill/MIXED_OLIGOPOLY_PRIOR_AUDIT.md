# Mixed-Oligopoly Prior Audit

## Strongest added threat: Zogheib & Bourreau (2021)

*Public vs. Private Investments In Network Industries* directly studies a profit-maximizing private firm against public firms in prices and infrastructure investment, with both national-public and local-public variants. The associated thesis description reports that in mixed duopoly private-firm prices are strategic complements while public-firm prices are strategic substitutes, and its Appendix B studies the case in which the private firm leads in investment, including a local-public-firms subsection.

This is substantially closer to the candidate game than the earlier mixed-oligopoly screen because it simultaneously contains:

- private versus public objectives;
- multiple local public firms;
- network-industry infrastructure investment;
- strategic pricing;
- alternative timing involving private investment leadership.

However, the accessible evidence does **not** establish the target object `BR_i^{fixed′}>0>BR_i^{endogenous′}` for two local public investments. The paper also lacks the two-sided participation feedback that generates the positive B3 benchmark in the frozen model. The HAL theorem PDF was blocked in this run, so no unsupported claim is made about propositions that were not directly visible.

Classification: **STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE; Distance 2–3; not an exact/corollary kill on present evidence.**

## Other mixed-oligopoly priors

- **Buehler & Wey (2014):** state-owned/private investment crowd-out. Important for the fact that mixed objectives can generate strategic investment substitution; no third-price benchmark reversal.
- **Inoue, Kamijo & Tomaru (2009):** regional/local public objective competing with a private firm; absorbs regional-welfare interpretation, not the full game.
- **Dadpay & Heywood (2006):** public firms associated with separate countries/constituencies; absorbs multiple decentralized public objectives as an institutional novelty claim.
- **Lee (2021):** mixed-duopoly R&D/information-sharing interaction; supporting evidence that public/private innovation games are established.

## Verdict

Mixed-oligopoly literature kills any claim that separate public objectives, local public firms, private/public investment interaction, or strategic substitute behavior are new. It does not, on the inspected theorem record, kill P2-R4.
