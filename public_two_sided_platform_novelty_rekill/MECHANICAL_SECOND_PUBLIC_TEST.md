# Mechanical Second-Public Test

## Verdict

**PASS — no mechanical one-line second-public extension kills P2-R4.**

### Liu / Kim route

Liu et al. (2026) and Kim (2024) have one public and one private two-sided platform. Adding `H_2` does not merely duplicate an existing comparative static. It creates a new object: government 2's marginal return to `x_2` as government 1 changes `x_1`, including the effect transmitted through the shared private network and `p_T^*(x_1,x_2)`.

The one-public models do not contain this cross derivative. Applying their public-to-private fee response “twice” does not determine its sign.

### Zogheib–Bourreau route

The new hostile search weakens any claim that *multiple local public firms* are themselves a contribution: Zogheib & Bourreau (2021) already considers local public firms facing a private firm in a network-industry price/investment game.

But that does not mechanically deliver P2-R4. A referee would still have to:

1. introduce the two-sided participation feedback responsible for positive B3 complementarity;
2. construct the exact timing in which the two public investments precede a distinct private price;
3. define the fixed-private-price B3 counterfactual; and
4. prove that the endogenous private-price contribution dominates and flips the public-public BR slope.

Those are new equilibrium derivatives, not variable relabeling.

## Conclusion

The “second public platform” itself is **not novel**, but neither Liu/Kim duplication nor the existing local-public-firms literature makes P2-R4 an immediate corollary.
