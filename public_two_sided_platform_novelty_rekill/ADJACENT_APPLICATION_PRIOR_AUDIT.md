# Adjacent-Application Prior Audit

The target was searched as the generic sequential architecture `(x1,x2) -> p -> demand/participation`, not by the institutional label.

## Highest-value adjacent threats

### López & Vives (2019): R&D -> product-market pricing
Their two-stage model is a direct warning against claiming P2-R3. First-stage R&D is followed by product-market behavior; in the Bertrand extension the investment FOC contains the endogenous price-response term

`psi = (n-1) * phi_{i,p_j} * p^*_{j,x_i}`.

They show this strategic term can be negative. Thus “an endogenous second-stage price response can reduce the first-stage return to investment” is established theory.

Why it does not absorb P2-R4: prices are chosen by the investing firms, not by a distinct third actor; objectives are private; there is no fixed-third-price benchmark whose public-public cross effect is positive; and there is no cross-side participation system generating that positive benchmark term.

### Hahn & Kim (2026): platform investment -> downstream seller pricing
Two intermediary platforms choose transaction fees and service-quality investments; a downstream seller subsequently chooses retail prices. The seller's price response attenuates the demand-expanding impact of investment and the impact of rival investment. This is very close to the target chain-rule architecture.

Why it does not absorb P2-R4: both investing platforms are private, the follower is a seller rather than a shared platform, and the paper does not establish that investment best responses switch from complements under fixed follower pricing to substitutes under optimizing follower pricing.

### Mun (2019): two regional public contributions -> operator charge
Two regional governments choose contributions to a common transport infrastructure to maximize regional welfare, while an operator collects/sets the user charge under the stipulated pricing regime. This is a close decentralized-public/third-charge architecture.

Why it does not absorb P2-R4: the operator is jointly established rather than a profit-maximizing outside platform, the pricing rule/objective differs, and there is no two-sided shared-private-network cross effect or fixed-vs-endogenous charge sign-reversal theorem.

### Mun & Nakagawa (2010): regional infrastructure pricing and investment
The paper compares cross-border infrastructure pricing/investment under alternative provision and private-involvement regimes. It confirms that regional investment incentives and price responses are mature theory objects, but no P2-R4-equivalent theorem was located.

### Fudenberg–Tirole / BGK / Athey–Schmutzler
These absorb the generic strategic-investment vocabulary and much of P2-R3's intuition. They do not supply the target three-player benchmark comparison.

## Adjacent-application verdict

**No adjacent Distance-0/1 theorem located.** The closest generic economics is the negative follower-response strategic term; the non-absorbed object is its ability to overturn a separately identified positive cross-side-network public-public cross effect.
