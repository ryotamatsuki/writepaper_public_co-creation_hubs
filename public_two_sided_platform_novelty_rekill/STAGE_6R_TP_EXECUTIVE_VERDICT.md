The frozen Stage-4 object is not the existence of two-sided effects, public/private competition, investment spillovers, or an endogenous fee response. The only surviving headline object is

`BR_i^{B3′} > 0 > BR_i^{G′}`,

where B3 holds the national-private fee fixed and G allows the third, profit-maximizing platform to optimally adjust that fee.

## Hostile re-audit update

A second hostile pass expanded the serious comparison set to **35 papers/models**. The added high-value threats materially strengthen the prior-art record:

- López & Vives (2019) explicitly derive a second-stage price-response contribution to first-stage investment incentives in a two-stage R&D/price game; that contribution can be negative. This substantially absorbs P2-R3 as generic sequential-investment economics.
- Hahn & Kim (2026) study two platforms choosing service investments before a downstream seller sets prices, and show that downstream price adjustment dampens investment effects. This is a close current platform analogue of the price-mediated channel.
- Zogheib & Bourreau (2021) study a profit-maximizing private firm against national/local public firms in prices and infrastructure investment. This is the closest mixed-objective player-architecture threat located.
- Mun (2019) has two regional welfare-maximizing governments and a charge-setting infrastructure operator. This is the closest decentralized-government/third-price architecture located outside platforms.

None of these sources states or yields by direct relabeling the target benchmark reversal: a positive first-stage cross effect with the third actor's price fixed that becomes negative when that actor's optimizing price response is switched on.

## Kill-test results

- **Exact absorption:** NOT ESTABLISHED. No single inspected model reproduces the economically relevant full game and P2-R4.
- **Immediate corollary:** NO Distance-0/1 mapping located.
- **Closest corollary distance:** **2–3**, depending on which predecessor is used; every attempted mapping requires changing the strategic-feedback network, not merely renaming variables.
- **Mechanical second-public extension:** FAILS TO KILL. Adding a second public actor to Liu/Kim is not sufficient; the target public-public cross derivative must be newly derived. Zogheib–Bourreau already contains local public firms, but still lacks the two-sided shared-private-network benchmark and the fixed-price/endogenous-price reversal.
- **Two-sidedness mechanicality:** two-sidedness is not independently novel and follower-price strategic effects are generic. However, no located one-sided theorem supplies the same positive fixed-price cross effect plus negative endogenous-third-price effect as an immediate corollary. In the frozen model, `beta=0` destroys the positive B3 side of the theorem.

## Strongest threats

**Closest mixed-objective architecture threat:** Zogheib & Bourreau (2021), *Public vs. Private Investments in Network Industries*. It contains a private firm, national/local public firms, prices and infrastructure investments, including a private-leader-in-investment appendix. Accessible primary/index evidence does not reveal P2-R4; full theorem-level access to the HAL paper was blocked during this run, so this remains the strongest evidentiary limitation.

**Closest generic sequential-investment theorem threat:** López & Vives (2019), *Overlapping Ownership, R&D Spillovers, and Antitrust Policy*. Their two-stage extension writes the strategic investment term explicitly through endogenous second-stage responses and shows that the price-response term can be negative. It does not compare a fixed-price positive first-stage cross derivative with an endogenous-price negative cross derivative and has no distinct third price setter or public objectives.

**Closest current platform-response threat:** Hahn & Kim (2026), *Price Parity Clauses and Platform Investments*. Two platforms invest and a downstream seller sets prices; endogenous seller-price adjustment dampens investment effects. The paper does not establish the P2-R4 best-response sign reversal.

## Revised contribution statement

We show that two decentralized welfare-maximizing investments linked by cross-side participation can be strategic complements when a third platform's fee is held fixed, yet strategic substitutes when that profit-maximizing platform optimally adjusts the fee. The reversal is a dominance result: the endogenous fee-response contribution can overturn the positive cross-side-network contribution to the public-public cross-partial.

This statement is intentionally narrower than a claim to a new theory of public platforms or to a new generic follower-response mechanism.
