# Kim / Liu Result-Level Kill

Relevant current threats:

- Jeong-Yoo Kim (2024), “Mixed duopoly in two-sided markets,” The Manchester School 92(3), 281–295, DOI `10.1111/manc.12467`.
- Youming Liu, Edona Reshidi, and Francisco Rivadeneyra (2026), “Public vs. Private Payment Platforms: Market Impacts and Optimal Policy,” Bank of Canada Staff Working Paper 2026-10, DOI `10.34989/swp-2026-10`.

Kim explicitly studies a welfare-oriented public platform competing with a private platform in a linear-city framework and includes platform investment. Liu–Reshidi–Rivadeneyra study a welfare-maximizing public platform against a profit-maximizing private platform and characterize private fee responses, participation/switching, and welfare.

Neither paper is the exact two-regional-public-investment plus one metro-price-follower game.

However:

- `dp_T*/dx_i<0` is not enough for novelty;
- public-vs-private price/quality interaction is already a live mixed-platform research object;
- linear-city differentiation is explicitly used in the closest public/private platform literature.

The only Stage 4 object not already component-level is the three-player cross-government price-mediated feedback, and that object is generated by the characteristic-space mapping rather than a matching-specific state.

**Verdict: P1 and generic public-private response claims KILL; full-game distinction remains architecture-level only.**
