# Market Creation vs Displacement — P4

The active metro regime exposes a structural limitation of the selected baseline.

If metro serves a positive mass, its type-independent gross offer satisfies `q>kappa`. Hence every project not choosing a public Hub receives strictly positive net utility from metro. Non-participation is absent.

Therefore, in the public–metro–public full-game regime:

- `ADD1 (0 -> H_i) = 0` locally;
- marginal local-Hub expansion reallocates projects at a public-metro boundary;
- creation and active metro competition cannot coexist in the same static regime under a constant metro match value.

At a public-metro cutoff, local project-side gross match value equals `q`, while metro project-side gross value is `m=q+p_T>q`. A switch from metro to local therefore lowers project-side gross value by the fee wedge `p_T`.

Under the frozen local-partner accounting, however, the local match also creates expected local-partner surplus `q`. At the private interior optimum,

`2q*-m=K>0`.

Thus the strict national accounting can value a marginal metro-to-local switch positively because of local partner surplus even though the project's own productive value falls.

This is fundamentally a displacement/incidence result, not the intended simultaneous market-creation-versus-duplication tradeoff.

**P4 classification: derivable, but KILL as a headline additionality mechanism.**
