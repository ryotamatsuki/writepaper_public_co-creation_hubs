# B3 vs G

B3 fixes `q=m-bar p`. In the active middle-metro regime,

`F_1^{B3}=[3d^2x_1^2+q^2-2 gamma u x_1^3]/(2u x_1^2)`

and `partial F_1^{B3}/partial x_2=0`.

G sets `q=q*(x_1,x_2)`. Therefore

`F_1^G=F_1^{B3}(q*)+W_{1q}q_{x_1}*`.

At symmetry,

`W_{1q}q_{x_1}*=-a^2/(8u)<0`.

If B3 is anchored at the **same metro offer** `q=q*(x^G,x^G)` as a symmetric full-game equilibrium, then at `x=x^G`

`F_1^{B3}=a^2/(8u)>0`,

because the G FOC is zero after adding the negative price-response term. Since the B3 objective is strictly concave inside the regime, removing the private price response creates upward pressure on public investment; the local B3 optimum is above the G point unless a regime/KKT boundary intervenes.

Thus endogenous private pricing is strategically relevant, not a passive outside option.

However an analogous own-investment price-response channel already exists in B1. The only genuinely three-player addition is the cross-government coupling, which is killed as a matching contribution by the characteristic-space isomorphism.

**P3 classification: TRUE algebraically; contribution KILL.**
