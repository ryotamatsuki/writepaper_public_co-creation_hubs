# Partner-Incidence Accounting

The frozen Stage 3 model explicitly assigns a local successful match one normalized project-side benefit and one normalized local-partner benefit. It specifies the metro quantity `m` only as a **project-side** expected match value. Stage 4 therefore does not invent an unmodeled metro-partner surplus.

Regional welfare counts:

1. resident-project net surplus, including metro fees as regional outflows;
2. local-partner surplus on all matches routed through the region's public Hub;
3. public investment cost.

National welfare counts metro fee payments and private profit as transfers. Under the frozen primitives, in a full-participation public–metro–public regime:

`SW = 4(A_1+A_2)+2mL-2kappa-gamma(x_1^2+x_2^2)/2`.

The factor `4(A_1+A_2)` is two project populations times project-side plus local-partner benefits for local matches. The metro term is `2mL` because only project-side metro match value was frozen.

## W1 counterfactual

Removing local-partner incidence gives `W_i^{W1}=P-gamma x_i^2/2`.

In the full game at symmetry, the baseline partner-incidence model has an own metro-price-response term `-a^2/(8u)<0`, whereas W1 has

`a(2m-a x)/(16u x)>0`

in the active metro regime. Partner incidence therefore flips the sign of the government's **own** incentive to manipulate the private price. However the triadic cross-government effect survives without partner incidence, so partner incidence is qualitatively important but not essential to the existence of full-game coupling.
