\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

The model isolates one mechanism: endogenous repricing by a shared private intermediary can change the strategic relation between decentralized public investments. Several boundaries matter for interpreting that result.

First, the result is local rather than global. The paper supplies a regular interior configuration and an open neighborhood in which the sign reversal persists, but it does not characterize all primitives for which the reversal occurs. The numerical certificate and local robustness exercises rule out a single-point accident; they do not substitute for a global sign theorem.

Second, the novelty is combinational and therefore exposed to a natural objection. Positive cross-side network feedback is familiar, and negative effects transmitted through an endogenous follower price are familiar. The economic content of the paper is the nested comparison showing that these forces can change the \emph{type of strategic game} played by two public investors. The claim is accordingly limited to that qualitative interaction; it does not relabel either familiar component as new.

Third, primary-route single-homing is a modeling abstraction. Innovation projects and the firms behind them can interact with several networks. The maintained interpretation is narrower: a focal project has one principal intermediation route for the activity being modeled. Allowing project multihoming could attenuate route substitution and lies outside the present analysis.

Fourth, the private objective and instrument set are reduced-form approximations. Real commercial innovation communities may value occupancy, membership revenue, sponsorship, ecosystem growth, or longer-run strategic goals, and may use richer contracts than a single posted price. Similarly, a posted membership or access fee may bundle workspace and community services. The model uses a one-dimensional effective access price to represent the margin through which a commercial alternative responds to project demand; it does not claim robustness to arbitrary contracting environments. The sequence in which public facilitation is chosen before private pricing is likewise a maintained feature of the research question rather than a claim about all institutional timings.

Fifth, the institutional evidence establishes an analogue class, not an observed treatment-control relationship. We do not observe a causal episode in which expansion of a regional public hub induced CIC Tokyo, QWS, Level39, or another named operator to cut its price. The negative price response is a theoretical prediction. A useful empirical design would combine changes in regional facilitation capacity with project routing, partner participation, and flexible private access prices.

Finally, strategic substitution is not a policy diagnosis. The fixed-price environment is an identification benchmark, not a recommendation to regulate private prices. Nor does the positive coordination wedge at the reported configuration imply that any specific merger, subsidy, mandate, or price restriction is optimal. The model's policy content is limited to identifying a coordination externality and a private repricing channel that a richer policy analysis would need to take into account.
