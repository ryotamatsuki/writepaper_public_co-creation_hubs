\section{Strategic Interaction and Private Repricing}\label{sec:mainresult}

The central result is a qualitative change in the strategic relation between the two public investments when the shared private intermediary is allowed to reprice.

\subsection{Fixed private price: cross-side reinforcement}

When $p_T$ is fixed, an increase in $x_1$ directly increases the support-side participation of $H_1$. This raises the value and project mass of the region-1 public route. The resulting displacement of projects from $H_T$ lowers support-side participation and project value at the private route. For region 2, the weakened private outside option shifts projects toward $H_2$, raising the marginal return to $x_2$. At the verified B3 configuration, the public cross derivative is positive:
\[
W_{2,x_2x_1}=0.0135412>0.
\]
With $W_{2,x_2x_2}<0$, the best-response slope is positive.

This complementarity relies on cross-side participation. Setting $\beta=0$ removes the relevant B3 public cross effect exactly. Thus two-sidedness is essential to the positive benchmark interaction used in the sign-reversal comparison.

\subsection{Endogenous private price: an offsetting price channel}

In the full game, government 2 evaluates
\[
\widetilde W_2(x_1,x_2)
=
W_2(x_1,x_2,p_T^*(x_1,x_2)).
\]
Its reduced cross derivative contains the direct fixed-price network terms as well as terms generated by the price policy and its derivatives. In particular, a term of the form
\[
W_{2,x_2p}\,p^*_{T,x_1}
\]
is absent from B3. At the verified full-game configuration, $p^*_{T,x_1}<0$: stronger public facilitation in region 1 induces the private intermediary to cut its access price. That price cut makes $H_T$ more attractive to region-2 projects and lowers the marginal return to $x_2$. The price-response contribution is sufficiently strong to overturn the positive fixed-price network contribution.

\begin{proposition}[Locally robust strategic reversal]\label{prop:reversal}
There exists a regular interior parameter configuration, and therefore a sufficiently small open neighborhood of configurations satisfying the same strict regularity conditions, in which regional public facilitation investments are strategic complements when the shared private access price is held fixed but strategic substitutes when the private intermediary optimally reprices:
\[
\BR_i^{B3\prime}>0>\BR_i^{G\prime}.
\]
\end{proposition}

The proposition is an existence and local-continuity result rather than a global sign theorem. The analytic decomposition identifies the direct network component and the additional endogenous-price component. A reproducible numerical certificate supplies a regular interior configuration at which the two best-response slopes have strict opposite signs; smoothness and nonsingularity then preserve the strict inequalities locally.

The corresponding own second derivatives are negative, and the public cross derivative switches from approximately $0.01354$ in B3 to $-0.00481$ in G. The private fee at the G equilibrium is the same fee imposed exogenously in B3, so the comparison isolates price endogeneity rather than a mechanical difference in the fee level.

The contribution of Proposition \ref{prop:reversal} is the benchmark-to-full-game change in the strategic relation between the two decentralized public investors.
