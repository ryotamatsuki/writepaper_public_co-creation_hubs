\section{Welfare and Coordination}\label{sec:welfare}

The sign of a government's best-response slope is not a welfare ranking. Strategic interaction describes how one government's optimal investment responds locally to the other's choice; welfare depends on project allocation, partner surplus, real facilitation costs, and private profit. We therefore keep the strategic reversal and the welfare analysis separate.

\subsection{Regional and aggregate welfare}

In the central regime, project surplus in region $i$ is
\begin{equation}
PS_i
=
\int_s^{t_i}(zq_T-\kappa_T-p_T)\,dz
+
\int_{t_i}^{1}(zq_i-\kappa_L)\,dz .
\label{eq:PS}
\end{equation}

For support-side route $h$, let $r_h$ denote the gross participation benefit before applying the support-cap constraint and let
\[
m_h=\min\{1,\max\{0,r_h\}\}
\]
denote the resulting participation mass. With support cost $c\sim U[0,1]$, national partner surplus at route $h$ is
\begin{equation}
S_h^P
=\int_0^{m_h}(r_h-c)\,dc
=r_hm_h-\frac{m_h^2}{2}.
\label{eq:partnersurplusgeneral}
\end{equation}
Thus $S_h^P=0$ for $r_h\leq0$, $S_h^P=r_h^2/2$ for $0<r_h<1$, and $S_h^P=r_h-1/2$ for $r_h\geq1$. Under the symmetric regional attribution used in the regional objectives, each region receives one half of national partner surplus. Regional welfare is therefore, in general,
\begin{equation}
W_i
=
PS_i
+\frac12\sum_h\left(r_hm_h-\frac{m_h^2}{2}\right)
-\frac{\gamma}{2}x_i^2.
\label{eq:Wi}
\end{equation}
When support participation is interior on every route, $m_h=r_h$ and \eqref{eq:Wi} reduces to the convenient local expression
\[
W_i
=PS_i+\frac{(m_1)^2+(m_2)^2+(m_T)^2}{4}-\frac{\gamma}{2}x_i^2.
\]
The local analytic derivations use this interior specialization. The all-regime numerical evaluator instead uses \eqref{eq:partnersurplusgeneral}, including at support-saturation histories.

Aggregate welfare is
\begin{equation}
W^N=W_1+W_2+\Pi_T.
\label{eq:WN}
\end{equation}
A project using $H_T$ pays $p_T$ in regional project surplus, while the same payment appears as private profit. Because private operating cost is normalized to zero, the transfer cancels exactly at the aggregate level:
\[
-p_Tn_T^F+\Pi_T=0.
\]
Accordingly, a lower private price does not mechanically imply higher aggregate welfare.

\subsection{A local cross-regional coordination wedge}

At the repaired reported G stationary candidate, government $i$'s own reduced first-order condition is numerically close to zero under the documented finite-difference check. Holding the rival public investment fixed while allowing downstream participation and private pricing to readjust, the national directional derivative decomposes as
\begin{equation}
\frac{dW^N}{dx_i}
=
\frac{d\widetilde W_i}{dx_i}
+
\frac{d\widetilde W_j}{dx_i}
+
\frac{d\Pi_T}{dx_i},
\qquad j\neq i.
\label{eq:wedge}
\end{equation}
The reproducible result pipeline computes all three components and also differentiates $W^N$ directly. At the reported state it gives approximately
\[
\frac{d\widetilde W_i}{dx_i}\simeq0,
\qquad
\frac{d\widetilde W_j}{dx_i}\simeq0.50053,
\qquad
\frac{d\Pi_T}{dx_i}\simeq-0.01008,
\]
with the direct national derivative
\[
\frac{dW^N}{dx_i}\simeq0.49045>0.
\]
The direct derivative agrees numerically with the sum in \eqref{eq:wedge}. Thus the reported G stationary candidate exhibits a positive local coordination wedge in the coordinated $+x_i$ direction. This is not a solved first-best or global social-optimum comparison, and its sign is not implied by the sign of $\BR_i'$.
