\section{Robustness}\label{sec:robustness}

The robustness analysis has an expository role distinct from the proof. Theorem \ref{thm:reversal} already establishes that the sign reversal holds on a nonempty interval of sufficiently small positive network interaction, conditional on regular beta-zero branches and strict second-order conditions. Proposition \ref{prop:exactcertificate} separately establishes the reversal exactly at the canonical interior vector. The computations below ask whether the displayed economic configuration is numerically knife-edge and how far the maintained local interpretation can be extended without changing the model.

\paragraph{Network interaction.}
The analytic result organizes variation in $\beta$ near zero: B3 starts from a zero cross effect with a strictly positive $\beta$ derivative, while G starts from a strictly negative cross effect. The canonical benchmark uses $\beta=0.05$ and satisfies the reversal by exact certification. Because the theorem leaves $\varepsilon$ implicit, we do not infer that every value between zero and $0.05$ lies on one globally regular branch solely from these two facts. That stronger connected-interval statement would require additional branch tracking and is not needed for the present local theorem.

\paragraph{Primitive perturbations.}
All inequalities defining the canonical configuration are strict. The project cutoffs and partner masses are interior, the relevant public and private second-order conditions are negative, and both best-response slopes are bounded away from zero. The participation system and first-order conditions are smooth with nonsingular local Jacobians. A deterministic perturbation exercise that varies the primitive vector by $\pm0.5$ percent in 20 reproducible draws preserves
\[
\BR_i^{B3\prime}>0>\BR_i^{G\prime}
\]
in all 20 cases. This exercise is not the source of the theorem; it confirms that the reported benchmark magnitudes are not a tolerance-level accident.

\paragraph{Distribution and network functional form.}
The uniform project-type distribution is not locally essential. Replacing it by a sufficiently small $C^1$ perturbation with positive density at the relevant cutoffs changes project masses and surplus terms smoothly. Conditional on the same interior ordering and nonsingularity, the strict sign inequalities persist by the implicit function theorem. The linear partner-participation specification is likewise locally nonessential: small $C^1$ perturbations of the network functions that preserve the relevant derivative signs and regularity induce continuous changes in the same equilibrium system.

\paragraph{Asymmetry and scope.}
Small asymmetric perturbations of the action state around the symmetric canonical configuration preserve the strict sign reversal by continuity. This is not a theorem for arbitrary region-specific primitives. The robustness claims remain deliberately local: we do not establish a global parameter region, arbitrary-distribution result, arbitrary nonlinear-network result, or fully heterogeneous-regions theorem. Broad parameter-plane classification would add substantial algebra and computation without strengthening the paper's core mechanism proportionately.
