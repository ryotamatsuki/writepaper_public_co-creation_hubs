\section{Related Literature}\label{sec:literature}

Our result combines ingredients that are individually familiar. The contribution is therefore best located by separating the known components from the qualitative comparison that remains.

\paragraph{Endogenous follower responses and investment incentives.}
The closest generic threat is \citet{LopezVives2019}. Their sequential-investment framework makes clear that a downstream or follower response can enter an earlier investment incentive; the existence of an endogenous price-response term is therefore not new here. Our narrower object is the strategic relationship between \emph{two} decentralized public investors. We hold the third party's equilibrium price fixed in a nested benchmark and then restore its optimizing price policy. The same pair of public investments is complementary in the former environment and substitutable in the latter. The comparison thus asks whether activating an already-understood response channel changes the qualitative game played by two other strategic actors.

\paragraph{Public and private investment competition.}
\citet{ZogheibBourreau2021} study public versus private investment in network industries, and \citet{Mun2019} and \citet{MunNakagawa2010} analyze investment and pricing in transport settings with public or regional strategic objectives. These papers make clear that mixed objectives and decentralized regional investment are not themselves novel. Relative to this line, the present model emphasizes cross-side participation and a shared price-setting outside option whose response alters the sign of public--public strategic interaction.

\paragraph{Two-sided platforms and network interaction.}
Strategic interaction in two-sided market oligopoly is developed in \citet{FarhiHagiu2008}. More recent work studies public and private payment platforms \citep{LiuReshidiRivadeneyra2026}, platform investment under contractual pricing constraints \citep{HahnKim2026}, sequential pricing on multisided platforms \citep{BontemsHamiltonLepore2025}, and pricing and service investment under mixed network externalities \citep{HeEtAl2026}. These contributions absorb important components of our environment: network feedback, platform pricing, and strategic investment. We do not claim novelty for those components.

\paragraph{Qualitative effects of sequential pricing.}
\citet{SanchezCartas2026} provides another nearby illustration of how follower responses can alter qualitative outcomes in sequential platform settings. Again, the distinction in the present paper is the nested fixed-price versus optimizing-price comparison for the \emph{public--public} best-response slope. In the prior-art audit underlying this manuscript, we did not identify a distance-zero or distance-one predecessor for that whole comparative object. This positioning is intentionally narrow: it does not imply a new general theory of public platforms or two-sided markets.
