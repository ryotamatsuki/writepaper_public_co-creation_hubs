## Structured abstract

**Introduction.** We study two regional governments that invest in public innovation intermediation while sharing a price-setting metropolitan private alternative. Cross-side participation can make public investments strategic complements when the private access price is held fixed, whereas endogenous private repricing adds an opposing strategic channel.

**Methods.** We compare a fixed-price benchmark with the full sequential game, derive the public cross effects analytically around the zero-network benchmark, and use exact rational interval methods to certify the canonical interior equilibrium pair.

**Results.** On regular interior equilibrium branches, the fixed-price public cross effect is zero at beta = 0 but has a strictly positive first derivative in beta, while the full-game cross effect is already strictly negative at beta = 0. Hence there exists epsilon > 0 such that, for sufficiently small positive network interaction, the fixed-price best-response slope is positive while the full-game slope is negative. At the canonical parameter vector, an exact rational Krawczyk certificate and interval implicit differentiation verify the matched but distinct fixed-price and full-game equilibria, strict second-order conditions, and opposite best-response slopes.

**Conclusion.** Endogenous commercial repricing can change the sign of decentralized public strategic interaction. The result is local rather than a global primitive-space classification, and welfare accounting is kept separate from the strategic reversal.
