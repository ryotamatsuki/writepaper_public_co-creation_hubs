Please consider the manuscript **“Strategic Interaction among Public Innovation Hubs under Private Repricing”** for publication as an Original Article in the *Journal of Public Economic Theory*.

The paper studies decentralized public investment when two regional public innovation intermediaries share a price-setting commercial alternative. Its central question is whether allowing that private intermediary to reprice changes the strategic relationship between the public investors. The fixed-price benchmark and the full game give qualitatively different answers: public investments can be strategic complements when the commercial access price is held fixed, yet strategic substitutes when the private price is chosen endogenously.

The main result is analytic rather than a numerical comparison. Around a regular zero-network benchmark, the fixed-price public cross effect is zero with a strictly positive first derivative in network strength, while the full-game public cross effect is already strictly negative. This yields a local sign-reversal theorem for sufficiently small positive network interaction. A separate exact computer-assisted proposition jointly isolates the matched but distinct benchmark and full-game equilibria at the canonical rational parameter vector and certifies the relevant second-order conditions and opposite best-response slopes. The exact certificate is presented as verification of the canonical equilibrium pair, not as a global primitive-space characterization.

We believe the manuscript fits JPET because the mechanism concerns decentralized public decision making, local public investment, external strategic interaction, and endogenous market response. The paper is positioned narrowly relative to the existing literatures on follower responses, public/private investment, and two-sided platforms: it does not claim novelty for endogenous follower pricing or network effects themselves, but for the qualitative reversal in the same public–public strategic object when the shared private pricing policy is activated.

The manuscript is a theoretical/computational study. It reports no external empirical dataset. Symbolic, numerical, and exact computer-assisted verification materials accompany the research record, and the submission package includes the required data-availability and generative-AI disclosures. Detailed algebra is kept in the Appendix so that the main text emphasizes the economic mechanism and theorem.
