# Candidate C — Project Single-Homing × Partner Multi-Homing

## Core structure

Projects are single-homing and choose `0,H1,H2,HT`. Partners may join multiple platforms independently.

Project type: `z ~ U[0,1]`, interpreted as productive value of accessing a sufficiently thick partner ecosystem.

Partner type: `c ~ U[0,1]`, interpreted as real per-platform participation/coordination cost.

Partner-side participation value on public platform i:

`rho + x_i + beta n_i^F - c`.

On the private platform:

`rho_T + beta n_T^F - c`.

Interior partner masses therefore satisfy

`n_i^P = rho + x_i + beta n_i^F`,

`n_T^P = rho_T + beta n_T^F`.

Project utility from platform h is

`U_rh(z)=z[v+alpha n_h^P]-kappa_rh-1{h=T}p_T`,

with outside payoff zero.

## Why it survives Stage 3

Partner multihoming avoids a second discrete-choice platform market while preserving endogenous two-sided feedback. Project single-homing preserves platform competition and a well-defined private demand. Pairwise project cutoffs are affine/rational in partner masses and price, so Stage 4 remains piecewise but low-dimensional.

The private price response affects both private partner mass and rival public participation. This creates a genuine P2-R channel absent in fixed-price B3.

Prior-art threat: one-side multihoming is standard (Belleflamme–Peitz 2019; Jeitschko–Tremblay 2020). It is not claimed as novelty.
