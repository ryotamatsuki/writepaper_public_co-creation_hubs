# Candidate A — Two-Sided Participation Matching

## Skeleton

Projects `(k,z)` and partners both choose whether/where to participate. Hub `h` has masses `(n_h^F,n_h^P)` and matching rate

`mu_h=mu(x_h,n_h^F,n_h^P,k)`.

Project payoff: `z mu_h-c_h`. Partner participation is determined by expected match surplus net of participation cost.

## Participation

PASS in principle. Continuous `z` yields non-use; heterogeneity in `k` and regional partner composition can support positive H1/H2/HT masses.

## Matching state

`Xi_h=(n_h^F,n_h^P)` is endogenous.

## Hard kill

The minimal version is exactly the familiar two-sided-platform chicken-and-egg problem: each side's benefit depends on participation on the other side. Caillaud–Jullien and Armstrong already treat competing platforms/intermediaries with endogenous cross-side participation and pricing. Replacing `matching rate` by `cross-side service/network benefit` preserves the fixed point and pricing logic.

Adding congestion to escape that reduction moves the model to B/F; adding heterogeneous capacity assignment moves it to C.

**Verdict: KILL — K4/K11 generic two-sided-platform absorption.**
