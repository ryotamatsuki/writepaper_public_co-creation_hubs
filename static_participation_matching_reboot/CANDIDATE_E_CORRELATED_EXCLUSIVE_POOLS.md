# Candidate E — Correlated / Exclusive Partner Opportunities

Let potential match values across H1/H2/HT have a joint distribution with overlap/exclusivity.

For a risk-neutral single-homing project that chooses one intermediary before realization, expected payoff from Hub h depends only on its own marginal distribution:

`E[V_h | omega]-P_h`.

Changing the cross-Hub joint distribution while holding all marginal provider distributions fixed does not change single-homing choice, demand, pricing, or public investment incentives. Correlation matters only if firms can sample multiple Hubs, search sequentially, condition a later action on earlier failure, or compete for common scarce partners. Those are prohibited here or belong to C.

Caillaud–Jullien also note that cross-matchmaker correlation primarily changes the benefit of multihoming; our single-homing baseline is even less able to exploit the joint state.

**Verdict: KILL — K5/K6. Joint opportunity geometry is cosmetic under static single-homing expected utility.**
