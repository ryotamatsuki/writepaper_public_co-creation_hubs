# Targeted Prior-Art Update

Search date: 2026-09-02. This is a mechanism-targeted update, not a repeat of Stage 2.

## Two-sided participation / platform externalities

- Bernard Caillaud and Bruno Jullien (2003), “Chicken & Egg: Competition among Intermediation Service Providers,” RAND Journal of Economics 34(2), 309–328, DOI 10.2307/1593720. Competing matchmakers, cross-side participation/network externalities, pricing, and registration/non-registration are central.
- Mark Armstrong (2006), “Competition in Two-Sided Markets,” RAND Journal of Economics 37(3), 668–691, DOI 10.1111/j.1756-2171.2006.tb00037.x. Competing platforms with cross-group participation externalities.

These strongly threaten Candidate A.

## Matching-function / directed-search congestion

- Arthur J. Hosios (1990), “On the Efficiency of Matching and Related Models of Search and Unemployment,” Review of Economic Studies 57(2), 279–298.
- Espen R. Moen (1997), “Competitive Search Equilibrium,” Journal of Political Economy 105(2), 385–411, DOI 10.1086/262077.
- Kenneth Burdett, Shouyong Shi, and Randall Wright (2001), “Pricing and Matching with Frictions,” Journal of Political Economy 109(5), 1060–1085, DOI 10.1086/322835. Buyers choose capacity-constrained sellers; queue/matching frictions and endogenous pricing are solved.

These strongly threaten B/F.

## Capacity / bilateral assignment

- John William Hatfield and Paul R. Milgrom (2005), “Matching with Contracts,” American Economic Review 95(4), 913–935, DOI 10.1257/0002828054825466. Tractable matching with heterogeneous contracts relies on substitutability / aggregate-demand structure.

This confirms that C's assignment state is genuinely matching-theoretic, but also highlights the structural restrictions needed for tractability.

## Complementarities

- Guilherme Carmona (2024), “Stable matching in large markets with occupational choice,” Theoretical Economics, DOI 10.3982/TE5915. Large-market matching can accommodate complementarities and externalities under general existence conditions.

This means D is not novel merely because it invokes complementarity; strategic public/public/private integration would still need its own tractable theorem.

## Correlation

Caillaud–Jullien explicitly note that correlation of matching success across matchmakers changes multihoming benefits; our frozen single-homing setting gives joint correlation even less independent leverage.

No searched mechanism supplies a clean minimal static candidate that passes all of the present gates.
