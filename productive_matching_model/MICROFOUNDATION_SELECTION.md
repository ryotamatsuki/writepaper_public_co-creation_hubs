# Microfoundation Selection

## Candidate A — Binary productive-match opportunity

**Rejected as too reduced-form.** It can encode a match probability but cannot derive both overlap and referral from partner access without directly assigning profile-specific probabilities.

## Candidate B — Match-quality draw

**Deferred.** It gives richer complementarity and best-match quality but adds an unnecessary distributional layer for the Stage 4 hard kill.

## Candidate C — Finite complementary partner pool

**Selected.** It is the smallest structure that simultaneously gives:

1. a distinction between contact and productive match;
2. complementary-resource compatibility;
3. overlap from the same opportunity being discoverable by either hub;
4. referral from a distinct foreign-specialist opportunity that requires both sender and receiver hubs;
5. exact profile-level match probabilities;
6. a clean M0/M1 nesting;
7. transparent comparison with competing-matchmaker prior art.

## Candidate D — Matching function

**Rejected for the baseline.** A mass matching function would introduce participation/mass effects and risk recreating a generic indirect-network-externality platform model before such richness is diagnosed as necessary.

## Anti-ad-hoc test

The finite-pool structure would remain the preferred institutional representation even if the final government cross-effect never became positive. The overlap opportunity represents duplicated brokerage access; the referral opportunity represents actual cross-hub transfer of a complementary partner. Neither was chosen because of its sign implication.

## Verdict

**Candidate C — finite complementary partner pool.**
