# Downstream Competition Test

## Nonstrategic benchmark

Replace product-market competition with an independent per-firm match payoff `V>0`. Regional gross payoff is `V mu_i` and there is no rival-profit or consumer-surplus interaction.

The cross-entry effect is

`Gamma_NS = V[(t-p)-h] = V(k-p)`.

Therefore:

- M0 (`k=0`): strict substitution;
- complementarity iff `k>p`;
- the nonstrategic referral threshold is `k_NS*=p`, or `r_NS*=p/(1-p)^2` when feasible.

Thus downstream competition is **not necessary for the existence of cooperation–competition sign reversal**.

## Cournot regional-welfare threshold is strictly higher

In the Cournot model,

`Gamma_C(k) = Gamma_0 + Delta k[8A+Delta(11-14h-7k)]/18`.

Evaluate it at the nonstrategic threshold `k=p`:

`Gamma_C(p) = -7 Delta^2 p^2(p^2-4p+5)/18`.

Since

`p^2-4p+5=(p-2)^2+1>0`,

we have

`Gamma_C(p)<0`.

Because `Gamma_C(k)` is strictly increasing in `k`, whenever a Cournot complementarity threshold exists it satisfies

`k_C*>p=k_NS*`.

Equivalently, product-market interaction requires strictly stronger cross-hub referral before public hub provision becomes complementary.

## Mechanism

A productive match raises one firm's capability. Under Cournot this changes both firms' quantities, profits, total output and consumer surplus. The regional government does not treat a match as an isolated `V`; it internalizes its local firm's strategic gain and only its regional share of consumer surplus while the rival region's firm is also affected.

The threshold difference is order `Delta^2`: at `k=p`, the first-order direct match-value comparison is exactly balanced, while the strategic product-market interaction leaves the strictly negative term above.

## Classification

**Downstream competition: ESSENTIAL TO GOVERNMENT WEDGE.**

It is not essential to sign reversal itself, but it creates a theorem-level shift in the cooperation/complementarity threshold. Retaining downstream competition is therefore justified by the original regional-industrial-policy question rather than by a need to manufacture both signs.
