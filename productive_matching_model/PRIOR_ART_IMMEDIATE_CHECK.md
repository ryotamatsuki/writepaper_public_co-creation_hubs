# Immediate Prior-Art Check

This is a Stage 4 structural absorption test, not the final Stage 6M novelty re-kill.

## 1. Caillaud & Jullien (2003), RAND Journal of Economics

**Object:** imperfect price competition between intermediation service providers with indirect network externalities, nonexclusive/multihoming use, and discriminatory pricing.

**Overlap:** multiple intermediaries; matching/intermediation; cross-side/network effects; intermediary competition.

**Material distance:** private intermediary price/business strategy is endogenous; public provision is absent; productive capability changes of matched beneficiary firms are absent; downstream product-market rivalry between beneficiaries is absent; regional-welfare entry incentives are absent; the bilateral referral versus duplicated-opportunity architecture is not the same object.

**Stage 4 verdict:** strongest generic threat, but the full minimal theorem is **NOT ABSORBED by a one-page relabeling** on the evidence inspected.

DOI: `10.2307/1593720`.

## 2. Damiano & Li (2008), Journal of the European Economic Association

**Object:** competing matchmakers use prices to sort heterogeneous participants into matching markets under complementarity in match value.

**Overlap:** competing matchmakers; heterogeneous agents; formal complementarity; matching efficiency.

**Material distance:** no decentralized public provision, no fixed public hub decision, no cross-hub referral/public spillover layer, no beneficiary firms that subsequently compete in a product market, no regional-welfare cross-entry theorem.

**Verdict:** **NOT ABSORBED**.

DOI: `10.1162/JEEA.2008.6.4.789`.

## 3. Calcagnini, Giombini, Liberati & Travaglini (2019), Journal of Technology Transfer

**Object:** technology transfer arises from endogenous matching between technology-transfer offices and innovative firms; TTO search intensity and firm advertising generate externalities and possible excessive/insufficient transfer.

**Overlap:** innovation intermediation; matching/search; public/technology-transfer institutional motivation; externalities.

**Material distance:** no two decentralized public hubs choosing binary provision; no explicit duplicated matching opportunity plus bilateral referral structure; no downstream oligopoly between beneficiary firms; no regional strategic-complement/substitute hub theorem.

**Verdict:** **NOT ABSORBED**.

DOI: `10.1007/s10961-018-9667-3`.

## 4. Ichihashi (2021), RAND Journal of Economics

**Object:** competing data intermediaries collect consumer data and sell it to downstream firms; nonrival data and multisharing affect intermediary competition and downstream data prices.

**Overlap:** multiple intermediaries plus downstream firms; endogenous downstream consequences.

**Material distance:** data acquisition/sale rather than productive matching; private intermediary compensation/pricing rather than decentralized public provision; no complementary-partner matching; no overlap/referral public-good architecture; no regional-welfare entry game.

**Verdict:** **NOT ABSORBED**, but important because it proves that simply appending downstream firms to intermediary competition is not itself novel.

DOI: `10.1111/1756-2171.12382`.

## 5. Gehrig (1993), Journal of Economics & Management Strategy

**Object:** intermediaries reduce costly search frictions among heterogeneous traders; competition among intermediaries can yield Bertrand/Walrasian outcomes.

**Overlap:** search friction, heterogeneous agents, intermediary competition.

**Material distance:** no public regional provision, productive-match capability channel, cross-hub referral, or downstream beneficiary competition.

**Verdict:** component prior art only.

DOI: `10.1111/j.1430-9134.1993.00097.x`.

## 6. Sülzle (2009), International Journal of Industrial Organization

**Object:** duopolistic B2B matching-market competition with indirect network externalities, including independent versus collaborative ownership and exclusive/multihoming registration.

**Overlap:** competing matchmakers; collaboration among intermediary owners; network effects.

**Material distance:** private pricing/entry and participation rather than regional public provision; no productive capability change/downstream oligopoly; no bilateral-referral threshold theorem.

**Verdict:** component threat, not one-page absorption.

## 7. Banal-Estañol, Macho-Stadler & Pérez-Castrillo (2018), Management Science

**Object:** two-sided matching between heterogeneous academics and firms with complementarity/affinity and ability characteristics.

**Overlap:** productive collaboration; heterogeneous complementary agents; formal match value.

**Material distance:** no competing intermediaries, public hub provision, referral/overlap, or downstream firm competition.

**Verdict:** supports the productive-match microfoundation but does not absorb the public hub game.

DOI: `10.1287/mnsc.2016.2680`.

## Immediate Stage 4 conclusion

The matching core is heavily prior-art saturated. The potentially surviving object is narrower:

> decentralized public provision of overlapping productive-matching infrastructures, with bilateral referral expanding the partner set and matched regional firms subsequently competing downstream, creating a distinct referral threshold and decentralization wedge.

This surviving object must be proposition-level re-killed in Stage 6M. No novelty claim is made here.
