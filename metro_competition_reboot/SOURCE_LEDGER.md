# Source Ledger

Search cut-off: 2026-09-02. URLs/DOIs are recorded for reproducibility.

## Competing intermediation / platforms

1. Gehrig, T. (1993), “Intermediation in Search Markets,” *Journal of Economics & Management Strategy* 2(1), 97–120. DOI: `10.1111/j.1430-9134.1993.00097.x`. Verified publisher abstract: heterogeneous agents choose intermediation versus search.
2. Caillaud, B. & Jullien, B. (2003), “Chicken & Egg: Competition among Intermediation Service Providers,” *RAND Journal of Economics* 34(2), 309–328. DOI/JSTOR: `10.2307/1593720`.
3. Rochet, J.-C. & Tirole, J. (2003), “Platform Competition in Two-Sided Markets,” *JEEA* 1(4), 990–1029. DOI: `10.1162/154247603322493212`.
4. Damiano, E. & Li, H. (2008), “Competing Matchmaking,” *JEEA* 6(4), 789–818. DOI: `10.1162/JEEA.2008.6.4.789`. Verified publisher abstract: prices sort heterogeneous participants among competing matching markets.
5. Sülzle, K. (2009), “Duopolistic competition between independent and collaborative business-to-business marketplaces,” *IJIO* 27(5), 615–624. DOI: `10.1016/j.ijindorg.2009.02.004`.
6. Gal-Or, E. (2020), “Market segmentation on dating platforms,” *IJIO* 68, 102558. DOI: `10.1016/j.ijindorg.2019.102558`.
7. “Metasearch and market concentration” (2020), *IJIO* 70, 102614. DOI: `10.1016/j.ijindorg.2020.102614`. Verified abstract: competing intermediaries search on consumers' behalf and competition changes search effort.
8. “Whom to benefit? Competing platforms’ strategic investment in recommender systems” (2022), *Electronic Commerce Research and Applications* 56, 101210. DOI: `10.1016/j.elerap.2022.101210`. Verified abstract: platform matching-quality investment is endogenous.

## Search / referral

9. Weitzman, M. (1979), “Optimal Search for the Best Alternative,” *Econometrica* 47(3), 641–654.
10. Garicano, L. & Santos, T. (2004), “Referrals,” *AER* 94(3), 499–525. DOI: `10.1257/0002828041464506`. Verified AEA abstract/full-text availability: costly diagnosis and referral contracting.
11. Arbatskaya, M. & Konishi, H. (2012), “Referrals in search markets,” *IJIO* 30(1), 89–101. DOI: `10.1016/j.ijindorg.2011.06.002`.

## Public facility / regional policy competition

12. Takahashi, T. (2004), “Spatial competition of governments in the investment on public facilities,” *Regional Science and Urban Economics* 34(4), 455–488. DOI: `10.1016/S0166-0462(03)00070-X`. Verified publisher abstract: governments choose public-facility investment in a two-stage spatial game with welfare analysis.
13. Walz, U. (1996), “Transport costs, intermediate goods, and localized growth,” *Regional Science and Urban Economics* 26(6), 671–695. DOI: `10.1016/S0166-0462(96)02134-5`.
14. Agrawal, D., Hoyt, W. & Wilson, J. (2022), “Local Policy Choice: Theory and Empirics,” *Journal of Economic Literature* 60(4), 1378–1455. DOI: `10.1257/jel.20201490`.

## Innovation intermediation

15. Howells, J. (2006), “Intermediation and the role of intermediaries in innovation,” *Research Policy* 35(5), 715–728. DOI: `10.1016/j.respol.2006.03.005`.
16. Hoppe, H. & Özdenören, E. (2005), “Intermediation in innovation,” *IJIO* 23(5–6), 483–503. DOI: `10.1016/j.ijindorg.2005.03.003`. Verified publisher abstract: intermediary expertise sorts profitable from unprofitable inventions.
17. Banal-Estañol, A., Macho-Stadler, I. & Pérez-Castrillo, D. (2018), “Endogenous Matching in University-Industry Collaboration,” *Management Science* 64(4), 1591–1608. DOI: `10.1287/mnsc.2016.2680`.
18. Calcagnini, G., Giombini, G., Liberati, P. & Travaglini, G. (2019), “Technology transfer with search intensity and project advertising,” *Journal of Technology Transfer* 44(5), 1529–1546. DOI: `10.1007/s10961-018-9667-3`. Verified abstract: endogenous matching and KTO search intensity.

## Mixed public/private competition

19. Klumpp, T. & Su, X. (2019), “Price–quality competition in a mixed duopoly,” *Journal of Public Economic Theory* 21(3), 400–432. DOI: `10.1111/jpet.12357`.
20. Kim, J.-Y. (2024), “Mixed duopoly in two-sided markets,” *The Manchester School* 92(3), 281–295. DOI: `10.1111/manc.12467`. Verified abstract/full-text excerpt: public and private platforms compete; dynamic investment incentives differ and the private firm invests more.
21. Liu, Y., Reshidi, E. & Rivadeneyra, F. (2026), “Public vs. Private Payment Platforms: Market Impacts and Optimal Policy,” Bank of Canada Staff Working Paper 2026-10. DOI: `10.34989/swp-2026-10`. Official PDF checked. It models welfare-maximizing public versus profit-maximizing private platforms, endogenous private pricing, and explicitly distinguishes new outside-option participants from switchers.

## Public innovation intermediaries / empirical door

22. Fukugawa, N. & Goto, A. (2016), “Problem Solving and Intermediation by Local Public Technology Centers in Regional Innovation Systems,” RIETI DP 16-E-062. Official RIETI source: local-government technology centers solve problems, refer SMEs to external knowledge sources, and learn local technological needs.
23. Fukugawa, N. (2024), “Productivity effects of innovation intermediaries for SMEs: evidence from Japan’s Kohsetsushi,” *Asian Journal of Technology Innovation* 32(3), 632–651. DOI: `10.1080/19761597.2024.2305737`. Reports heterogeneous productivity effects by firm size, industry and spillover channel.
24. Fukugawa, N. (2024/2025), “Sources of heterogeneous treatment effects of incorporating public innovation intermediaries for SMEs,” *Asian Journal of Technology Innovation* 33(1), 76–99. DOI: `10.1080/19761597.2024.2341019`. Heterogeneity relates to agglomeration, knowledge bases and learning capacity.
25. Fukugawa, N. (2026), “From unawareness to awareness of local public technology support: evidence from Japan’s Kohsetsushi,” online 3 May 2026. DOI: `10.1080/19761597.2026.2666827`.
26. Carpentier, E., D’Adda, D. & Nepelski, D. (2026), “Which firms receive digital innovation support? Evidence from the European Digital Innovation Hubs network,” *International Small Business Journal*, online 29 Aug 2026. DOI: `10.1177/02662426261461491`. About 15,600 customer firms; access is selective toward larger/faster-growing/high-tech firms.
27. Duan, R. & Jin, L. (2022), “The role of public innovation intermediaries in regional innovation: a comparative study of two regions in Japan,” *Technology Analysis & Strategic Management* 34(5), 578–593. DOI: `10.1080/09537325.2021.1915473`.
28. Feser, D. (2022), “To Compete or Cooperate? A Case Study of Innovation and Creativity Labs in Berlin,” *Journal of the Knowledge Economy*. DOI: `10.1007/s13132-022-01039-1`. Qualitative evidence that regional innovation intermediaries face both competition and cooperation.
