# Metropolitan Hub Strategy Selection

## Selected: T2 — endogenous access price

The metropolitan intermediary has a fixed baseline matching technology `M_T(theta)` and chooses `p_T` to maximize profit.

`p_T^*(x_1,x_2)` is therefore a strategic response to public matching investment through demand substitution and participation.

### Candidate assessment

| Candidate | Verdict | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| T1 fixed outside option | benchmark only | too decorative for the metropolitan-competition requirement |
| T2 endogenous price | **SELECT** | minimal strategic private response and empirically meaningful access condition |
| T3 endogenous `x_T,p_T` | reject baseline | adds a second quality-investment game already threatened by mixed-platform theory |
| T4 free metro platform | counterfactual | removes the access threshold that helps identify uncovered projects |
| T5 multiple metro hubs | reject | unnecessary dimensionality |

## Prior-art discipline

Kim (2024) already studies public/private two-sided platform competition and dynamic investment incentives; Liu, Reshidi & Rivadeneyra (2026) study a welfare-maximizing public platform facing a profit-maximizing private platform and endogenous private price responses. Hence `public objective + private price response` is not a novelty claim.

The metro price remains because it changes the set of projects for which local investment creates a match rather than merely reallocating a match.
