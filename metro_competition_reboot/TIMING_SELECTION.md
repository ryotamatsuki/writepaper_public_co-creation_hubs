# Timing Selection

## Selected Timing A

1. `G_1,G_2` choose long-run matching-evaluation investments `(x_1,x_2)` simultaneously.
2. The metropolitan private intermediary observes them and chooses access price `p_T`.
3. Projects observe relevant hub values/prices and choose `h(theta) in {1,2,T,0}`.
4. Matching/evaluation takes place and productive surplus is realized.
5. Regional and total welfare are evaluated.

## Rationale

Public staffing, network-building and program capacity are plausibly slower-moving than private fees or membership terms. The sequence also produces an endogenous private competitive response without adding private matching investment.

## Rejected alternatives

- Timing B simultaneous `(x_1,x_2,p_T)`: useful robustness check, but less institutionally natural when public budgets are precommitted.
- Timing C private price first: makes a short-run fee commitment discipline long-run public budgets; weaker baseline plausibility.
- Timing D public provision before private entry: transforms the question toward entry deterrence.

Timing A is not chosen to manufacture a sign. Stage 1 must report whether the core overlap result survives simultaneous pricing as a robustness check if the algebra is tractable.
