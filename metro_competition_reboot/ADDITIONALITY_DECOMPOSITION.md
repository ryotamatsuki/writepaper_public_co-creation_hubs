# Local Public Hub Additionality Decomposition

Additionality is defined relative to the counterfactual with `H_i` absent or with a lower `x_i`, holding the equilibrium responses of rival hubs and `p_T` consistent with the chosen counterfactual.

## ADD-1 — Extensive-margin productive creation

A project is a creation case when, absent the local hub/increment, its optimal counterfactual is `0` and no productive collaboration is formed, while with the local hub/increment it forms a positive-surplus match.

`0 -> H_i`.

## ADD-2 — Intensive-margin match-quality gain

A project would already use `H_j` or `H_T`, but local investment raises realized/expected total productive match surplus by a strictly positive amount net of real search resources.

`H_j/H_T -> H_i` with `Delta productive surplus > 0`.

## ADD-3 — Pure displacement/duplication

The project switches to `H_i`, but total productive outcome is unchanged or changes only negligibly. Market share, price transfers, or venue changes are not productive additionality.

## ADD-4 — Regional incidence reallocation

The collaboration's total productive value is unchanged, but the identity/location of the matched partner changes the regional distribution of surplus. This can matter to `G_i` without being productive additionality.

## Marginal decomposition

For public investment, the object to derive is conceptually

`dW_i/dx_i = Creation_i + QualityGain_i - Duplication_i + Incidence_i + PriceResponse_i - C_i'(x_i)`.

This is not yet a theorem. Stage 1 must derive the terms from primitive partner opportunity sets and endogenous choices.

## Prior-art warning

Liu, Reshidi & Rivadeneyra (2026) already distinguish new users from an outside option and users switching from a private platform. Therefore the extensive/intensive decomposition itself is **absorbed**. The prospective contribution is only the way **partner-pool overlap changes the composition and therefore the public investment incentive**.
