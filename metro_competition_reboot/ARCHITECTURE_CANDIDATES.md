# Architecture Candidates

## MC-A — Public matching investment × private metro price — SELECTED CONDITIONALLY

`G_i: x_i`, `H_T: p_T`, projects choose `{1,2,T,0}`. `x_i` is screening/evaluation capacity over `S_i`, not generic quality.

Why it survives: minimal architecture that simultaneously preserves local strategic investment, endogenous private response, no-match creation, and ecosystem-overlap competition.

## MC-B — Binary public provision × private metro price — REJECT

Good entry benchmark, but too coarse for the central question of how marginal matching capability changes additionality and for nontrivial local conduct competition.

## MC-C — Public partner-pool specialization × private metro price — REJECT BASELINE

Makes composition strategic but is high dimensional and dangerously close to generic differentiated-facility design. Could be a later extension only if MC-A survives.

## MC-D — Public matching investment × fixed metro outside option — REJECT AS MAIN

Useful benchmark, but violates the requirement that metropolitan competition be strategic.

## MC-E — Public matching investment × private matching investment — REJECT BASELINE

Conceptually viable but loses the access-price margin and moves directly into heavily occupied platform investment territory.

## MC-F — Public matching investment × private price and quality — REJECT

Nonminimal. It adds two private controls before the core overlap-additionality lever is verified.

## Fatal-weakness rule

The numerical score is secondary. MC-A survives only because it can be stated using common/unique partner opportunity sets. If `x_i` reduces to a scalar facility-quality shifter, MC-A is killed regardless of score.
