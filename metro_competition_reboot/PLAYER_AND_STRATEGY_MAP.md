# Player and Strategy Map

| Player | Objective | Baseline strategy | State/information |
|---|---|---|---|
| `G_i` | resident/regional welfare | `x_i >= 0` | local project distribution and partner ecosystem |
| `H_i` | integrated with `G_i` in baseline | no separate strategy | implements matching/evaluation technology funded by `x_i` |
| `H_T` | profit | `p_T >= 0` | observes public investments before pricing under Timing A |
| project `theta` | own expected net productive surplus | `h(theta) in {1,2,T,0}` | knows type and hub technologies/pools ex ante |
| social planner | total real surplus over two regions + metro sector | counterfactual `x_1,x_2` | transfers/prices cancel at social boundary |

## Baseline objectives

For local government, the precise accounting to be formalized in Stage 1 is resident firm match surplus plus resident-side partner incidence, less real public investment cost `C_i(x_i)`. The government does not maximize hub market share.

The metropolitan intermediary earns `Pi_T = (p_T-c_T) D_T`; `c_T=0` may be normalized if it has no mechanism role.

The social planner internalizes all real productive match surplus and real resource costs across the two regions and metropolitan intermediary. Access fees are transfers at the social boundary.

## Strategic feedbacks required

- `x_j` must change `G_i`'s marginal return to `x_i` through uncovered-project mass, overlap/duplication, or regional incidence—not merely market share.
- `(x_1,x_2)` must change private demand and therefore `p_T^*`.
- `p_T` must change the participation/choice thresholds and the creation-versus-displacement composition of public investment.
