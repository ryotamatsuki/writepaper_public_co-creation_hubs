# Search Log

Search date: 2026-09-02.

## Family A — competing matchmakers/intermediaries

Queries covered Gehrig (1993), Caillaud–Jullien (2003), Rochet–Tirole (2003), Damiano–Li (2008), Sülzle (2009), Gal-Or (2020), competing search intermediaries/metasearch, matching-quality/recommender investment, recent matching-market competition and compatibility platforms.

Key finding: endogenous choice, price competition, heterogeneous compatibility, search effort, and matching-quality investment all have substantial prior art.

## Family B — search/referral

Queries covered Weitzman search, Garicano–Santos diagnosis/referral, Arbatskaya–Konishi referrals, competitor/platform referrals, search intermediation and steering.

Key finding: referral/diagnosis should not be used as a novelty rescue; diagnosis may only microfound `x_i`.

## Family C — public facility/interjurisdictional competition

Queries covered Takahashi (2004), Walz (1996), local public input/facility investment, fiscal/public expenditure spillovers, and Agrawal–Hoyt–Wilson (2022).

Key finding: scalar public quality/investment competition is already occupied; immobility helps distinguish the application but does not by itself create a new mechanism.

## Family D — innovation intermediaries

Queries covered Howells, Hoppe–Özdenören, Banal-Estañol–Macho-Stadler–Pérez-Castrillo, Calcagnini et al., TTO/KTO theory, innovation search, Kohsetsushi, public innovation intermediaries and EDIH evidence.

Key finding: innovation matching, diagnosis expertise and endogenous search are known; empirical literature strongly supports treatment heterogeneity and the measurability of local capability/ecosystem moderators.

## Family E — public/private mixed markets

Queries covered mixed oligopoly, public/private platforms, public/private price-quality competition and 2026 public payment-platform theory.

Key finding: Kim (2024) includes public/private platform investment incentives; Liu et al. (2026) is especially close and explicitly separates new outside-option participation from switching.

## Family F — place-based business support

Queries covered Kohsetsushi, regional public innovation intermediaries, EDIH, local business support and regional intermediary competition/cooperation.

Key finding: strong empirical and institutional support exists for regional heterogeneity and alternative knowledge channels, but no exact formal model of two local public partner ecosystems competing with a priced metropolitan private matcher was located.

## Access quality

Primary publisher/official abstracts were used whenever available. Full-text/PDF inspection was feasible for selected open sources, including the 2026 Bank of Canada working paper; paywalled papers were not treated as if their full model had been independently reconstructed. Claims in the hard kill are therefore calibrated to what was directly verified.
