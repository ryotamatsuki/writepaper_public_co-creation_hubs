# Prior-Art Hard Kill

Search date: 2026-09-02. Seminal papers, recent published work, working papers, and policy/empirical innovation-intermediary evidence were checked.

## Exact prior-art questions

| Question | Answer | Hard-kill interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| PA1: exact `2 local public + 1 private` intermediary model? | **NO located** | absence of exact ingredient combination is not sufficient novelty |
| PA2: local government invests in matching capability? | **NEAR** | Takahashi has public-facility investment; Calcagnini et al. have endogenous innovation-search intensity; exact local-government matching investment not located |
| PA3: firms choose among matching intermediaries by productive fit? | **YES/NEAR** | Damiano–Li and Gal-Or absorb heterogeneous sorting/compatibility choice |
| PA4: public and private intermediaries with different objectives? | **YES** | Kim (2024), Liu et al. (2026), plus mixed-market theory |
| PA5: immobile firms under regional intermediary competition? | **NO exact located** | regional/public-facility models are close, but often spatial/service competition or mobility channels differ |
| PA6: local partner-ecosystem fit changes public investment incentives? | **NO exact formal result located** | strongest surviving candidate |
| PA7: investment effect split into market creation vs business stealing? | **NEAR/YES analog** | Liu et al. explicitly separate new outside-option participants from switchers; decomposition alone is killed |
| PA8: metro private outside option changes local public intermediary effectiveness? | **NO exact located** | must still create a new theorem, not an ingredient claim |
| PA9: relabel Takahashi facility as hub? | **YES if `x_i` is scalar quality; NEAR otherwise** | selected architecture survives only with set-based productive overlap |
| PA10: Damiano–Li plus public investment layer? | **NEAR-ABSORBED** | survives only if overlap changes creation/displacement through correlated opportunity sets, not generic quality |

## Strongest threats by family

### Competing matchmakers/platforms

- Gehrig (1993): heterogeneous agents choose intermediation versus search.
- Caillaud & Jullien (2003), Rochet & Tirole (2003): platform/intermediary price competition and governance.
- Damiano & Li (2008): prices sort heterogeneous participants among competing matchmakers; closest classic threat.
- Sülzle (2009): competing B2B matching intermediaries.
- Gal-Or (2020): compatibility-based segmentation under competing matchmaking platforms.
- Metasearch and related work: competing intermediaries can choose search effort; matching investment is not open territory.
- Recommender-system investment work: competing platforms can invest directly in match quality.

### Search/referral

Weitzman-style search, Garicano–Santos diagnosis/referral, and Arbatskaya–Konishi referrals absorb many search/diagnosis variants. Referral is therefore excluded from the baseline.

### Public facility/interjurisdictional competition

Takahashi (2004) is the direct threat: governments strategically invest in excludable public facilities in a two-stage spatial game and analyze welfare. Agrawal, Hoyt & Wilson (2022) survey multiple local strategic-interaction mechanisms. Walz (1996) is a regional-location benchmark but less directly isomorphic because the present beneficiaries are immobile.

### Innovation intermediaries

Howells (2006) establishes the broad functions of innovation intermediation; Hoppe & Özdenören (2005) formalize innovation intermediation via sorting expertise; Banal-Estañol et al. formalize heterogeneous university–industry matching; Calcagnini et al. (2019) endogenize search intensity in technology transfer. These kill any claim that `matching`, `diagnosis`, or `search intensity` alone is novel.

### Mixed public/private competition

Klumpp & Su (2019) absorb public/private price-quality competition in a mixed provider market. Kim (2024) directly studies a public and private two-sided platform and dynamic investment incentives. Liu, Reshidi & Rivadeneyra (2026) are the strongest current threat: a welfare-maximizing public platform competes with a profit-maximizing private one, private fees respond, and new participation is explicitly separated from switching.

## Surviving candidate contribution

Only one candidate remains: a theorem in which **overlap of regionally anchored partner opportunity sets changes the marginal social and regional value of public matching-evaluation investment by changing the probability/mass of creation versus duplication/displacement**, while a private metropolitan price responds endogenously.

This candidate is not declared novel until Stage 1 proves it from primitives.
