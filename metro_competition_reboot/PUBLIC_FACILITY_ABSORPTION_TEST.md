# Public-Facility Absorption Test

## Threat

Takahashi (2004) studies strategic government investment in public facilities producing excludable nonrival goods, with spatial relations, third-party regions, a two-stage game, equilibrium characterization and welfare. This kills any model whose content is simply `two governments choose facility quality and users choose the better facility`.

## Hard test

If local payoff can be written without loss as

`U_i(theta)=q_i(x_i,theta)`

and the only cross-effect is that higher `q_j` takes users from `i`, then the co-creation-hub interpretation is a relabeling and must be killed.

## Required surviving structure

`q_i` must instead be a derived order-statistic/screening object over a partner opportunity set. The same project can have correlated potential outcomes across local hubs because `S_1` and `S_2` overlap. Increasing overlap changes the counterfactual status of marginal projects: a candidate found by `H_i` is more likely to have been discoverable through `H_j` already.

Thus competition must operate through **duplicate productive opportunities**, not through taste or generic facility quality.

## Verdict

`NEAR-ABSORBED`. Takahashi remains fatal unless Stage 1 derives an overlap-additionality result that cannot be represented as a generic facility-quality cross-elasticity.
