# Matchmaker Absorption Test

## Threats

Damiano & Li (2008) already make heterogeneous participants sort among competing matchmakers. Gal-Or (2020) adds compatibility-driven segmentation. Competing search intermediaries can endogenize search effort, and recent platform work endogenizes investment in recommender/matching quality. Therefore neither heterogeneous fit nor matching investment is a free novelty margin.

Calcagnini et al. (2019) additionally show endogenous search intensity in technology-transfer matching, so calling `x_i` 'search intensity' does not solve the problem.

## Hard test

If the selected model can be restated as

> two matchmakers choose quality, a third sets price, heterogeneous users choose the best one,

it is **ABSORBED**.

## Candidate distinction

The surviving candidate is not ordinary platform quality. It is capacity applied to **regionally anchored, partially overlapping partner sets**. The cross effect of `x_j` on `x_i` should depend on the common measure/correlation of productive opportunities because overlap changes whether a marginal successful match is new to the system or duplicated by the rival ecosystem.

## Verdict

`NEAR-ABSORBED`. Stage 1 must show that the overlap term emerges from the matching technology rather than being inserted into utility or demand.
