# One-Page Absorption Test

## Label-stripped structure

> Two welfare-maximizing principals choose costly capacities that expand or improve evaluation of heterogeneous productive opportunities drawn from partially overlapping opportunity sets. A profit-maximizing third intermediary sets an access price. Heterogeneous immobile users choose among the three intermediaries or nonparticipation according to expected productive surplus. Investment changes both the mass of previously uncovered users who obtain a positive productive match and the mass diverted from other intermediaries. The overlap of the first two opportunity sets determines how much marginal investment is creation versus duplication.

## What is already absorbed

- competing intermediary choice and heterogeneous sorting: Damiano & Li and platform literature;
- public strategic facility investment: Takahashi;
- public/private objective asymmetry and investment/quality competition: mixed-market literature including Kim;
- extensive-margin participation versus switching: Liu et al. (2026);
- innovation matching/search effort: Hoppe–Özdenören and Calcagnini et al.

## What cannot yet be mapped in one page to a located single model

The joint object `partially overlapping productive opportunity sets × public matching-evaluation investment × creation/duplication composition × regional incidence`, with a priced private outside intermediary, was not located as an existing formal proposition.

## Verdict

\[
\boxed{\textbf{NEAR-ABSORBED — ONE STRUCTURAL LEVER SURVIVES}}
\]

This is deliberately weaker than `STRUCTURALLY DISTINCT`. Stage 1 must prove that overlap operates through the joint distribution/set intersection of productive opportunities and yields a nontrivial comparative-static or welfare wedge. If `M_i(theta,x_i)` can be replaced by an arbitrary scalar quality `q_i(theta,x_i)` without changing the proposition, the model is absorbed.
