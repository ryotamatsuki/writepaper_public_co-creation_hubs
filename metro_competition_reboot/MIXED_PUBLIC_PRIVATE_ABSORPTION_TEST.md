# Mixed Public-Private Absorption Test

## Strongest threats

### Klumpp & Su (2019)

A public provider and private provider compete in price/quality; the private best response to public quality is central. This kills generic `public quality disciplines private provider` claims.

### Kim (2024)

A public and private platform compete in a two-sided market, quality matters, and the paper compares dynamic incentives to invest, finding the private platform invests more. This kills `different objectives imply different platform investment` as a contribution.

### Liu, Reshidi & Rivadeneyra (2026)

A welfare-maximizing public payment platform competes with a profit-maximizing private platform. The private platform changes fees in response. Crucially, the analysis explicitly separates (i) new consumers from an outside option and (ii) consumers switching from the private platform. This directly absorbs the generic market-creation-versus-displacement narrative.

## Consequence

The present paper cannot claim novelty from:

- welfare-maximizer versus profit-maximizer;
- public free/low-price access versus private fees;
- private price response to public competition;
- extensive versus intensive participation margins.

## Surviving candidate

Only the **regional partner-ecosystem overlap effect on public matching-investment additionality** is not absorbed by these mixed-platform papers.

## Verdict

`NEAR-ABSORBED`. Public/private asymmetry is a necessary institutional feature, not the theoretical contribution.
